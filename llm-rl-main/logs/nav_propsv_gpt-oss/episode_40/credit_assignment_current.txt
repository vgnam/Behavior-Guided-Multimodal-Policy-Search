High‑reward rollouts consistently interleave a brief forward thrust (action 1) with immediate, precise corrective turns (actions 0 or 2) at short, regular intervals, and each corrective burst coincides with a +5.0 reward spike, keeping the agent’s state tightly bounded (≈ 1–3). In contrast, low‑reward rollouts either postpone the corrective turn, allowing the forward motion to drift into walls, or execute prolonged back‑and‑forth oscillations that never line up with a positive reward, leading to state excursions and collisions. The timing difference is critical: the successful runs apply a rapid turn within a few frames of the forward move, “locking‑in” the reward before the trajectory can overshoot, whereas the failures wait many frames or over‑rotate, causing over‑correction and loss of control. This disciplined, fast‑turn‑after‑move pattern yields steady progress and repeated goal contacts, while its absence or reversal produces unstable trajectories and large negative penalties.
