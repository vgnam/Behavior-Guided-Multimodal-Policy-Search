The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, decisive change in action – typically a switch from a neutral or forward command to a different discrete action within the first few steps – which quickly drives the episode toward termination and prevents the agent from remaining in the environment long enough to accrue the maximal penalty. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts stay on the same forward action for hundreds of steps, producing a persistent oscillatory pattern in the state that never resolves and therefore yields the worst possible reward. The brief, purposeful turn or stop seen in the better rollouts also tends to be timed just before the agent’s state begins to diverge sharply, acting as a forced “stop‑gap” that caps the damage. By avoiding prolonged, repetitive forward commands, the high‑reward trajectories limit the amount of overshoot and oscillation that dominate the low‑reward behavior, explaining why they achieve a comparatively higher (less negative) return.
