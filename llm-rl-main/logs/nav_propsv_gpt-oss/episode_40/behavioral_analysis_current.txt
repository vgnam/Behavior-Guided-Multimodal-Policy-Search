High‑reward rollouts all contain at least one deliberate change of direction – most often a sharp turn away from a wall or a switch from the default forward action (action 0) to a different steering command – that postpones a collision and yields a small positive interim reward, whereas the low‑reward episodes persist with the same forward action for the entire horizon, driving straight into a boundary almost immediately. In the better runs the agent briefly follows the inner wall of the L‑shaped corridor before turning right, showing a simple wall‑following heuristic that keeps it inside the maze longer; the worst runs simply drift along the outer wall or thrust forward without any corrective turn, resulting in rapid termination. The timing of these corrective actions also differs: high‑reward trajectories make the turn after a few steps (often after the second or third frame), while low‑reward trajectories never introduce a corrective step, so the episode ends after the first or second frame. This pattern of early, purposeful steering versus sustained straight‑ahead thrust explains why the former achieve higher (or less negative) rewards despite the stochastic environment.
