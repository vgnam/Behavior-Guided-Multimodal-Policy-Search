High‑reward rollouts consistently begin by **orienting the agent with a sharp turn before applying forward thrust**, so the first few actions are turns (actions 1 or 2) that line the vehicle up with the inner corridor, after which a sustained series of forward actions (action 0) carries it forward for several frames; low‑reward rollouts do the opposite, launching immediately with a strong forward thrust (action 2) while still mis‑aligned, which sends them into walls. The successful trajectories also show **regular, low‑amplitude oscillations** – brief corrective turns spaced every 3–5 steps – that keep the state value drifting only slightly, whereas the failures exhibit **large, abrupt state jumps** and long‑lasting direction changes that overshoot the safe region. Moreover, high‑reward episodes maintain **longer uninterrupted forward segments** (multiple consecutive action 0 steps) punctuated by brief adjustments, giving steady progress and accumulating positive reward, while low‑reward episodes interleave forward and turn actions almost every step, producing jittery motion and premature terminations. Finally, the best rollouts delay any aggressive corrective turn until after a clear forward gain, allowing momentum to be harnessed, whereas the poor rollouts react instantly with harsh corrections that cancel momentum and lead to instability, explaining the stark reward gap.
