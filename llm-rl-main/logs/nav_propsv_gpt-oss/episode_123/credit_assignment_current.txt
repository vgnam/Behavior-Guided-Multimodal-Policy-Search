High‑reward rollouts consistently keep a forward‑biased action sequence (mostly actions 0 or 2) punctuated by well‑timed right‑turn actions (action 1) at natural corridor junctions, producing smooth, uninterrupted progress; low‑reward rollouts either insert early backward actions (action 1 used to step back) or make sharp turns without a preceding forward run, causing sudden negative rewards and early terminations. The successful trajectories also show a disciplined timing pattern: a few forward steps are always followed by a single corrective turn before the next forward segment, whereas the poor trajectories exhibit rapid, repeated oscillations—alternating forward and backward or frequent over‑corrections within the same segment. This lack of oscillation in the high‑reward cases keeps the agent’s velocity and orientation stable, preventing collisions with walls or dead‑ends that dominate the low‑reward cases. Consequently, the consistent forward‑forward‑turn rhythm yields higher cumulative reward, while erratic, back‑and‑forth control drains reward and leads to failures.
