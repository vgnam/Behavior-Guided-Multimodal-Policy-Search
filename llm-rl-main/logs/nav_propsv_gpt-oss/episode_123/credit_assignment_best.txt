High‑reward rollouts repeatedly alternate moderate “turn‑left” (action 0) and “turn‑right” (action 2) commands, creating a smooth oscillatory pattern that continuously corrects the heading while keeping forward thrust modest; this balanced correction prevents the agent from drifting into walls and lets it gradually explore the corridor. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts often issue a single strong forward command (action 1) followed by an abrupt, large‑angle turn, producing a straight‑line thrust that quickly pushes the agent into obstacles and eliminates any chance for corrective adjustment. The successful trajectories also delay aggressive acceleration until after several short forward segments, using brief bursts of thrust only when the oscillation has already centered the vehicle, whereas the failures show early, sustained thrust that overshoots safe zones. Finally, the high‑reward episodes time their direction changes at regular intervals—roughly every few timesteps—so the agent never accumulates a large heading error, while the low‑reward episodes either turn too late or over‑turn, leading to excessive lateral deviation and collisions. This combination of balanced, periodic steering and restrained thrust explains the large reward gap.
