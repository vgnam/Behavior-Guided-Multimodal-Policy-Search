The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early **directional correction** – a turn or change of action (often from 2 to 0 or a sharp right‑hand turn) after a few forward steps – whereas the low‑reward rollouts stick with the same forward‑motion action 2 for the entire episode. This brief corrective maneuver prevents the agent from drifting into the wall or a dead‑end, keeping its position trajectory relatively smooth and bounded, while the always‑forward policy generates large, repeated swings in the state value. High‑reward trajectories also show **short bursts of accelerated forward motion** followed by a pause or slower action, giving the agent time to stabilize, while the low‑reward runs exhibit continuous acceleration without any deceleration phase, producing chaotic oscillations. By interleaving forward pushes with timely turns or slower actions, the better rollouts limit overshoot and collisions, which translates directly into a less negative cumulative reward.
