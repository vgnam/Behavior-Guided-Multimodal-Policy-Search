High‑reward rollouts consistently contain long stretches of action 0 (steady forward thrust) punctuated by brief, well‑timed corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) exactly when the agent reaches a corner, allowing it to stay on the optimal path; low‑reward rollouts either replace those forward stretches with immediate abrupt turns or omit the corrective turn altogether, sending the agent into walls or out of bounds. In the successful episodes the agent’s oscillatory adjustments are small and occur after a few forward steps, whereas the poor episodes show large, early oscillations that over‑rotate the vehicle before any forward progress is made. High‑reward trajectories also delay any sharp right‑hand swings until after the agent has accumulated positive reward, using them to negotiate tight passages, while the low‑reward runs unleash rapid right turns at the start, causing premature loss of control. Finally, the successful rollouts maintain a pattern of forward‑move → minor correction → forward‑move repeatedly, which keeps the state values within a moderate range, whereas the failing rollouts exhibit a forward‑move → big correction → forward‑move sequence that pushes the state to extreme values and triggers termination. This combination of sustained forward momentum, correctly timed modest turns, and restrained oscillations explains the large reward gap.
