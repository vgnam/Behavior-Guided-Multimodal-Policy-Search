[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently contain long stretches of action 0 (steady forward thrust) punctuated by brief, well‑timed corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) exactly when the agent reaches a corner, allowing it to stay on the optimal path; low‑reward rollouts either replace those forward stretches with immediate abrupt turns or omit the corrective turn altogether, sending the agent into walls or out of bounds. In the successful episodes the agent’s oscillatory adjustments are small and occur after a few forward steps, whereas the poor episodes show large, early oscillations that over‑rotate the vehicle before any forward progress is made. High‑reward trajectories also delay any sharp right‑hand swings until after the agent has accumulated positive reward, using them to negotiate tight passages, while the low‑reward runs unleash rapid right turns at the start, causing premature loss of control. Finally, the successful rollouts maintain a pattern of forward‑move → minor correction → forward‑move repeatedly, which keeps the state values within a moderate range, whereas the failing rollouts exhibit a forward‑move → big correction → forward‑move sequence that pushes the state to extreme values and triggers termination. This combination of sustained forward momentum, correctly timed modest turns, and restrained oscillations explains the large reward gap.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly begin with an immediate forward move (action 0) that rapidly reduces the state value, establishing early momentum, whereas low‑reward episodes often start with a right turn (action 2) or linger before committing forward, delaying progress. Throughout successful trajectories the agent interleaves brief corrective turns (mostly short bursts of action 2) with long stretches of uninterrupted forward actions, creating a smooth, predominantly forward‑driving pattern; in contrast, the failing runs exhibit prolonged oscillations—alternating forward and backward actions—or excessive turning that stalls advancement. The timing of corrections also differs: in high‑reward cases adjustments occur after just a few forward steps and are quickly resolved, while low‑reward cases show delayed or repeated over‑corrections that amplify deviation from the goal. This combination of early, sustained forward thrust and concise, well‑timed fixes keeps the agent on a productive path, whereas the opposite pattern leads to stagnation or collisions that incur large negative rewards.

[WORST POLICY]
The better rollouts all keep the forward‑thrust action (2) short and intersperse it with an early turn (action 0), whereas the worst rollouts persist with long stretches of pure thrust before a sudden turn appears only near the end. By switching direction sooner the high‑reward episodes avoid building up large positional errors and therefore terminate after fewer timesteps, limiting the cumulative penalty. In contrast, the low‑reward trajectories wander back and forth for hundreds of steps, producing large oscillations in state that accrue additional negative reward before the final abrupt stop. This pattern of premature, sustained thrust without corrective turning creates an unstable, over‑corrective motion that drives the reward further down. Consequently, the presence of early, alternating actions and short episode lengths consistently distinguishes the relatively higher‑reward rollouts from the much lower‑reward ones.
