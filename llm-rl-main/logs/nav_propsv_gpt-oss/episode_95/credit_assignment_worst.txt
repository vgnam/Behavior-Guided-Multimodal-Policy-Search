The better rollouts all keep the forward‑thrust action (2) short and intersperse it with an early turn (action 0), whereas the worst rollouts persist with long stretches of pure thrust before a sudden turn appears only near the end. By switching direction sooner the high‑reward episodes avoid building up large positional errors and therefore terminate after fewer timesteps, limiting the cumulative penalty. In contrast, the low‑reward trajectories wander back and forth for hundreds of steps, producing large oscillations in state that accrue additional negative reward before the final abrupt stop. This pattern of premature, sustained thrust without corrective turning creates an unstable, over‑corrective motion that drives the reward further down. Consequently, the presence of early, alternating actions and short episode lengths consistently distinguishes the relatively higher‑reward rollouts from the much lower‑reward ones.
