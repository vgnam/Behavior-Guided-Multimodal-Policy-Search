High‑reward rollouts all begin with an extended stretch of the forward action (action 0) that carries the agent deep into the maze before any turn is attempted, whereas the low‑reward episodes either turn immediately (action 1 or 2) or execute only a single forward step before changing direction. When turns do occur in the successful runs they are brief, low‑amplitude corrective actions placed after a long forward run and are timed near the natural corners of the layout; in the failing runs the same turns happen early or are followed by large, rapid back‑and‑forth adjustments that push the agent into walls. The successful trajectories also show smooth, monotonic state progressions with only minor oscillations, while the poor trajectories exhibit sudden jumps in state values, indicating abrupt loss of control. Finally, high‑reward behavior tends to postpone any negative‑reward actions until after the agent has already accrued a large positive reward buffer, whereas low‑reward rollouts incur negative rewards almost immediately, preventing recovery. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the former accumulate large positive returns and the latter collapse quickly.
