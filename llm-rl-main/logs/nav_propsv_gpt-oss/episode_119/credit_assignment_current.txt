High‑reward rollouts repeatedly begin with a strong forward thrust (action 0 or 2) and then keep a steady stream of forward actions, inserting only brief, small corrective actions (the occasional action 1 or 2) at regular short intervals, which keeps the state value within a modest band. In contrast, low‑reward episodes either accelerate in one direction for many steps before a sudden, large‑magnitude correction or make the first correction much later, producing extreme state excursions and abrupt direction flips. The successful rollouts also time their adjustments early—within the first few frames—so that any drift is damped before it can amplify, whereas the failing rollouts let errors accumulate and then try to recover with an oversized maneuver that leads to loss of control. This pattern of continuous forward drive combined with frequent, modest, early corrections yields smoother trajectories and avoids the catastrophic over‑corrections that dominate the low‑reward cases, directly explaining the large reward gap.
