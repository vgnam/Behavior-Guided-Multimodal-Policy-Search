In the high‑reward episodes the agent typically opens with several consecutive forward actions (action 2) that move it smoothly toward a wall, and it only executes a turn (action 0 or 1) when contact with the boundary is imminent, immediately following that turn with another forward action to stay aligned with the edge. This disciplined “forward‑then‑turn‑then‑forward” rhythm keeps the state trajectory confined to a narrow range and allows quick drift correction without large reversals. The low‑reward rollouts, however, often begin with an early turn or a backward‑like motion, generate sudden large jumps in the state value, and then neglect an immediate forward correction, resulting in overshoots, collisions, or erratic oscillations. The consistent presence of timely forward‑after‑turn corrections and the avoidance of abrupt, uncorrected turns therefore separates the successful trajectories from the failing ones and accounts for the observed reward disparity.
