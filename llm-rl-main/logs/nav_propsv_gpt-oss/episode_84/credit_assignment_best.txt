High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long stretch of pure forward actions (action 0) that steadily raises the state value before any turn is taken, whereas the low‑reward episodes either turn immediately (action 1/2) or make a single forward step and then terminate. In the successful runs the agent introduces turns only after it has accumulated momentum, using brief, alternating left/right adjustments (actions 1 and 2) that correct its heading without breaking forward progress; the failing runs either lack these corrective micro‑oscillations or apply a large turn too early, causing a rapid drift toward a wall. The timing of turns is also distinct: high‑reward trajectories space turns out over dozens or hundreds of steps, creating smooth arcs, while low‑reward trajectories cluster turns together or execute a single turn that ends the episode. Finally, the high‑reward behavior shows a gradual, monotonic increase in the state (or position) metric, indicating sustained navigation through the corridor, whereas low‑reward rollouts exhibit abrupt spikes or drops in the state followed by immediate negative rewards, reflecting collisions or loss of control. These differences plausibly explain the reward gap because sustained forward motion builds cumulative reward and delayed, small corrective turns keep the agent inside safe regions, while early or aggressive turning leads to boundary contact and large penalties.
