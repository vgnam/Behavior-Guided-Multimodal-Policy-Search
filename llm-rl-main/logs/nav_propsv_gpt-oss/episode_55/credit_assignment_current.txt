The high‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained forward thrust (action 0) and keep that thrust dominant, inserting only brief (1–2 step) corrective turns before resuming forward motion, whereas the low‑reward runs contain longer backward or reverse phases (action 2) or extended turn sequences that interrupt forward progress. In the successful trajectories the corrective bursts are tightly timed – they occur immediately after a forward segment and never linger – while in the poor runs the agent either delays its corrections or over‑corrects, producing prolonged oscillations that lead to large negative reward spikes. The high‑reward episodes also end with a modest negative reward that signals a controlled stop at the goal, contrasting with the low‑reward cases that exhibit abrupt, large‑magnitude negatives associated with crashes or loss of control. By preserving momentum with frequent forward actions and limiting the duration of any corrective maneuver, the agent avoids the costly reversals and late‑stage crashes that drive down reward in the low‑performing rollouts.
