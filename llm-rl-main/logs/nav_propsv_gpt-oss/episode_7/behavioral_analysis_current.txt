**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
- **Early purposeful turn into an open corridor** – in the best roll‑outs the agent makes a sharp, decisive heading change within the first 1–2 timesteps that lines it up with a clear passage. This prevents immediate contact with walls and positions the agent to travel straight toward the goal.  
- **Sustained straight‑line motion with a constant action** – after the initial alignment the agent keeps the same action (often the neutral “0”) for the remainder of the episode, producing a smooth, monotonic increase (or decrease) in the state variable and avoiding oscillations. The lack of unnecessary steering reduces the chance of accidental collisions.  
- **No corrective spikes after the alignment phase** – successful trajectories contain no sudden reversals or large magnitude actions later in the episode, indicating that the policy trusts the early orientation and lets the dynamics carry the agent forward. This stability translates directly into higher (less negative) rewards.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
- **Prolonged zero‑action drift that runs into walls** – low‑reward roll‑outs repeatedly show the agent issuing the neutral action for many steps, drifting unchecked until it hits a boundary, which yields the large negative terminal penalty.  
- **Over‑aggressive forward thrust without environmental checks** – several failures begin with a strong forward move that immediately pushes the agent into a wall, suggesting the policy is not modulating thrust based on proximity cues.  
- **Absence of an early orientation correction** – when the agent starts near a wall and never performs a corrective turn, its trajectory stays parallel to the wall and eventually collides, reflecting a missing early‑stage decision to re‑aim.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS (ranked)**  
1. **Early decisive turn into a corridor** – appears in 2/5 high‑reward roll‑outs and 0/5 low‑reward roll‑outs; its presence is a strong predictor of success and should be heavily reinforced.  
2. **Maintaining a constant action after alignment (smooth straight travel)** – observed in 4/5 high‑reward and 0/5 low‑reward episodes; the optimizer should bias the policy toward low‑variance action sequences once a safe heading is found.  
3. **Avoiding prolonged zero‑action drift** – absent in high‑reward roll‑outs and present in 4/5 low‑reward roll‑outs; the policy must be penalized for long stretches of inactivity that lead to wall collisions.

**4. TIMING**  
- The **early turn** occurs within the first 1–2 timesteps, making it a causal driver of success rather than a by‑product.  
- The **constant‑action straight travel** follows immediately after the turn and persists for the bulk of the episode, reinforcing the benefit of early alignment.  
- The **absence of zero‑action drift** is relevant from the start; the moment the agent defaults to “do nothing” it begins a trajectory that almost inevitably ends in failure.
