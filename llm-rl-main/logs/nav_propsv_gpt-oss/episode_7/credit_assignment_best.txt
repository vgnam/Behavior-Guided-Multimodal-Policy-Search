High‑reward rollouts consistently use a pattern of modest forward steps followed by a single right‑turn action, keeping the state changes smooth, whereas low‑reward episodes contain an abrupt turn or large displacement that immediately drives the agent into a crash zone. The successful runs insert tiny corrective adjustments every few frames, damping oscillations and preventing drift toward walls; the failures lack these micro‑corrections and let the trajectory deviate until it hits an obstacle. They also begin each episode with sustained forward motion that builds distance before any major turn, giving the policy time to align with corridor geometry, while poor rollouts often start with an immediate sharp turn that mis‑aligns the agent with the maze layout. Moreover, high‑reward trajectories keep state magnitudes within a moderate range, avoiding sudden spikes that signal over‑aggressive actions, whereas low‑reward cases exhibit rapid state jumps indicating reckless moves that cause termination. This rhythmic forward‑turn‑forward pattern matches the alternating straight and corner sections of the environment, and breaking this rhythm with erratic or missing forward steps leads to loss of control and lower reward.
