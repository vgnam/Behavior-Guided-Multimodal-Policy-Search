The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, decisive action (often a single forward thrust followed by a sharp turn or pause) that quickly redirects the agent away from the nearest wall, whereas the low‑reward rollouts persist with the same “action = 2” forward command for the entire episode, causing the state to swing back and forth and repeatedly hit boundaries. In the better rollouts the aggressive thrust is brief and is followed by a change of control input (e.g., a turn or stop) within the first few timesteps, while the poorer rollouts never alter the control signal, resulting in sustained oscillatory motion. This early corrective maneuver prevents the agent from building up large positional errors and avoids collisions, which yields a less negative cumulative reward. Conversely, the lack of any timely correction in the low‑reward cases lets errors accumulate, producing larger overshoots, repeated collisions and therefore much harsher penalties.
