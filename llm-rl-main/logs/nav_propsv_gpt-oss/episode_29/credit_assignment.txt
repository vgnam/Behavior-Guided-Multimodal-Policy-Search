[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep long, uninterrupted stretches of the forward‑thrust action (action 0), only inserting brief, modest speed‑adjustments (action 1) after several frames and rarely using the sharp‑turn action (action 2); this sustained forward drive yields steady positive rewards and prevents the agent from drifting into walls. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts switch to actions 1 or 2 almost immediately, producing rapid direction changes and frequent over‑corrections that break momentum and lead to collisions. The timing of corrections also differs: successful episodes delay any lateral or braking maneuver until the agent has already built up forward progress, whereas failing episodes intersperse corrective actions every few steps, creating a stop‑and‑go pattern that amplifies instability. By preserving a dominant forward thrust and limiting abrupt turns, the high‑reward behavior maintains a smoother trajectory and accumulates reward, while the erratic, early‑turning pattern of the low‑reward rollouts repeatedly sacrifices progress for instability.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave action 0 (forward thrust) with brief corrective actions 1 or 2, using the corrections only after a few consecutive forward steps and spacing them roughly every 8–12 frames; this keeps the agent near the corridor centre while still making progress. In contrast, low‑reward episodes either sustain action 0 for the entire horizon (Rollouts 1–2) or issue abrupt corrective actions 1/2 without a preceding forward run, causing sudden turns that drive the agent into walls. The successful rollouts also exhibit a smooth, monotonic increase in the state value after each corrective action, whereas the failures show large immediate drops or oscillations in state immediately following a turn. By limiting sharp, frequent direction changes and timing corrections to moments when the state begins to drift, the high‑reward policy maintains stable navigation and avoids collisions, which directly translates into the much larger accumulated reward.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain a short interval of smooth, directed motion—often a forward thrust (action 2) that keeps the agent close to a wall—followed by a deliberate, timed sharp turn (action 0) that briefly corrects the heading before the episode ends, whereas the low‑reward rollouts stay in a prolonged forward‑only mode with frequent, untimed oscillations and only a single late turn that precipitates an abrupt crash. In the better runs the agent postpones any erratic correction until after it has accumulated several seconds of stable progress, limiting the total time spent in high‑penalty states; the poorer runs begin to wobble and over‑correct almost immediately, so the negative reward accrues much faster. The higher‑reward trajectories also show occasional purposeful wall‑following, keeping the state values within a narrower band, while the low‑reward trajectories wander widely across the state range, indicating uncontrolled drift. Because cumulative reward is heavily penalized by time spent in unstable or colliding states, the presence of an early, controlled segment and a later, intentional turn in the high‑reward rollouts plausibly explains their comparatively less negative scores.
