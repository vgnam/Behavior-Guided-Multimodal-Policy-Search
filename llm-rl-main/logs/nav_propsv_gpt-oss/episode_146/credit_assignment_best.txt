High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave a short forward thrust (action 0) with a corrective turn (action 1 or 2) within two to three frames, keeping the agent’s state bounded and hugging the corridor walls; low‑reward rollouts either omit the turn entirely or delay it long enough that the forward drift grows unchecked, leading to large, abrupt state jumps and boundary collisions. The successful episodes also exhibit a rhythmic “push‑and‑adjust” cadence—each positive‑reward segment is quickly followed by a small negative‑reward correction that re‑centers the trajectory—whereas the poor episodes contain long stretches of uncorrected forward motion that accumulate error before a single, oversized correction that overshoots. Additionally, the best rollouts begin with a modest turn that steers the agent into the inner corridor, establishing a safe lane early, while the worst rollouts start with a straight thrust that pushes the agent toward the outer edge and later requires a drastic, destabilising maneuver. This disciplined timing of turns and modest adjustments prevents the agent from drifting out of bounds, directly translating into higher cumulative rewards.
