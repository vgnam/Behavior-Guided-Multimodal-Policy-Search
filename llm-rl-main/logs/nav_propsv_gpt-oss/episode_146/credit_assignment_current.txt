High‑reward rollouts consistently feature long stretches of the same forward‑movement action (either action 0 or 2) followed by extended, smooth turning blocks, whereas low‑reward episodes abruptly interleave opposite actions after only a few steps, producing sudden, large‑angle turns. The successful trajectories keep corrective actions brief and spaced out, allowing the agent to maintain a steady heading and avoid hitting walls; the failures show aggressive early thrusts that quickly over‑correct, leading to oscillations that drive the agent into obstacles. When a turn is required in the high‑reward cases, the agent executes it gradually and holds the turning action for many consecutive timesteps, creating a predictable spiral or curved path, while the low‑reward cases switch directions sharply and repeatedly, resulting in erratic, jittery motion. This disciplined timing of actions preserves stability and maximises forward progress, whereas the erratic timing in the failures generates collisions and large negative rewards.
