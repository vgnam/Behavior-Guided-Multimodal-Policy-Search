The high‑reward rollouts all terminate after only one or two decisive actions—a brief forward thrust (action 2) followed almost immediately by a change or stop—whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep applying the same forward action for many timesteps before a late “stop” (action 0) that finally ends the episode. This early termination limits the agent’s exposure to the maze’s hazardous regions, preventing the large cumulative penalties that accrue during the long, oscillatory trajectories seen in the low‑reward cases. In the better rollouts the agent also makes a rapid directional change (a sharp right turn) within the first few frames, while the poorer rollouts remain on a straight path and only wobble because of state fluctuations. The brief, purposeful motion in the high‑reward episodes produces only modest state changes, avoiding the large spikes and over‑corrections that dominate the low‑reward trajectories and that drive the harsher negative rewards. Consequently, the combination of an early action switch and a quick turn keeps the agent out of dangerous states, yielding comparatively higher (less negative) returns.
