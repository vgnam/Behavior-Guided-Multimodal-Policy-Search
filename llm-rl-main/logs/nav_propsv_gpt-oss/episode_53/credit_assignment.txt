[CURRENT POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained forward action (action 0) and introduce corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) almost immediately after any deviation, keeping the trajectory tightly bounded; the low‑reward runs either delay those turns or replace them with backward steps, allowing the agent to drift before it reacts. In the successful episodes the corrective actions occur at short, regular intervals (every 1–3 steps), producing a fine‑grained oscillatory but stable path, whereas the poorer episodes show long gaps between corrections, resulting in large excursions and occasional loss of control. Positive “+5” reward spikes in the high‑reward cases line up with these timely corrections, while the low‑reward trajectories have far fewer such spikes and more frequent “‑0.5” penalties when the delayed turns overshoot. Consequently, the prompt, frequent turning after forward thrust stabilizes motion and yields higher cumulative reward, whereas delayed or absent corrections generate chaotic swings that depress the return.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently interleave forward thrusts (action 1) with timely corrective turns (action 0), creating short bursts of movement followed by brief pauses that let the agent re‑orient before proceeding, whereas the low‑reward episodes often sustain a single action (e.g., continuous forward action 2) with little or no turning and thus drift into obstacles or dead‑ends. The successful rollouts also exhibit sharp, intentional turns early in the episode that redirect the trajectory toward the corridor’s curvature, while the failing runs either omit these early turns or over‑rotate later, causing the agent to miss the navigable passage. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories display periodic stabilization periods—moments of no‑action or minimal thrust—allowing the state to settle before the next maneuver, a pattern absent in the low‑reward cases where motion remains aggressive and uncontrolled. These corrective, paced actions prevent collision and keep the agent on a purposeful path, directly translating into the large positive cumulative rewards seen in the best policy.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, decisive change in heading – the agent turns sharply or reverses direction within the first two frames, whereas the low‑reward episodes keep applying the same action (action 2) for the entire horizon. This early maneuver shortens the episode, so the agent accrues far fewer per‑step penalties (‑0.5 each step) than the low‑reward runs that linger for many timesteps. In the high‑reward cases the action sequence is heterogeneous (forward, then right‑turn, then abrupt left shift), while the low‑reward cases are homogeneous, producing repeated oscillations around an unstable equilibrium. The heterogeneous, early‑turn behavior therefore prevents the agent from staying in the drifting, low‑speed regime that continuously adds negative reward, explaining why those rollouts end with a less negative total.
