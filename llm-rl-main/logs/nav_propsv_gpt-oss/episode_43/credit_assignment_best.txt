High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long stretch of the forward‑movement action (action 0) that propels the agent deep into the maze before any turning action is taken, whereas low‑reward episodes either start with a backward jump or introduce a turn almost immediately, causing the agent to leave the reward corridor. When a turn is needed, the successful rollouts delay it until the forward streak is complete and then execute a single, sharp action 2 followed by a brief corrective action 1, while the poor rollouts repeatedly alternate between actions 1 and 2, creating rapid, uncontrolled oscillations. The high‑reward trajectories also keep backward motions (action 1 that reduces the state) to a minimum and only use them as short, purposeful nudges to re‑align, whereas the low‑reward episodes contain extended periods of backward or lateral movement that push the state away from the goal region. By maintaining sustained forward progress and limiting noisy direction changes, the high‑reward rollouts stay within high‑reward zones longer and avoid collisions, directly accounting for their much larger cumulative rewards.
