High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a sustained forward action (action 0) that lasts for many timesteps before any turn is taken, whereas the low‑reward runs intersperse turns almost immediately or execute long strings of turning actions (actions 1 or 2) right from the start. When a turn does occur in the successful episodes, it is brief—typically a single timestep—followed quickly by a return to forward motion, while the poorer episodes show clusters of consecutive turns or rapid back‑and‑forth oscillations that destabilize the trajectory. The good rollouts also exhibit a “recover‑fast” pattern: after an abrupt change in state value they promptly apply a corrective forward step, preventing the state from drifting far from the desired lane; the bad rollouts either fail to correct or over‑correct, producing large state spikes that precede crashes. Additionally, the high‑reward trajectories keep the magnitude of state changes modest throughout the episode, avoiding the sudden large jumps seen near the end of the low‑reward runs, which are typically followed by large negative rewards. These timing and action‑selection differences plausibly explain why the former maintain stable progress and accumulate reward, while the latter lose control and incur penalties.
