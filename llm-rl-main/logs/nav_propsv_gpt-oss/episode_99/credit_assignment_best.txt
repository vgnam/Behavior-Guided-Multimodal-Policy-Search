High‑reward rollouts consistently feature long uninterrupted stretches of action 0 (forward) followed by only occasional, gentle turns, allowing the agent to make steady progress before any correction is needed; low‑reward rollouts break forward motion with rapid alternations of actions 1 and 2, producing jittery, back‑and‑forth oscillations that waste time and steer the agent off‑course. In the successful episodes the first major turn occurs after the agent has already built up momentum, whereas in the failing episodes a sharp turn is made almost immediately, often followed by immediate over‑corrections that undo the previous step. High‑reward trajectories also show a clear “settling” phase where the agent stabilizes its orientation and avoids late‑stage abrupt direction changes, while low‑reward runs contain late‑stage spikes of action 1 or 2 that precipitate collisions or boundary exits. This combination of sustained forward thrust, delayed and smooth turning, and the absence of frantic oscillations plausibly yields higher cumulative rewards by minimizing wasted movements and preventing catastrophic failures.
