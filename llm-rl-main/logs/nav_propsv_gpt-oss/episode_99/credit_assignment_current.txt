High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a sustained forward action (action 0) that builds momentum, followed shortly by a single, well‑timed corrective turn (action 1 or 2) just before each corner, whereas low‑reward episodes either delay the initial forward push or replace it with an abrupt turn that sends the agent drifting away from the optimal path. In the successful runs the agent alternates quickly between forward moves and brief corrective turns, never lingering on a turn for more than one or two steps; the poorer runs contain extended sequences of the same turn action, producing over‑rotation and a loss of alignment with the corridor. The high‑reward trajectories also exhibit small, incremental adjustments—tiny “wiggles” that keep the agent centered—while the low‑reward ones show large, sudden direction changes that lead to collisions or the dramatic –200 penalty at the episode’s end. Finally, the effective policy makes its corrective actions exactly when a corner is approached, using the reward signal to anticipate the turn, whereas the low‑reward behavior either reacts too late or over‑reacts, causing the agent to overshoot and incur negative rewards. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the former consistently achieve twice the reward of the latter.
