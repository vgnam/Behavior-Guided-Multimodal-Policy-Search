The higher‑reward rollouts all contain a short phase of a controlled forward thrust that is quickly followed by either a modest slowdown or a corrective turn, allowing the agent to settle into a stable trajectory before the episode ends; in contrast, the −500 rollouts keep applying the same forward‑type action (action 2) for the whole episode, producing large overshoots and repeated oscillations around a central point. The successful runs also show at least one deliberate change of direction—usually a right‑hand turn that lines the agent up with a corridor—whereas the low‑reward runs never deviate from their initial heading, leaving them to bounce against obstacles. Moreover, the better rollouts exhibit a brief pause or reduction in acceleration after the first few steps, which damps the momentum that would otherwise drive the agent into the walls; the worst rollouts maintain high‑speed, unmodulated motion throughout, causing the continual “jumping” seen in the state values. These timing and action‑variation differences keep the agent inside the navigable region longer, preventing early termination and therefore yielding the relatively higher (less negative) rewards.
