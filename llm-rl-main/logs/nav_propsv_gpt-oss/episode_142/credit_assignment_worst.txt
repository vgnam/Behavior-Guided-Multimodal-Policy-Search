The high‑reward rollouts all feature a brief burst of a strong forward thrust followed almost immediately by a sudden change of action that ends the episode, so the agent spends only a few steps accumulating penalty before terminating. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts keep the same forward action for a long stretch, producing prolonged, large‑amplitude oscillations in the state value that keep the episode alive and accrue far more negative reward. The early “stop‑early” decision in the high‑reward cases limits exposure to the environment’s costly dynamics, whereas the sustained, repetitive forward command in the low‑reward cases forces the agent to repeatedly over‑correct and bounce between high and low states. Consequently, the brief, decisive termination in the better rollouts yields a substantially higher (less negative) average return.
