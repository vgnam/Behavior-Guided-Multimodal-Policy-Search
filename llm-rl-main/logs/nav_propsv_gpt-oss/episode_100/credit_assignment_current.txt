High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with an extended series of forward‑thrust actions (action 2) that carry the agent well away from the start wall before any turning is introduced, whereas low‑reward rollouts often turn immediately or oscillate between forward and reverse actions within the first few steps, leading to early collisions. When turns do occur in the successful trajectories, they are timed after a stable forward segment and are executed as single, decisive actions rather than rapid back‑to‑back changes, which keeps the agent on a coherent path; the poor rollouts exhibit clusters of consecutive turn actions (or alternating turn‑forward pairs) that produce jittery, over‑corrected motion. The high‑reward sequences also display longer uninterrupted forward runs later in the episode, allowing the agent to accumulate positive reward steadily, while the low‑reward episodes break forward motion with frequent, short thrusts interleaved with pauses or opposite‑direction moves, preventing sustained progress. Finally, successful rollouts avoid “sharp‑turn‑then‑immediate‑reverse” patterns that appear in the low‑reward cases, a behavior that likely triggers negative rewards by bumping into walls or exiting the navigable region. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the former accumulate large positive returns and the latter terminate with low or negative scores.
