High‑reward rollouts all begin with an extended stretch of the forward action (action 0) that lasts for several hundred timesteps, allowing the agent’s state value to rise steadily before any turn is attempted; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts switch to a turn or a different action within the first few steps, causing an immediate drop in state value and early termination. The successful runs also exhibit only occasional, well‑timed corrective actions (actions 1 or 2) that are spaced far enough apart to let the forward momentum continue, whereas the failing runs display rapid, back‑to‑back switches between actions, producing jittery, oscillatory motion that quickly drives the agent into walls. When turns do occur in high‑reward trajectories they happen near clear environmental landmarks (e.g., corners) and are followed by a brief re‑stabilisation period, while low‑reward trajectories make abrupt, mistimed turns that never allow the agent to recover a stable heading. This pattern of sustained forward progress punctuated by sparse, purposeful corrections preserves cumulative positive reward, whereas the erratic, premature turning and over‑correction in the low‑reward cases generate negative rewards and early episode collapse.
