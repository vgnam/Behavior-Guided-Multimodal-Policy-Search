The better rollouts all end with an early, decisive turn (action 0) after only one or two forward moves (action 2), causing the episode to terminate within a handful of steps, whereas the poor rollouts never introduce a corrective turn and keep applying action 2 for the entire horizon. This early turn creates a sudden state jump that stops further interaction with the environment, limiting the accumulation of negative step‑wise rewards; the long‑running, purely forward runs in the low‑reward cases produce many oscillations and large state excursions that continually add penalty. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories show a brief, focused burst of motion followed by immediate failure, while the low‑reward trajectories exhibit sustained, erratic oscillatory motion over many timesteps. The combination of a short episode length and an early corrective action therefore explains why the “high‑reward” rollouts receive far less negative total reward than the prolonged, turn‑free low‑reward rollouts.
