High‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained stretch of forward‑type actions (typically action 0 or 2) that keep the state in a moderate range for dozens of timesteps before any sharp turn is introduced, whereas the low‑reward episodes either start with an immediate large‑magnitude action or make a sudden turn within the first few steps, causing a rapid state drop or collision. The successful trajectories also delay corrective turns until after a clear forward momentum has been built up, timing the turn only after the agent has accumulated positive reward for a substantial interval; the failing runs show erratic, back‑and‑forth or over‑corrected turns almost at every timestep, never allowing a stable forward phase. Moreover, high‑reward rollouts avoid abrupt state jumps—state values fluctuate smoothly between roughly 0.4 and 2.5—while the low‑reward cases exhibit spikes that take the state to extremes (near 0 or > 8) and then reverse direction abruptly, indicating loss of control. These patterns suggest that maintaining prolonged, stable forward motion and postponing sharp corrective actions prevents collisions and keeps the agent in rewarding regions of the environment, whereas early or frequent aggressive maneuvers lead to instability and large negative rewards.
