High‑reward rollouts keep a steady stream of forward actions (action 0) for several consecutive steps before making a brief corrective turn, whereas low‑reward rollouts intersperse turns (actions 1 or 2) much more often and often replace forward motion with immediate direction changes. In the successful episodes the agent tends to receive a small positive reward and then reinforce it with another forward action, creating short “positive‑feedback loops” that sustain progress; the poorer episodes either lack this coupling or follow a positive reward with a turn, breaking the momentum. The best trajectories also show a delayed onset of large state‑value fluctuations, staying within a bounded region for most of the episode, while the bad trajectories exhibit early spikes in state value that culminate in a sudden, large negative reward. Finally, high‑reward runs display a pattern of brief, low‑amplitude corrections after each forward burst, whereas low‑reward runs over‑correct with aggressive, high‑amplitude turns that lead to collisions or out‑of‑bounds exits. These differences in the timing and sequencing of forward versus turning actions plausibly explain why the former accumulate much higher total reward.
