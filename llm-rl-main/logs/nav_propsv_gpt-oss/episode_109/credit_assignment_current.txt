High‑reward rollouts repeatedly alternate short forward thrusts (action 0) with brief, low‑amplitude corrective actions, keeping the state value inside a modest range and earning the recurring +5 reward, whereas low‑reward episodes either accelerate continuously or make a single large turn that pushes the state far outside that range and triggers a −0.5 penalty. In the successful runs the agent inserts a pause or a small turn after every few forward steps, preventing the momentum from building up; the failing runs omit these pauses and instead sustain long monotonic thrusts or abrupt backward moves (action 2) that quickly destabilize the trajectory. The high‑reward behavior also tends to initiate movement from a corner or near‑goal position with a gentle forward push, while the low‑reward cases start with a rapid increase in speed that soon leads to a collision or exit. By repeatedly “thrust‑then‑stabilize” the agent avoids the large oscillations and crashes that dominate the low‑reward rollouts, which explains the large gap in episodic reward.
