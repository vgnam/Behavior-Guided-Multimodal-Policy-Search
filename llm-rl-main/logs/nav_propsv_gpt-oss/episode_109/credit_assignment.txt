[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly alternate short forward thrusts (action 0) with brief, low‑amplitude corrective actions, keeping the state value inside a modest range and earning the recurring +5 reward, whereas low‑reward episodes either accelerate continuously or make a single large turn that pushes the state far outside that range and triggers a −0.5 penalty. In the successful runs the agent inserts a pause or a small turn after every few forward steps, preventing the momentum from building up; the failing runs omit these pauses and instead sustain long monotonic thrusts or abrupt backward moves (action 2) that quickly destabilize the trajectory. The high‑reward behavior also tends to initiate movement from a corner or near‑goal position with a gentle forward push, while the low‑reward cases start with a rapid increase in speed that soon leads to a collision or exit. By repeatedly “thrust‑then‑stabilize” the agent avoids the large oscillations and crashes that dominate the low‑reward rollouts, which explains the large gap in episodic reward.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep a steady stream of forward actions (action 0) for several consecutive steps before making a brief corrective turn, whereas low‑reward rollouts intersperse turns (actions 1 or 2) much more often and often replace forward motion with immediate direction changes. In the successful episodes the agent tends to receive a small positive reward and then reinforce it with another forward action, creating short “positive‑feedback loops” that sustain progress; the poorer episodes either lack this coupling or follow a positive reward with a turn, breaking the momentum. The best trajectories also show a delayed onset of large state‑value fluctuations, staying within a bounded region for most of the episode, while the bad trajectories exhibit early spikes in state value that culminate in a sudden, large negative reward. Finally, high‑reward runs display a pattern of brief, low‑amplitude corrections after each forward burst, whereas low‑reward runs over‑correct with aggressive, high‑amplitude turns that lead to collisions or out‑of‑bounds exits. These differences in the timing and sequencing of forward versus turning actions plausibly explain why the former accumulate much higher total reward.

[WORST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts all begin with an early, decisive action that moves the agent away from the start wall and yields a small positive reward, whereas the low‑reward episodes persist with the same action (typically “2”) from the outset and never receive an initial bonus. In the better runs the agent makes a quick, purposeful turn that aligns it with the narrow corridor or the outer wall, while the poor runs keep a straight‑line command that repeatedly drives it into the middle of the space, causing large corrective swings. The high‑reward trajectories also show a brief period of smooth, wall‑following motion before the episode ends, contrasting with the low‑reward traces that display continual oscillations and abrupt jumps in state without any stabilising segment. Because the large negative penalty is triggered by collisions or extreme over‑corrections, the early alignment and short, controlled wall‑follow in the good rollouts avoid those crashes, whereas the constant‑action, never‑adjusted behavior in the bad rollouts inevitably leads to the –500 penalty.
