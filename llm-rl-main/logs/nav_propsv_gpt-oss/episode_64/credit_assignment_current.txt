High‑reward rollouts keep a long uninterrupted stretch of action 0 (the forward thrust) right after the start, only inserting a single corrective action (1 or 2) when a wall is imminent, whereas low‑reward episodes intersperse actions 1 and 2 almost every frame, producing rapid back‑and‑forth thrusts. In the successful runs the agent delays sharp turns until the accumulated forward momentum has carried it close to the corner, then executes one decisive turn; the poor runs turn prematurely, often within the first few frames, which sends the ship into the corridor walls. The high‑reward trajectories show a smooth, monotonic increase in the state‑value signal during forward phases, while the low‑reward trajectories exhibit large, erratic jumps in state value whenever an over‑correction occurs. Finally, the successful rollouts reserve the occasional turn for a clear geometric cue (e.g., a right‑angle corner), whereas the failures turn at ambiguous points, causing the agent to lose alignment and collide, directly lowering the episodic reward.
