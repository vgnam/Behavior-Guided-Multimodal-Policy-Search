The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early intervention – either a swift change from the default forward action (2) to the stop action (0) or an abrupt right‑turn within the first few frames – that truncates the episode before the agent can accumulate a large forward‑thrust penalty, whereas the low‑reward rollouts persist with action 2 for the entire horizon, driving a prolonged, unstable forward motion. This early switch curtails the distance travelled and therefore limits the magnitude of the negative per‑step reward, while the continued “always‑forward” signal in the low‑reward cases produces many more steps of drifting and oscillation that pile up large penalties. In the high‑reward cases the abrupt change also prevents the agent from entering deeper parts of the maze where collisions are inevitable, while the low‑reward trajectories never perform such a pre‑emptive correction and eventually hit walls after many erratic hops. Consequently, the presence of a timely action 0 (or sharp turn) early in the episode is the key behavioral component that separates the relatively better outcomes from the disastrous, constantly‑thrusting runs.
