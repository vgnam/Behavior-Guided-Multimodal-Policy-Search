[CURRENT POLICY]
**SUCCESS PATTERNS** – The rollouts that earn the highest rewards all begin with a *moderate* thrust rather than a maximal push, then apply a sequence of *small, incremental actions* that keep the speed growing slowly. Within the first 2‑3 steps they execute a *corrective turn* that points the agent toward the interior of the L‑shaped track, after which they stay roughly centered and avoid the walls for several timesteps. This combination of gentle acceleration and early heading adjustment lets the agent travel farther before any crash, which directly translates into a larger (less negative) episodic reward.

**FAILURE PATTERNS** – The low‑reward rollouts share an *aggressive, single‑burst thrust* at the very start, followed by little or no subsequent steering correction. They either continue drifting straight into a wall or oscillate wildly while trying to correct, producing chaotic, over‑corrected motions that quickly terminate the episode. The absence of incremental speed control and the failure to align with the inner corridor cause immediate collisions and the large negative returns observed.

**DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** –  
1. **Gradual acceleration (multiple small actions)** – present in >80 % of high‑reward runs, absent in low‑reward runs.  
2. **Early corrective turn toward the inner corridor (within first 2‑3 steps)** – appears in the majority of successful rollouts, virtually never in failures.  
3. **Sustained centering (maintaining a mid‑track position)** – evident throughout most high‑reward episodes, but missing or highly erratic in low‑reward ones.  

The optimizer should strongly reinforce patterns 1 and 2, and suppress the opposite (large single thrusts and lack of early turn).

**TIMING** – Both the gradual acceleration and the early corrective turn occur *at the start* of the episode (first 1‑3 actions), making them likely causal drivers of success rather than downstream correlates. The centering behavior unfolds *throughout* the episode, reinforcing the early decisions but is less useful as a primary target for early‑stage policy adjustments.
