The high‑reward rollouts all contain a regular rhythm of a short forward thrust (action 0) followed quickly—usually within one or two steps—by a single corrective turn (action 1 or 2), producing a “burst‑and‑adjust” pattern that keeps the agent steadily advancing while only briefly tweaking its heading. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts spend longer stretches executing consecutive turns or linger in over‑correction loops before ever issuing a forward thrust, which creates erratic, jerky trajectories and wastes time away from the goal. The successful rollouts also tend to fire the forward thrust as soon as the state value rises above a modest threshold, immediately capitalising on positive momentum, whereas the poorer rollouts delay this thrust until after multiple oscillations, allowing the state to drift and reducing the chance of hitting reward‑granting waypoints. Finally, the high‑reward episodes avoid long periods of pure turning without interleaved forward moves, a behavior that the low‑reward episodes exhibit repeatedly, leading to slower progress and more negative reward spikes. This disciplined timing of forward actions and minimal, well‑placed corrections explains why the former achieve consistently higher episodic returns.
