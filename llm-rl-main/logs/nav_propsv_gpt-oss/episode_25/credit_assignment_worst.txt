The high‑reward rollouts all contain a **prompt, decisive change in action** – typically a sharp turn or an abrupt switch to action 0 – that forces the episode to end within only a few frames, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep applying the same forward action (action 2) for the entire horizon and therefore accumulate far more negative step‑penalties. In the better runs the agent’s early thrust is aggressive but quickly redirected toward a wall, producing an early collision and limiting the total cost; the poorer runs show a sustained, almost monotonic forward thrust that bounces back and forth, creating long‑lasting oscillations in the state value. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories include at least one moment of **action reversal or deceleration**, which is completely absent from the low‑reward examples that never deviate from the forward command. This early termination and occasional corrective action reduce the cumulative penalty and thus explain why those rollouts achieve markedly higher (less negative) rewards.
