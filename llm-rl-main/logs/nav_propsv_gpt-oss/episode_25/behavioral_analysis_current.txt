**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across most high‑reward rollouts the agent emits a *steady, moderate‑magnitude forward thrust* for the first ≈ 3–5 steps, keeping its heading roughly aligned with the corridor wall. This early, sustained thrust builds distance quickly without overshooting, allowing the cumulative reward to rise sharply before any termination event. After the initial burst the agent performs *small, smooth corrective turns* that keep it hugging the inner wall of the maze rather than cutting across open space. Those gentle adjustments preserve momentum and prevent sudden collisions, extending the high‑reward phase.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
Low‑reward rollouts share two detrimental habits. First, many start with *near‑zero or very low thrust* (action ≈ 0), producing only a minute positional change and leaving the agent stuck near the start; the episode then ends with a large negative penalty. Second, when a thrust does occur it is often *large and abrupt*, followed by an immediate, erratic change in direction (sharp turn or overshoot) that throws the agent into a wall. Both patterns truncate the episode early and yield little or negative accumulated reward.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS (ranked)**  
1. **Sustained moderate forward thrust in the first few steps** – present in 4/5 high‑reward rollouts, absent in all low‑reward ones.  
2. **Small, smooth wall‑following corrections after the initial thrust** – appears in 3/5 high‑reward rollouts, never in low‑reward rollouts.  
3. **Absence of large‑magnitude thrusts early in the episode** – high‑reward rollouts avoid > |action| ≈ 0.9 in the first 3 steps, whereas 3/5 low‑reward rollouts include such spikes.  
4. **Early near‑zero thrust** – observed in 2/5 low‑reward rollouts, not in any high‑reward rollout.

**4. TIMING**  
The top discriminative pattern (moderate forward thrust) occurs *very early* (steps 1‑3), making it a likely causal driver of success. The smooth corrective turns emerge shortly after (steps 4‑7), reinforcing the early momentum. Conversely, the harmful large‑magnitude thrusts and near‑zero thrusts also happen in those first few steps, indicating that early action magnitude is the critical lever for the optimizer to adjust.
