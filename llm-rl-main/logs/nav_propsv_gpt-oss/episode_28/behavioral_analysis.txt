**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across the majority of the top‑scoring rollouts the agent executes a **large‑magnitude forward thrust in the first 1–3 timesteps**, then **quickly follows it with a sharp corrective turn** (usually to the opposite side of the corridor) before the velocity peaks. This “burst‑then‑steer” combo lets the agent cover a long straight segment while still staying inside the U‑shaped lane, which yields the big positive step‑rewards observed in the 682‑ and 1141‑point runs. A secondary pattern is the **use of brief, high‑gain steering bursts (≈90° turns) exactly at corridor corners**, which re‑aligns the heading for the next leg and prevents drift. Both patterns appear early (within the first half of the episode) and consistently precede the longest uninterrupted forward‑motion phases, explaining their contribution to high cumulative reward.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
Most low‑reward rollouts share two detrimental behaviours. First, the agent **maintains a constant modest thrust without any steering correction**, causing it to drift into the outer wall when the corridor bends – the “steady‑forward, no‑turn” style seen in all five low‑reward examples. Second, when a turn is attempted the agent **applies an abrupt, uncontrolled acceleration without a subsequent dampening or corrective steering**, leading to overshoot and immediate collision (as in the extreme‑jump rollouts). Both patterns produce rapid termination and large negative rewards because they either fail to negotiate corners or blow past the safe corridor limits.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS (ranked)**  
1. **Burst‑then‑steer (early high thrust + prompt sharp turn)** – present in 4/5 high‑reward rollouts, absent in all low‑reward ones.  
2. **Corner‑specific high‑gain steering bursts** – appears in 3/5 high‑reward rollouts, never in low‑reward rollouts.  
3. **Absence of any steering (steady‑forward only)** – dominates low‑reward (5/5) and is virtually missing from high‑reward (0/5).  
4. **Uncontrolled post‑turn acceleration** – common in low‑reward (3/5) and rare in high‑reward (1/5).  

The optimizer should **strongly reinforce patterns 1 and 2** and **suppress patterns 3 and 4**.

**4. TIMING**  
The top discriminative pattern (burst‑then‑steer) occurs **very early**, typically within the first 1–3 decision steps, making it a likely causal driver of success. The corner‑specific steering bursts appear **mid‑episode**, right at the first or second bend of the U, and are also causal because they prevent drift before the episode ends. In contrast, the failure patterns manifest **later** (after the agent has already entered a bend) or persist throughout the episode, indicating they are the proximate cause of termination rather than mere correlates. Adjusting early‑phase thrust magnitude and the timing of the first corrective turn should therefore yield the biggest reward gains.
