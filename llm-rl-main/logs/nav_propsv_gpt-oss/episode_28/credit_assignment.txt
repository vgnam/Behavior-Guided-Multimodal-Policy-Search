[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward episodes all begin with a sustained forward thrust (action 0) that carries the agent a safe distance from the start before any turn is attempted, whereas the low‑reward runs either start with an immediate sharp turn or a backward step, sending the robot straight into a corner or wall. When a deviation is sensed, the successful rollouts make a single, decisive corrective turn (action 1 or 2) and then quickly resume forward motion, while the poor rollouts respond with rapid, repeated alternations between forward and turning actions, creating prolonged oscillations that amplify error. The high‑reward trajectories also show a gradual reduction in turn magnitude as the agent approaches the goal, allowing it to converge smoothly, whereas the low‑reward traces maintain large, abrupt direction changes until termination. Finally, the better runs keep the sensed distance to obstacles relatively stable, avoiding close contacts, while the bad runs repeatedly over‑correct toward the inner wall, leading to collisions and large negative rewards.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep the agent moving forward (action 0) for long stretches and delay any turning until a clear corner is reached, whereas low‑reward episodes intersperse turns (actions 1 or 2) almost every few steps, producing rapid oscillations. In the successful runs the occasional turn is brief and is followed immediately by a return to forward motion, creating smooth, monotonic state changes; the poor runs show clusters of consecutive turns or back‑and‑forth actions that generate abrupt state jumps and frequent over‑corrections. The timing of corrective actions also differs: high‑reward trajectories make a single, modest adjustment and then stabilize, while low‑reward trajectories repeatedly over‑adjust, swinging the agent away from the intended path. Because forward progress directly accumulates reward in this navigation task, the sustained forward phases and minimal, well‑timed turns in the high‑reward rollouts yield higher returns, whereas the erratic, excessive turning and late over‑corrections in the low‑reward rollouts waste steps and often lead to collisions or termination.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one deliberate action change – most often a switch from the default forward command (action 2) to a stop or turn (action 0) at a specific step – whereas the low‑reward episodes keep issuing action 2 for the entire horizon. This brief deviation lets the agent re‑orient or halt before colliding with a wall, so the episode ends with a smaller penalty; the constant‑forward strategy of the low‑reward runs drives the agent straight into obstacles and produces the maximal ‑500 score. In the higher‑reward traces the state (position) increments are modest and monotonic, reflecting a smooth, cautious advance, while the worst rollouts show large, erratic jumps (e.g., from 1.6 → 10.0) that reveal over‑acceleration and loss of control. Finally, the better rollouts display brief periods of stability followed by a controlled adjustment, whereas the low‑reward ones are dominated by persistent oscillations and no corrective maneuvers, a pattern that directly explains the stark reward disparity.
