The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one brief interval of decisive forward thrust followed by a purposeful, often sharp, turn that quickly redirects the agent toward a new corridor, whereas the low‑reward episodes stick to a single repeated action (action 2) for the entire horizon and never introduce a deliberate reorientation. In the better rollouts the agent’s state trajectory shows short, stable segments of monotonic progress before an abrupt correction, while the poor rollouts exhibit continual up‑and‑down oscillations with large overshoots and no sustained forward movement. Timing also differs: the high‑reward trials make the corrective turn early (within the first few frames) and then terminate, preventing prolonged instability, whereas the low‑reward trials keep the same control signal for hundreds of steps, allowing the oscillations to compound. This contrast suggests that brief, intentional direction changes break the chaotic feedback loop that drives the oscillatory over‑correction seen in the worst rollouts, leading to less negative cumulative reward.
