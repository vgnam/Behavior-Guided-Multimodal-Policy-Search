High‑reward rollouts consistently alternate a forward action (0) with rapid corrective turns (1 or 2) every few timesteps, producing short forward bursts that are immediately followed by a sharp left‑right adjustment; low‑reward runs tend to string together longer sequences of forward actions before any turn, allowing the agent to drift into walls or lose control. In the high‑reward episodes the corrective turns appear almost symmetrically (left then right, or vice‑versa), keeping the state values oscillating around a safe corridor, whereas the low‑reward episodes show asymmetric or delayed turns that push the state toward extreme values and trigger large negative rewards. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories begin their corrective pattern almost from the start of the episode, while the low‑reward ones often delay correction until after a substantial forward thrust, at which point a collision occurs. This early, frequent “over‑correction” prevents boundary breaches and sustains positive incremental rewards, whereas the sparse or mistimed corrections in low‑reward rollouts lead to crashes or stagnation, accounting for the large reward gap.
