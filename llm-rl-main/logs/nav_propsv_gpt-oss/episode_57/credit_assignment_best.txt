High‑reward rollouts all begin with a brief “cautious” phase of short forward actions (action 0) followed quickly by a corrective turn (action 1 or 2) that keeps the agent hugging a wall or staying inside a narrow corridor; this oscillatory pattern repeats throughout the episode, allowing the agent to recover from overshoots and maintain a bounded state range. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts either launch straight with a prolonged thrust (continuous action 0 or 2) or fail to execute timely corrective turns, so the state drifts rapidly toward the arena’s edges and terminates early. The successful trajectories also show frequent, small‑amplitude adjustments—brief back‑and‑forth moves that reverse direction after each aggressive thrust—whereas the failures exhibit monotonic movement with few or no reversals. Because the high‑reward policy continuously re‑orients itself, it avoids collisions and stays in rewarding zones, while the low‑reward policy’s lack of timely turns and corrective oscillations leads to uncontrolled drift and large negative penalties.
