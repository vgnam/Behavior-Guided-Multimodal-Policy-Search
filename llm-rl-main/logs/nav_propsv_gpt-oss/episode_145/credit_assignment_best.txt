High‑reward rollouts repeatedly begin with a sustained forward action (action 0) that keeps the agent in a stable region for many steps before any turn, whereas low‑reward episodes either turn immediately or intersperse turns with forward moves from the start, causing early destabilisation. When a turn is finally executed in the successful runs, it is preceded by a clear state‑value drop and followed quickly by another block of forward actions, creating a rhythm of “forward → corrective turn → forward” that maintains momentum toward the goal; the failing rollouts show erratic turn timing, often executing sharp turns at high speed or after only a single forward step, which leads to collisions or overshoots. The good trajectories also exhibit occasional brief negative‑reward actions (typically action 1 or 2) that are isolated and quickly compensated by subsequent forward moves, indicating purposeful correction rather than persistent oscillation, while the poor runs contain clusters of negative‑reward turns that are not corrected, resulting in prolonged deviation. Finally, the high‑reward agents display smooth deceleration before sharp turns, allowing them to re‑orient safely, whereas the low‑reward agents accelerate into turns without slowing, producing aggressive thrusts that end in crashes; this disciplined timing preserves alignment with the maze’s corridors and yields substantially higher cumulative reward.
