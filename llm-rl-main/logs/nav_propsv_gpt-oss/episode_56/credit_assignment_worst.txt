The high‑reward rollouts all share a brief, decisive maneuver—typically an abrupt turn or a single forward step—followed almost immediately by episode termination, whereas the low‑reward rollouts consist of long sequences of the same forward action that keep the agent moving back‑and‑forth for many frames. This early, aggressive change in direction or rapid stop prevents the agent from lingering in the maze and thus limits the cumulative penalty it can accrue, while the prolonged forward thrust in the low‑reward cases produces sustained oscillations and repeated corrections that add up to a much larger negative return. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes exhibit a clear shift in orientation (e.g., from facing outward to turning inward) within the first couple of steps, a behavior that never appears in the low‑reward runs, which maintain a roughly constant heading throughout. Because penalties are incurred at each time step, the combination of fewer steps, an early decisive turn, and a rapid termination plausibly explains why those rollouts achieve relatively higher (less negative) rewards.
