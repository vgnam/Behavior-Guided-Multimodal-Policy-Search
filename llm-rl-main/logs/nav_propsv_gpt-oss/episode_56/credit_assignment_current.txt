High‑reward episodes all feature long stretches of uninterrupted forward thrust (action 0) that carry the agent deep into the corridor before any turn is attempted, with only occasional brief corrective actions (1 or 2) timed just after a clear corner is reached; this preserves momentum and keeps the agent on the intended path. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts show an early switch from forward to a turning or braking action, often within the first few steps, which produces sudden changes in heading, loss of speed and immediate negative rewards. The successful runs also limit oscillatory behavior, using corrective actions sparingly and only after several stable forward steps, whereas the failing runs continuously alternate between actions, resulting in jerky back‑and‑forth motions that cause the agent to drift off the track. By maintaining a steady forward drive and postponing corrections until they are geometrically necessary, the high‑reward rollouts avoid the destabilising over‑corrections that dominate the low‑reward trajectories and therefore accumulate far higher cumulative rewards.
