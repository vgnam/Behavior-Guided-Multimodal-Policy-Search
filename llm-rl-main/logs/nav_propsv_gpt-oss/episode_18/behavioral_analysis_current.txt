**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across the majority of the high‑reward rollouts the agent **initiates a modest forward thrust and then quickly aligns itself with the nearest wall, maintaining a “wall‑hugging” trajectory for several timesteps**. This alignment usually occurs within the first 2–3 decision points and is followed by small, smooth steering adjustments that keep the agent sliding along the wall rather than heading straight into open space. By staying close to the boundary the agent avoids early collisions, accrues many positive reward increments, and only later—when a sharp turn is required—does the episode terminate. The prolonged wall‑following phase is the primary driver of the unusually high cumulative scores.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
In the low‑reward rollouts the agent exhibits a **single, aggressive forward thrust with virtually no steering correction**, often from the initial pose. This pattern appears immediately (step 1) and leads to a direct impact with a boundary or obstacle, terminating the episode after one or two timesteps. The lack of any wall‑following, speed modulation, or corrective turn means the agent never gathers intermediate rewards and incurs a large negative penalty right away.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** (ranked by how uniquely they separate the two groups)  
1. **Early wall‑following (within the first 2–3 steps)** – present in > 80 % of high‑reward rollouts, absent in all low‑reward ones.  
2. **Gradual speed modulation (initial modest thrust followed by slower, smoother motion)** – evident in the majority of successful runs, never observed in the failed runs which use a maximal thrust throughout.  
3. **Small corrective steering adjustments (minor angle changes rather than large, abrupt turns)** – common in high‑reward episodes, rare in low‑reward episodes where the sole turn (if any) is a single large, fatal maneuver.

**4. TIMING**  
The top discriminative pattern—early wall‑following—occurs **right at the start of the episode (steps 1‑3)**, making it a likely causal factor for the higher returns rather than a downstream correlation. The accompanying speed‑modulation and fine‑grained steering adjustments also manifest early, within the first half of the trajectory, reinforcing that these behaviours are antecedent drivers of success rather than by‑products of a surviving episode.
