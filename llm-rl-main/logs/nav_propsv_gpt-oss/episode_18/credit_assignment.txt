[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep action 0 (forward) as the dominant choice for most of the episode and only introduce the turning actions 1 or 2 in short, isolated bursts after the agent has already progressed a safe distance (often after t≈200–400). In those bursts the turns are quickly followed by a return to forward motion, producing only brief negative‑reward spikes that are quickly recovered. By contrast, low‑reward rollouts either execute a turn almost immediately or sustain a turning action for several steps, causing the agent to drift toward walls and incur prolonged negative rewards. The successful rollouts also display a pattern of small, corrective oscillations that keep the distance to the goal within a narrow band, whereas the failing rollouts show large, unchecked overshoots that lead to collisions. This disciplined timing of turns and tight distance regulation prevents crashes and yields the higher cumulative rewards observed.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep a prolonged stretch of forward‑motion actions (the baseline “action 0”) before inserting only brief, single‑step corrective turns (actions 1 or 2), whereas low‑reward rollouts either replace the forward thrust with an early turn or follow a forward run with a sudden, multi‑step turn that drives the state into a wall. In the successful runs the occasional turn is timed just enough to realign with the corridor and then the agent immediately resumes forward motion, while the failing runs show either a missed turn (no correction before an obstacle) or an over‑correction that produces a large, abrupt change in state value. The high‑reward trajectories also display modest, damped oscillations around the desired path, keeping the state within a narrow band, whereas the low‑reward paths exhibit large swings and jerky accelerations that push the agent out of the safe region. These disciplined timing and magnitude differences let the high‑reward policy stay inside the maze longer, accumulating positive reward, while the erratic timing and excessive turns in the low‑reward rollouts cause collisions and premature termination.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early switch from the default forward thrust (action 2) to a stopping or corrective action (action 0) after only a few steps, truncating the episode before the agent can accumulate large penalties, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep action 2 on for the whole horizon, producing long runs of forward motion that overshoot and oscillate. In the better rollouts the forward thrust is brief and relatively smooth, followed by a rapid change in orientation or a sudden positional jump that appears to reset the agent’s state, while the worst rollouts show a monotonic, prolonged increase in the state value with no corrective action until the final frame. This early corrective action limits exposure to wall collisions and the growing negative reward that comes from sustained high‑velocity travel. Conversely, the persistent “always‑2” pattern in the low‑reward cases drives the agent into repeated over‑corrections and large state swings, which continuously add negative reward until a catastrophic termination occurs.
