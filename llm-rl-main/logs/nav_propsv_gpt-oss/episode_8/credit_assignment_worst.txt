The high‑reward rollouts all contain a brief, purposeful forward thrust followed quickly by a corrective maneuver—usually a sharp right turn or a sudden change in direction—that redirects the agent away from the wall, whereas the low‑reward rollouts persist with the same forward‑movement action for many steps and never execute such a correction. In the better runs the corrective action occurs early (within the first few frames), limiting the distance traveled before the episode ends, while the poorer runs keep applying the forward action far longer, allowing large, erratic state swings to accumulate. Moreover, the successful episodes show occasional pauses or reduced activity after the turn, giving the agent time to settle, whereas the worst rollouts display continuous, unchecked acceleration that produces oscillations and overshoot. These patterns plausibly improve reward because early redirection prevents collisions and excessive drift, and brief stabilization periods curb the buildup of unstable dynamics that otherwise drive the agent into highly negative outcomes.
