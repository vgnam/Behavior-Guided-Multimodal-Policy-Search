High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long stretch of pure forward thrust (action 0) that lasts dozens of timesteps, establishing steady progress before any turning action is taken, whereas the low‑reward episodes either start with a brief forward thrust followed immediately by a backward or turning command (actions 1/2) or never sustain forward thrust at all. When a correction is needed, the successful runs insert a single, modest turn (action 1 or 2) and then quickly return to forward thrust, spacing these adjustments several steps apart; the failing runs, by contrast, flip between opposite actions on consecutive frames, producing rapid back‑and‑forth oscillations. The high‑reward trajectories keep the state changes smooth and incremental, avoiding large jumps in the position/orientation variable, while the low‑reward trajectories contain abrupt spikes—often accompanied by a negative reward—indicating collisions or loss of control. Finally, the successful rollouts reserve backward actions for rare, last‑minute fine‑tuning, whereas the poor rollouts use backward thrust early and repeatedly, which quickly drives the agent out of the safe corridor and ends the episode with a penalty. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the former accumulate large positive rewards and the latter terminate with low or negative returns.
