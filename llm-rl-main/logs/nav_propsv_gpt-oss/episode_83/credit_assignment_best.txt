In contrast, low‑reward rollouts begin with or quickly resort to aggressive thrusts (action 2) or abrupt turns that produce large state jumps, leaving little time for corrective fine‑tuning and often driving the agent into walls. The successful trajectories also exhibit tightly timed, small‑amplitude corrections—turns are introduced just enough to negotiate corners and are followed by an instant return to action 0—whereas the poor trajectories show over‑corrections and erratic back‑and‑forth actions that amplify oscillations and destabilize the path. By maintaining continuous forward progress and limiting aggressive, poorly timed maneuvers, the high‑reward rollouts accumulate positive rewards, while the low‑reward rollouts lose reward through rapid, uncontrolled movements that trigger negative penalties or terminations.
