High‑reward rollouts consistently feature long stretches of the forward action followed by only a single‑step turn (right or left) and then another forward stretch, creating a rhythmic pattern of forward‑pause‑adjust‑forward that keeps the agent aligned with the corridor. They also make frequent tiny corrective actions—small oscillations that subtly re‑center the vehicle rather than executing large, abrupt direction changes. In contrast, low‑reward episodes contain very short forward segments, immediate multi‑step or aggressive turns that thrust the state far from the safe path, and they lack the steady micro‑adjustments that maintain alignment. This disciplined pacing lets the high‑reward agent stay inside the track and accrue positive step rewards, while the erratic, sudden motions of the failures lead to collisions and early negative termination. Moreover, the successful rollouts include brief pauses or zero‑action intervals that let the agent stabilize before the next maneuver, a stabilizing pause that is absent from the constantly shifting low‑reward runs.
