High‑reward rollouts all begin with a brief forward thrust followed quickly by a turn (often alternating left‑right) that steers the agent into the corridor rather than persisting on a long straight segment. After each turn they execute a short, stabilising forward stretch before making another corrective turn, keeping the state values within a modest band instead of allowing large spikes. This pattern of frequent, modest‑amplitude oscillations produces regular progress toward the goal while preventing the agent from running into walls. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts either continue straight for too long, make an abrupt single correction that overshoots, or exhibit chaotic, large‑amplitude swings that quickly drive the state out of the safe range. The disciplined timing of short forward moves followed by timely, balanced turns therefore explains why the high‑reward trajectories accumulate far more positive reward.
