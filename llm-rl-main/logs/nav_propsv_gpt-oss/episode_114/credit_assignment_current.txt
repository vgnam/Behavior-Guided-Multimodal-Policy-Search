High‑reward rollouts all begin with a rapid sequence of forward actions that generate an early stream of positive reward, and whenever a negative reward appears they immediately follow it with a brief, decisive corrective action that quickly restores forward momentum; low‑reward episodes either lack this initial forward burst or respond to negative signals with prolonged or absent corrections, letting the penalty accumulate. The successful rollouts also time sharp turns precisely at junctions, using a single decisive turn before resuming smooth forward motion, whereas the poorer rollouts either omit the turn, turn too late, or execute multiple jittery turns that keep the agent oscillating. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s oscillations are short‑lived—brief back‑and‑forth moves are followed by at least five consecutive forward steps—while the low‑reward examples sustain oscillatory cycles for many steps, causing drift away from the optimal path. Finally, the best rollouts never end with a terminal collision; they finish the episode after a stabilising forward run, whereas several low‑reward runs culminate in a sudden large negative reward caused by an ill‑timed forward action into an obstacle. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the former accumulate far higher total rewards.
