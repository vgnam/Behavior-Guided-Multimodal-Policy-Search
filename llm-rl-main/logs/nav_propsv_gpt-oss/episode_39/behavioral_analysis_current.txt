High‑reward rollouts consistently exhibit a slow, steady forward thrust that is maintained for many timesteps, allowing the agent to progress smoothly along the corridor without sudden jumps in position; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts begin with an abrupt, full‑power action that instantly drives the agent into a wall or out of bounds. The successful episodes also show early, modest steering adjustments (e.g., slight turns) that keep the agent centered in the passage, whereas the failures either make no turn at all or execute a large, sudden change that misaligns the agent with the safe path. Timing is another key difference: high‑reward trajectories delay any aggressive acceleration until after several stable steps, while the low‑reward runs accelerate immediately, causing early termination. Finally, the high‑reward behavior maintains a consistent orientation and avoids rapid oscillations, whereas the low‑reward behavior is marked by erratic, uncontrolled motion that rapidly reduces the state value and triggers a negative reward. These patterns suggest that gradual, well‑timed movement and careful alignment with the corridor are the primary drivers of the observed reward gap.
