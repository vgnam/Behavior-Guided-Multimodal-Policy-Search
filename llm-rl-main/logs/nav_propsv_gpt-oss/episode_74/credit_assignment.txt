[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts start with a sustained forward action that lasts several steps before a single, modest turn, while low‑reward episodes intersperse forward moves with immediate sharp or reverse turns. The timing of turns in the high‑reward cases follows a regular rhythm—each turn occurs only after a stable forward streak, producing smooth arcs—whereas the low‑reward trajectories feature back‑to‑back turns that create drift and overshoot. High‑reward behavior relies on small corrective adjustments (minor left/right actions) instead of large, abrupt direction changes, thereby avoiding the frequent –0.5 penalties seen in low‑reward rollouts. This disciplined forward bias and restrained turning keep the agent on the optimal corridor longer, allowing it to collect many +5 rewards, while the jittery over‑corrections in low‑reward rollouts waste steps and reduce total reward.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly string together several consecutive forward actions (action 0) before issuing a single, modest turn (action 1 or 2), allowing the state value to rise steadily and only make small corrective adjustments after a short “run” of forward thrusts; low‑reward rollouts instead fire an aggressive turn or a large‑magnitude thrust (often action 2) almost immediately, producing abrupt jumps in the state and terminating the episode. The timing of the corrective turns is also inverted: the successful trajectories delay turning until the agent has accumulated momentum, whereas the failures turn or brake early, interrupting forward progress. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes exhibit bounded oscillations—each turn is quickly followed by another forward step that re‑stabilizes the trajectory—while the low‑reward episodes contain uncontrolled spikes where a single action drives the state far from the goal and never recovers. These patterns plausibly explain the reward gap because sustained forward motion with only minor, timely corrections yields continuous positive reward, whereas early, large, or poorly timed deviations generate negative rewards and premature episode termination.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain a brief, purposeful deviation from the default forward action 2 – usually within the first few frames they either execute a sharp turn or a rapid descent (a large drop in the state value) and later switch to a different action (often 0) that terminates or corrects the trajectory, whereas the low‑reward rollouts stay on action 2 for the entire episode and never make such a switch. This early aggressive maneuver lets the agent leave hazardous regions quickly, while the late corrective action prevents prolonged exposure to the environment’s penalties; the constant‑action, oscillatory behaviour of the low‑reward rollouts keeps the agent bouncing around unstable states and accumulating large negative rewards. Because the high‑reward trajectories combine a quick, decisive change of direction with a timely termination, they spend less time in high‑penalty zones, leading to the comparatively better scores.
