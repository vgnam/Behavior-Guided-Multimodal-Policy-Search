High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a quick forward push (action 0) followed almost immediately by a sharp turn (action 1 or 2) within the first few steps, creating a rapid zig‑zag that pushes the agent into the inner L‑shaped corridor, whereas low‑reward rollouts tend to linger in long straight segments before turning. The successful rollouts also display a high‑frequency alternation between forward moves and brief corrective turns, producing short bursts of motion interleaved with back‑and‑forth adjustments that keep the state near the boundary and quickly correct overshoots; the lower‑reward rollouts show prolonged periods of the same action and only occasional, delayed corrections. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories contain early‑stage rapid accelerations—clusters of consecutive forward actions—followed by brief pauses or slower motions, which allow the agent to cover distance fast and then fine‑tune its path, while the low‑reward runs remain uniformly moderate in speed. These patterns give the high‑reward agent better positional progress and more effective corner navigation, directly translating into higher cumulative reward.
