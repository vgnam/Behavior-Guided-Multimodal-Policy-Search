The high‑reward episodes all feature a prolonged, steady use of the forward‑motion action (action 0) with only gentle, late‑stage adjustments, producing a smooth, monotonic trajectory that stays close to the corridor centre and avoids sudden turns; in contrast, the low‑reward rollouts repeatedly introduce abrupt direction changes or early, large‑magnitude turns that drive the agent into walls. When the agent does turn in the successful runs, it does so after a clear period of stable forward motion and with a small corrective angle, whereas the failures show over‑corrections, oscillatory swings, or instantaneous sharp pivots that destabilize the path. Timing-wise, the good rollouts allow several time‑steps of controlled thrust before any heading change, while the poor rollouts either thrust immediately into an obstacle or switch actions within one or two steps, leading to collisions. Thus, consistent forward thrust, delayed and modest steering, and smooth, low‑frequency trajectory updates appear to be the key behavioural components that separate the higher‑reward outcomes from the erratic, low‑reward ones.
