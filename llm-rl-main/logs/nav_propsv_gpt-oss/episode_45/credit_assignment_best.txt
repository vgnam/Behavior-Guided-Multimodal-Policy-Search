High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a sustained sequence of forward actions (action 0) that maintain steady progress, punctuated by deliberate, low‑amplitude turns (actions 1 or 2) timed to align with environmental corners; this keeps the agent on the track and yields frequent positive waypoint rewards. By contrast, low‑reward rollouts feature either a single, aggressive forward thrust followed by immediate failure or abrupt, large‑magnitude turns that occur without preceding forward momentum, causing the agent to leave the safe corridor or collide. The successful trajectories also exhibit a pattern of modest oscillations—small corrective adjustments that quickly return the agent to a forward heading—whereas the failures show uncontrolled oscillations or a sudden, unchecked change in state value. These timing and magnitude differences explain why the high‑reward runs accumulate reward continuously while the low‑reward runs incur early, large penalties.
