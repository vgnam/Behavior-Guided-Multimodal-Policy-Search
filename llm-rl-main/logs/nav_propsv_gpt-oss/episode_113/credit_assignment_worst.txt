The higher‑reward rollouts all contain at least one brief interval of purposeful, directed thrust—often a single forward or modest turning action—that produces a smooth, monotonic change in the state before any correction is needed, whereas the lowest‑reward episodes consist almost exclusively of the same action being repeated for the entire episode, creating continual, large‑amplitude oscillations in the state value. In the better rollouts the agent occasionally switches actions (e.g., from a forward thrust to a stabilising maneuver) at moments when the state curve begins to flatten or reverse, which damps overshoot and prevents the runaway swings that dominate the bad rollouts. The timing of these switches is early enough to keep the trajectory within the corridor of the maze, while the poor rollouts show no such early intervention and instead let the state drift far from the desired path before any correction is attempted. Because the occasional corrective action limits collisions and keeps the agent inside the navigable region, the episode accumulates a less severe penalty; the relentless, identical action in the low‑reward cases forces the agent to repeatedly bounce between extreme positions, incurring the maximal negative reward each time.
