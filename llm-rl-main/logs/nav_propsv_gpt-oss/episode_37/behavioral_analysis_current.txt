The high‑reward episodes all contain a brief, modest thrust (action 0 or 1) followed almost immediately by a pause or reversal, keeping the agent’s velocity low enough to stay inside the L‑shaped corridor and avoid wall contact, whereas the low‑reward episodes feature a single aggressive thrust that is sustained for many steps, causing rapid acceleration and overshoot into the outer walls. In the better rollouts the agent’s orientation changes only minimally before the episode ends, indicating that it either stops or turns gently after the initial move; the poorer rollouts show large, sudden jumps in orientation (e.g., from ≈1.5 rad to >4 rad) that betray a loss of directional control. Timing-wise, the successful trials terminate within a few timesteps after the first action, preventing the cumulative penalty of long‑duration motion, while the failures continue moving for dozens of steps, accumulating larger negative rewards before crashing. Thus, a pattern of short, controlled thrusts with early termination—and the avoidance of prolonged high‑speed drift—distinguishes the high‑reward behavior from the aggressive, uncorrected thrusts that dominate the low‑reward rollouts.
