High‑reward rollouts repeatedly introduce short, timely action switches—most often flipping between action 0 (forward) and action 2 (away) within a few frames after the first negative reward—so the agent can quickly correct a slip and stay near the goal, whereas low‑reward runs tend to persist with a single action for long stretches, allowing drift or overshoot to accumulate unchecked. In the successful episodes the agent makes an early, decisive turn when it reaches a junction (usually within the first 5–8 frames), then oscillates back and forth around the target corridor, which keeps its state values bounded; the poor rollouts either skip this early turn or continue straight until termination, producing a monotonic decline in state value. Whenever a negative reward appears, the high‑reward trajectories immediately respond with an opposite action, converting the penalty into a corrective maneuver, while the low‑reward trajectories ignore the signal and keep the same motion, letting the penalty compound. Finally, the high‑reward behavior deliberately “wiggles” around the goal—alternating actions to hover close to the optimal region—whereas the low‑reward behavior exhibits a single aggressive thrust that pushes the state far from the goal and results in divergence.
