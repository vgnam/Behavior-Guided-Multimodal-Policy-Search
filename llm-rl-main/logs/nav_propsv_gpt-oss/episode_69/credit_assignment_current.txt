High‑reward rollouts repeatedly pair a brief forward thrust (action 0) with an almost immediate corrective maneuver (often action 2 or 1) within the same or next timestep, keeping the agent’s state tightly bounded around the target corridor; low‑reward episodes either omit this quick correction or postpone it, allowing the state to drift far before a large, destabilising adjustment is attempted. The successful rollouts also insert short “pause” frames—moments of no thrust or minimal action—right before a direction change, which lets any overshoot settle before the next thrust, whereas the poorer rollouts show abrupt direction flips without an intervening pause, producing overshoot and loss of control. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories generate frequent small positive‑reward events (≈5 pts) at regular intervals, reflecting steady progress, while the low‑reward runs have long gaps between such spikes or experience a single large negative correction that terminates the episode. This pattern of rapid, localized correction combined with brief stabilization periods prevents cumulative error buildup, explaining why the same oscillatory style yields much higher total reward when those timing cues are present.
