In the high‑reward rollouts the agent interleaves forward thrusts (action 0) with short corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) within the first few dozen steps, keeping the state value inside a narrow band instead of letting it drift upward; the low‑reward runs either persist with long stretches of pure forward motion or delay the first turn until much later, which causes the state to climb to unsafe levels and results in collisions. Whenever the trajectory approaches a wall, the successful episodes insert a quick opposite‑turn or backward adjustment (a brief action 1 or 2) exactly before the wall is reached, whereas the poor episodes either miss this correction or apply it after impact. The best rollouts also exhibit brief bursts of lateral thrust (action 2) right after entering a corner, realigning the heading early, while the worst rollouts either skip the early lateral move or execute it too late, leading to overshoot. Finally, the high‑reward trajectories maintain a regular, low‑amplitude oscillation in the state variable throughout the episode, preventing the large swings that appear after a few hundred steps in the low‑reward trajectories and thereby preserving stability and accumulating reward.
