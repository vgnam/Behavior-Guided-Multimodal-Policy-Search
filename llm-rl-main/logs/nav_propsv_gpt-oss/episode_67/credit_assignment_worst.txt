In the better rollouts the agent abandons the default forward thrust (action 2) within the first two steps, either by issuing a stop (action 0) or a turn, which quickly caps the distance it travels toward a wall. The poorer rollouts never break the monotonic forward action; they keep applying action 2 for the entire episode, producing large back‑and‑forth swings in the state value and frequent over‑corrections. The high‑reward trajectories therefore consist of a single, purposeful translation followed by an immediate termination, while the low‑reward ones exhibit prolonged oscillatory motion that repeatedly drives the agent into obstacles. By correcting early, the high‑reward agent avoids the cumulative penalties of repeated collisions, whereas the low‑reward agent accrues larger negative rewards from sustained unstable movement. Consequently, early action diversification and timely stopping are the key behavioral differences that drive the reward gap.
