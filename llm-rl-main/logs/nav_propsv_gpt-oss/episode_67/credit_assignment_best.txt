High‑reward rollouts keep a steady stream of forward actions (action 0) punctuated by only brief, small‑angle corrective turns, so the agent moves smoothly along the corridor and rarely loses momentum; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts contain long bursts of aggressive thrust followed by sudden large turns or over‑corrections, which break continuity and create abrupt speed changes. The successful runs also align the agent’s heading early in the episode and maintain that orientation for many steps, allowing it to collect the bulk of the positive per‑step reward, whereas the poor runs drift toward walls or outer edges before any correction is attempted, leading to early collisions and negative rewards. When a turn is needed, high‑reward trajectories execute it gradually and immediately after detecting an upcoming wall, while low‑reward trajectories either delay the turn or swing wildly, causing the agent to hit obstacles. Moreover, the best rollouts exhibit consistent timing – a pattern of 2–3 forward steps then a single turn – which keeps the agent centered in the maze; the worst rollouts show irregular timing, with long idle or backward‑like actions that waste time and expose the agent to hazards. These differences in action regularity, early alignment, and gentle correction directly translate into higher cumulative rewards for the high‑performing rollouts.
