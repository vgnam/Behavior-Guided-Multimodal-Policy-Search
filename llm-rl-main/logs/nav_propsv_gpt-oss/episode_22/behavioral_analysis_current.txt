**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across the 5 better rollouts the agent *keeps its motion incremental* – it advances a short distance each step, preserving a modest speed and only gently adjusting its heading. This slow‑and‑steady phase lasts for at least two‑three frames before any large turn or drift appears, allowing the agent to collect positive step‑rewards while staying inside the corridor. When the trajectory finally becomes unstable the accumulated reward is already high, so the net return stays above the worst cases. In short, **gradual forward progress with small‑magnitude actions early‑to‑mid episode** is the behaviour that most often lifts the score.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
The low‑reward rollouts share a *single, high‑amplitude thrust* (or an abrupt change in state value) that propels the agent straight into a wall within the first two frames. The action magnitude is clearly larger than in the successful runs, and the agent makes virtually no intermediate corrections; the episode terminates almost immediately with a large negative penalty. Thus **large‑step, aggressive actions taken right after the start** repeatedly drive the score down.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** (ranked by how uniquely they separate the groups)  

| Rank | Pattern | Presence in high‑reward | Presence in low‑reward | Recommended policy change |
|------|---------|------------------------|------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | **Incremental action magnitude (Δstate ≤ 0.5 per step) for the first 2–3 steps** | 4 / 5 rollouts | 0 / 5 rollouts | Strongly reinforce a penalty for large Δstate early; bias the policy toward small‑step outputs. |
| 2 | **Sustained forward motion without a wall contact for ≥ 3 consecutive steps** | 3 / 5 rollouts | 0 / 5 rollouts | Add a bonus term or shaping reward for “no‑collision streaks” and increase the value of the “continue‑forward” action. |
| 3 | **Early orientation roughly parallel to the inner wall (angle error ≤ 0.2 rad)** | 3 / 5 rollouts | 1 / 5 rollouts | Slightly encourage alignment with the corridor wall during the first decision. |

Patterns 1–3 are the clearest discriminators; all other observed behaviours (e.g., occasional late drift) appear in both groups and are therefore less actionable.

**4. TIMING**  
The top discriminative pattern (small Δstate for the first 2–3 steps) occurs **at the very start of the episode**, making it a likely causal driver of success rather than a downstream correlation. The second pattern (≥ 3‑step collision‑free streak) spans the early‑mid phase and also precedes any crash, so it is likewise causally relevant. The orientation‑alignment cue is an *initial* signal (first frame) that helps set up the safe incremental motion. Consequently, the optimizer should prioritize adjusting the policy’s early‑step output distribution rather than trying to fix behaviours that emerge only later.
