High‑reward rollouts consistently interleave a primary forward action (0) with frequent short corrective actions (1 or 2), creating a rhythmic “forward‑adjust‑forward” pattern that keeps the agent near the centre of the corridor and prevents it from drifting into walls; low‑reward episodes lack these adjustments, instead sustaining a single forward command for many steps or applying an immediate large thrust, which quickly drives the agent into a boundary or off the track. In the successful runs the agent applies modest thrust early on and then uses periodic small‑angle turns (action 2) at regular intervals (≈ every 30–50 steps), allowing it to navigate bends smoothly, whereas the failures either never turn or execute a sudden, aggressive turn only once right before termination. The high‑reward trajectories also exhibit early‑stage oscillations—brief backward or lateral moves (action 1) that correct overshoot—so the cumulative state values stay within a stable range, while the low‑reward cases show a monotonic decrease in state value with no mid‑episode correction, leading to uncontrolled descent or collision. Finally, successful rollouts keep the magnitude of each reward change modest and balanced, reflecting controlled progress, whereas the poor rollouts contain a few very large positive spikes followed by a single catastrophic negative spike, indicating that unchecked acceleration followed by a lack of corrective behavior drives the large reward loss. These contrasting timing and action‑selection patterns explain why the oscillatory, corrective style yields high cumulative reward and the single‑action, uncorrected style results in failure.
