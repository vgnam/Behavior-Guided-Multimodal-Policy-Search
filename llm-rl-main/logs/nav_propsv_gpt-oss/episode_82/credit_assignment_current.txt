High‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained forward action and keep the forward bias for many consecutive steps, only inserting brief, small‑angle corrections instead of large, sudden turns; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts contain an early, abrupt turn (often the very action that triggers termination) or a rapid switch to backward motion. The successful trajectories also limit the magnitude of positional reversals, showing at most minor drifts before resuming forward progress, whereas the poor rollouts exhibit pronounced backward steps and repeated back‑and‑forth oscillations that waste time and push the agent toward obstacles. When a turn is required, high‑reward episodes execute it smoothly and immediately follow with another forward move, while low‑reward episodes either over‑rotate or linger in the turning state, creating prolonged indecision and loss of momentum. Finally, the high‑reward runs avoid any final “panic” action that produces a large negative reward, whereas the low‑reward runs end with a sudden, uncontrolled action that crashes the agent, directly accounting for the stark reward gap.
