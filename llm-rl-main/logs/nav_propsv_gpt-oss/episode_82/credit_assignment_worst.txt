High‑reward rollouts all contain at least one moment where the agent switches away from the default “action 2” thrust – either inserting a brief forward push (action 0) or a corrective turn – and then settles into a short, smoother forward segment before the episode ends. In contrast, every low‑reward rollout sticks with action 2 for the entire horizon, producing a persistent oscillatory motion with no purposeful re‑orientation or speed change. The occasional action change in the better runs lets the agent gain a few timesteps of positive progress (or avoid a wall) that reduces the cumulative penalty, whereas the unwavering thrust in the worst runs forces the agent to continually bounce off walls and accrue the maximum negative reward. This timing pattern – brief, purposeful deviation followed by a controlled glide versus relentless, unvarying thrust – plausibly explains why the high‑reward episodes finish with a less severe score.
