The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, decisive maneuver—typically a sharp turn or thrust (often a sudden change from action 2 to a different action) that sends the agent into a failure state within only a few timesteps, so the episode ends quickly and the accumulated penalty is limited. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts lack this early reversal and instead repeat the same action (usually action 2) for most of the episode, producing long‑lasting oscillations or slow drift that keep the agent alive for many steps and therefore accrue far larger negative rewards. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories show a brief window of relatively smooth forward motion before the abrupt crash, whereas the low‑reward runs either persist in a noisy oscillatory pattern or maintain a monotonic progression without any abrupt corrective action, both of which prevent early termination. This difference in timing—quick, decisive collapse versus prolonged unstable wandering—directly explains why the “high‑reward” rollouts receive a less severe total penalty than the consistently low‑reward ones.
