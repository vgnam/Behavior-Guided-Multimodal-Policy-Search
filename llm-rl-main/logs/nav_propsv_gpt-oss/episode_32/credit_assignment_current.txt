High‑reward rollouts repeatedly exhibit sustained forward bursts (action 2) followed by a single, well‑timed turn, allowing the agent to progress deep into the maze before making any correction, whereas low‑reward rollouts intersperse forward moves with frequent backward actions (action 1) and rapid, jittery turn‑arounds that keep the agent near its start. The successful runs also delay corrective adjustments until after a clear segment has been traversed, resulting in only occasional, brief oscillations, while the poor runs constantly over‑correct at corners, producing many small, back‑and‑forth motions that waste steps and incur negative rewards. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s turn decisions are clustered around maze junctions and are executed with a single, decisive rotation, whereas the low‑reward episodes show multiple micro‑turns within the same junction, indicating indecision and loss of momentum. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories include short pause periods that stabilize orientation before the next forward burst, a pattern absent in the low‑reward sequences where pauses are replaced by immediate opposite‑direction moves that destabilize the path. These differences—long forward runs, delayed and decisive turning, minimal backward motion, and strategic pausing—reduce unnecessary oscillations and keep the agent on reward‑rich sections of the environment, thereby explaining the observed reward gap.
