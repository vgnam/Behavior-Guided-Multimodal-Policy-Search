High‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained forward action (action 0) that carries the agent deep into the arena before any turning is attempted, whereas low‑reward runs either turn early (action 2 or 1) or intersperse forward moves with abrupt backward steps. In the successful episodes the agent introduces turns only after a short, stable forward segment and the turn is modest – a single action 2 followed quickly by another forward step – which keeps the trajectory smooth; the poor runs exhibit large, back‑to‑back turns or immediate reversals that push the state out of bounds. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories maintain a regular rhythm of “forward → small correction → forward” without long pauses, while the low‑reward ones contain irregular gaps where the agent stalls or over‑corrects, leading to delayed responses and collisions. Finally, the best rollouts avoid any prolonged backward thrusts (negative‑reward actions) and instead use brief, low‑amplitude adjustments, a pattern that is absent in the failing rollouts where backward actions dominate the later frames, causing the agent to lose control and terminate early. These timing and action‑selection differences keep the agent oriented toward the goal and prevent the destabilizing oscillations that drive down the reward.
