[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave a brief forward thrust (action 0) with immediate corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) within just a few frames, creating short “burst‑and‑pause” cycles that keep the state hovering near the corridor centre. In contrast, the low‑reward episodes either launch a long uninterrupted forward push or delay any corrective turn, allowing the state to drift to the walls before a sudden large‑magnitude action is taken, which provokes the terminal negative reward. The successful rollouts also exhibit a balanced use of all three actions throughout the episode, whereas the failures show a bias toward a single action (often prolonged action 0 or a single aggressive action 2) and lack the frequent mid‑episode adjustments. Because the environment rewards staying within tight passages, the early, rapid corrections in the high‑reward cases prevent boundary collisions, while the delayed or absent corrections in the low‑reward cases cause overshoots and crashes that drastically reduce the return.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave short forward moves with immediate, modest corrective actions (typically a single turn or slight back‑track) that keep the agent centered in the corridor, whereas low‑reward runs often issue long uninterrupted forward thrusts that push the agent straight into walls. The successful trajectories also show early, frequent oscillations that quickly settle into a smooth, clockwise or forward‑biased rhythm, while the poor trajectories display a single early drift followed by no further adjustments until a sudden, large turn causes a crash. Moreover, the best rollouts time their sharper turns early and use them sparingly, using gentle turns only when the state value begins to drop, whereas the worst rollouts delay turning until the very end, then execute an abrupt, high‑magnitude turn that yields a massive negative reward. This pattern of continual micro‑corrections and early, measured turning prevents collisions and sustains positive reward accumulation, explaining the large performance gap.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain a short, decisive burst of forward thrust (often a single action 2) followed quickly by a change in direction or an early termination, producing a monotonic, mostly unidirectional state trajectory with little oscillation; by contrast, the low‑reward rollouts sustain the same forward action for many timesteps, causing the state value to swing wildly up and down. In the better rollouts the agent makes an explicit turn or a brief backward correction soon after the initial thrust, which curtails the forward momentum before it can collide with a wall, whereas the poor rollouts never introduce a corrective action and keep pushing forward until the episode ends in a crash. The timing of the direction change in the high‑reward cases—typically within the first few frames—keeps the agent’s path short and limits exposure to hazardous geometry, while the low‑reward episodes linger in the environment for hundreds of frames, accumulating large penalties. Finally, the high‑reward trajectories display smooth, single‑axis drift (downward or along a wall) with minimal jitter, whereas the low‑reward trajectories are marked by persistent jittery oscillations that reflect an unstable control loop, directly translating into the large negative rewards observed.
