The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early deviation from a pure forward thrust – either a switch to action 1 (a turn) or a brief pause before the next move – whereas the low‑reward episodes persist with action 0 for every observed timestep, driving straight into a wall almost immediately. This early re‑orientation gives the agent extra time to adjust its heading, which is reflected in the longer episode lengths (several frames before termination) compared with the low‑reward cases that end after one or two steps. Moreover, the higher‑reward trajectories show a gradual change in the state variable (e.g., a modest rise from ~1.5 to ~1.8) before a sudden crash, suggesting the agent was able to follow the corridor for a while; the worst rollouts jump abruptly to a low state or a large drop, indicating an instant collision with no intermediate navigation. Together, the presence of an early corrective action, a slower progression toward the obstacle, and a more extended sequence of frames consistently correlate with the comparatively better rewards.
