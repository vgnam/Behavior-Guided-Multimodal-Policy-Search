The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early non‑forward decision – a turn or a brief reduction in thrust – that lets the agent briefly follow a wall or re‑orient before the crash, whereas the low‑reward episodes apply the same forward‑only action (action = 2) at every step and never change direction. This single corrective maneuver appears within the first few frames of the better rollouts, creating a short period of stable, wall‑hugging motion that slows the buildup of large state swings; the worst rollouts lack any such timing cue and therefore oscillate wildly around a single thrust level. Because the better rollouts intermittently break the monotonic forward pressure, they avoid the extreme overshoot and repeated collisions that generate the –500 penalty, while the constantly thrusting low‑reward trajectories accumulate large, uncorrected state fluctuations that terminate with the maximum negative reward. Consequently, the presence of an early directional adjustment and variable action timing is the key behavioral component that separates the relatively higher‑reward outcomes from the uniformly disastrous ones.
