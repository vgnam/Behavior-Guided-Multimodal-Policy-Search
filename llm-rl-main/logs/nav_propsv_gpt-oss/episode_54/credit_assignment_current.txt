High‑reward rollouts keep long uninterrupted stretches of the forward action (action 0) while only inserting occasional, gentle right‑turns (action 2) to stay flush with the outer or inner wall, producing a smooth wall‑following trajectory; low‑reward rollouts instead flip rapidly between actions (0, 1, 2) producing abrupt direction changes and large jumps in the state value, which leads to collisions or dead‑ends. The successful runs also maintain a relatively constant distance‑to‑wall signal (state values staying within a narrow band), whereas the poor runs show wide oscillations that indicate over‑correction and loss of contact with the boundary. Timing-wise, the good trajectories exhibit extended periods (several frames) of the same action before a turn, while the bad ones switch actions every frame or two, creating jerky motion. Because smooth wall‑following keeps the agent inside the navigable corridor and avoids sudden impacts, it accrues steady positive rewards, whereas erratic, high‑frequency turning drives the agent into obstacles and yields large negative penalties.
