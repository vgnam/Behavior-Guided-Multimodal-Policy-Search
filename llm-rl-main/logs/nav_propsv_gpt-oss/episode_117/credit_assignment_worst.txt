High‑reward rollouts keep the agent’s state confined to a relatively narrow band and deliberately execute a wall‑following turn early in the episode, whereas low‑reward rollouts let the state swing wildly across the full range and never introduce a purposeful change of direction until the final step. In the better runs the agent occasionally inserts a “stop/turn” action (0) well before the episode ends, signalling a planned termination that prevents it from drifting into a boundary; the worst runs only switch to action 0 at the moment of collapse after prolonged, unchecked forward thrusts (action 2). These early corrective turns produce smoother trajectories with modest oscillations, while the low‑reward cases exhibit large, erratic jumps that repeatedly overshoot and then reverse, indicating over‑correction. By staying closer to the wall and limiting extreme excursions, the high‑reward behaviors avoid the catastrophic collisions that generate the large negative rewards seen in the low‑reward rollouts.
