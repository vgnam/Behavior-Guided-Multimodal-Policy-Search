High‑reward rollouts keep a steady forward thrust (action 0) for extended intervals and only insert brief, well‑timed left or right turns (actions 1 or 2) when a corner is imminent, producing a smooth, progressive trajectory; the low‑reward rollouts break this pattern with sudden halts or backward thrusts (often an unexpected action 0 or a rapid switch to action 2) that immediately generate large negative rewards and lead to collisions. The successful trajectories also show minimal oscillation—each turn is followed by a quick return to forward motion—whereas the failing runs contain frequent jittery adjustments, over‑corrections, and rapid direction flips that destabilize the path. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s orientation changes gradually, allowing its sensory input to anticipate walls, while the low‑reward cases display abrupt orientation spikes that cause the agent to run into inner boundaries. Because continuous forward progress coupled with precisely timed, brief turns maximizes distance covered without incurring penalty, whereas erratic reversals and mistimed stops waste steps and trigger crashes, this contrast directly explains the large reward gap.
