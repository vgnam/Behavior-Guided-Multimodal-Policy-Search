The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one moment where the agent deviates from the default “action 2” thrust – either by issuing a single turn (action 0) or by stopping the thrust altogether – which quickly reduces the magnitude of its state changes, whereas the low‑reward rollouts stick to action 2 for the entire episode, producing large, repeated oscillations in the state value. In the better rollouts this corrective or “no‑thrust’’ decision occurs early (within the first few hundred timesteps), truncating the episode and preventing the cumulative penalty that builds up when the agent keeps overshooting; the worst rollouts keep the aggressive thrust on continuously, so the state swings back and forth without ever stabilising. Moreover, the higher‑reward trajectories show either a single, modest forward push followed by an immediate halt or a very gentle, almost negligible movement, which limits collisions with walls, while the low‑reward runs exhibit persistent high‑amplitude swings that repeatedly drive the agent into obstacles. These timing and action‑selection differences plausibly explain why the former earn less negative rewards: they introduce occasional corrective actions that curb runaway motion, whereas the latter’s unvarying thrust leads to sustained instability and maximal penalties.
