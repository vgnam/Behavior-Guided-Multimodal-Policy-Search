High‑reward rollouts all begin with an extended burst of action 2 (steady forward thrust) that lasts several timesteps before any turn is taken, whereas low‑reward rollouts immediately alternate between actions 0/1, producing rapid, short‑lived direction changes. In the successful runs the first sharp turn (action 0) occurs only after the forward segment has moved the agent close to a waypoint, while in the poor runs turns are triggered prematurely, often while the agent is still near its start location. The high‑reward trajectories also exhibit long idle or small‑adjustment intervals followed by another sustained forward phase, creating a rhythm of “push‑then‑correct”; the low‑reward trajectories lack these pauses and instead perform continuous, jittery corrections with no clear forward momentum. Finally, the high‑reward episodes contain fewer consecutive negative‑reward steps after a turn, suggesting that the timing of turns avoids collisions or boundary violations, a pattern that is absent in the low‑reward rollouts where back‑to‑back turns lead to repeated penalties. These systematic differences in action duration, turn timing, and the presence of purposeful pause‑and‑thrust cycles plausibly explain why the former achieve higher cumulative rewards.
