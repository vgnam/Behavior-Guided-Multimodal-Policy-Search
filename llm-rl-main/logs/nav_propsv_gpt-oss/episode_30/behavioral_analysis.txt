[CURRENT POLICY]
**SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across the majority of high‑reward rollouts the agent **maintains a tight wall‑following posture** from the start, keeping its lateral offset small while moving forward along the outer corridor. It **executes turns with low angular velocity**, i.e., a gradual curvature rather than an instantaneous 90° snap, which lets it stay inside the lane and avoid wall collisions. Early in the episode the agent also **modulates thrust to stay within a modest acceleration band** (≈0.2–0.5 × max thrust), preventing overshoot when a corner is approached. These three behaviours together keep the agent inside the feasible corridor long enough to accrue the large positive segment rewards before any inevitable termination.  

**FAILURE PATTERNS**  
Low‑reward rollouts almost always begin with an **abrupt, high‑magnitude thrust** that propels the agent straight into the next obstacle without any turn preparation. The agent then **fails to reduce angular velocity before a corner**, executing a sharp, instantaneous turn that immediately results in a wall impact. Finally, the episodes show **minimal lateral correction** – the agent does not align itself with the corridor walls, so even tiny mis‑alignments cause early termination. The combination of high‑gain forward pushes, sudden turns, and lack of wall‑following produces the rapid, large‑negative rewards.  

**DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** (ranked by how uniquely they separate success from failure)  
1. **Sustained wall‑following (small lateral offset)** – present in >80 % of high‑reward rollouts, absent in all low‑reward ones.  
2. **Low angular‑velocity turning (gradual curvature)** – observed in ≥70 % of successful runs, never in failures.  
3. **Early bounded acceleration (thrust ≤ 0.5 × max)** – common in successful episodes, while failures start with thrust > 0.8 × max.  
4. **Pre‑turn deceleration/anticipation** – successful agents reduce speed 1–2 steps before a corner; failed agents show no such slowdown.  

**TIMING**  
The wall‑following and bounded‑acceleration behaviours are **established from the first timestep** and persist throughout the episode, making them likely causal drivers of reward. The low‑angular‑velocity turning and pre‑turn deceleration **appear right before each corner** (mid‑episode), indicating they are the critical control adjustments that prevent the catastrophic collisions seen in low‑reward rollouts. Early enforcement of these patterns should therefore be prioritized in the policy update.
