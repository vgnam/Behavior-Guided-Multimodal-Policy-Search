High‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained forward thrust (action 2 or 0) and keep that motion for several steps before making a single, small‑angle correction (action 1) exactly as a corner is approached, which lets the agent hug the wall smoothly; low‑reward rollouts either switch abruptly to a hard turn or stop (action 0) early or wait too long, producing large, sudden direction changes that send the agent into walls. The timing of the corrective action is also consistent in the successful runs – it occurs within one or two frames of detecting a turn – whereas the poor runs show delayed or multiple back‑and‑forth corrections that create oscillations and loss of control. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s speed remains roughly constant, only modulating slightly for the corner, while the low‑reward episodes exhibit jerky speed spikes followed by rapid deceleration, which amplifies instability. These smooth, well‑timed adjustments keep the agent on the intended path and accrue positive rewards, whereas the erratic, mistimed turns and speed fluctuations cause collisions or premature terminations and thus much lower scores.
