The high‑reward rollouts all contain a short burst of forward thrust followed quickly by a change of action that stabilises the agent’s motion, whereas the low‑reward episodes keep the same forward‑thrust action (2) for the entire episode, producing relentless oscillations. In the better rollouts the aggressive acceleration ends after only a few frames and is replaced by corrective or idle actions, preventing the agent from overshooting and colliding; the worst rollouts never make this switch, so they continuously over‑correct and bounce back and forth. This timing pattern—early termination of high‑power moves and introduction of varied actions—appears consistently in the higher‑reward cases and is absent in the lower‑reward ones. Because the variable‑action pattern limits collisions and keeps the trajectory smoother, it yields less negative reward than the always‑thrust, oscillatory behaviour of the worst rollouts.
