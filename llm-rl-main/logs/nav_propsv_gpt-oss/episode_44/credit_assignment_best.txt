High‑reward rollouts all begin with a short burst of steady forward actions (action 0) that quickly establishes a safe trajectory, after which the agent interleaves brief corrective moves (alternating actions 1 and 2) to keep its state within narrow bounds; low‑reward rollouts either skip these early forward bursts or replace them with a single large thrust that drives the state far from the safe corridor. The successful episodes also exhibit regular, short‑duration correction cycles—typically a few steps of action 1 followed by a few steps of action 2—whereas the poor episodes show either no corrections or a single prolonged corrective phase that comes too late, allowing the agent to drift into danger. Timing is another discriminant: high‑reward runs apply corrections at every change of direction (every 2–4 frames), keeping the motion smooth, while low‑reward runs let the state accelerate for many consecutive frames before any adjustment, producing jerky, unstable trajectories. Finally, the high‑reward behavior maintains moderate, bounded state values throughout the episode, preventing the large negative rewards that occur in low‑reward rollouts when the state spikes abruptly; the absence of these bounded, regularly corrected motions in the failures explains their much lower cumulative reward.
