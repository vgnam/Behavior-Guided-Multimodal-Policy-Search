High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a series of modest, forward‑only actions (action 0) that keep the agent’s position changing gradually, allowing it to explore the environment without sudden jumps; the occasional, well‑timed switch to action 1 produces a controlled turn that captures a positive reward before the episode ends. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts feature an immediate, aggressive thrust—often a single large change in state after the first action—or an abrupt switch to a turning action that produces a rapid, uncontrolled displacement and early termination. The high‑reward episodes also delay any major directional change until the agent has built momentum, whereas the failures execute sharp turns or large accelerations almost instantly, leading to collisions. By maintaining smooth, incremental motion and reserving sharp maneuvers for moments when the agent is already positioned safely, the policy avoids premature crashes and accrues higher cumulative rewards.
