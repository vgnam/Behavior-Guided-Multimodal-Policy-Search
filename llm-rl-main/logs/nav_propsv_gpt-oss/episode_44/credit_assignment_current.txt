High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a prolonged sequence of forward actions (action 0) that keeps the agent advancing toward the goal before any turns are introduced, whereas low‑reward rollouts often insert a turn (action 1 or 2) within the first few steps, breaking forward momentum and leading to early collisions. When corrective turns do occur in the successful runs, they are timed after a clear decline in state value—typically around the middle of the episode—and are followed by another sustained stretch of forward moves, creating a pattern of “advance‑then‑adjust‑then‑advance”; the failing rollouts either never recover after a turn or make multiple rapid, alternating turns without a stabilising forward phase. The high‑reward trajectories exhibit smooth, monotonic increases in cumulative reward during these forward phases, while the low‑reward trajectories show frequent reward spikes and drops that correspond to oscillatory actions (quick alternation between actions 0, 1, 2). Additionally, successful episodes tend to limit unnecessary lateral motion, keeping lateral actions (1 or 2) to brief, purposeful bursts, whereas the poor episodes display erratic side‑to‑side jitter throughout, preventing the agent from maintaining a coherent path. This combination of early committed forward motion, well‑timed single corrections, and minimal oscillatory turning plausibly explains why the high‑reward rollouts accumulate far greater returns than the low‑reward ones.
