High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a short burst of sustained forward actions (action 0) that quickly accumulates positive reward before any turn is made, whereas the low‑reward episodes either start with minimal forward movement or immediately introduce erratic turns that prevent early reward buildup. Throughout the successful runs the agent intersperses turns sparingly and only at clear corner points, keeping the turn‑to‑forward ratio low; in contrast, the failing runs exhibit rapid, repeated switches between backward (action 1) and sideways (action 2) inputs, producing high‑frequency oscillations that destabilize the trajectory. The good rollouts also show timely corrective adjustments—short, purposeful turn sequences that realign the agent with the maze wall—while the poor rollouts over‑correct, swinging back and forth around a point and eventually drifting into walls. This disciplined pattern of front‑loaded forward motion, limited and well‑timed turning, and avoidance of prolonged backward/sideways bursts lets the agent stay on a productive path, whereas the opposite behavior leads to loss of control and large negative penalties.
