High‑reward rollouts contain long uninterrupted sequences of action 0 (forward) that are punctuated only by a single, well‑timed turn (action 1 or 2) after several forward steps, whereas low‑reward rollouts constantly interleave short forward bursts with frequent turns, creating a jerky, oscillatory pattern. The successful rollouts also exploit the known positive reward of action 0 (+5) and deliberately minimise the use of actions 1 and 2, which repeatedly incur small penalties (‑0.5); the poorer rollouts waste time by turning excessively and thus accrue those penalties. In the high‑reward trajectories the agent commits early to a corridor—turning twice at the start and then maintaining forward motion for many frames—while the low‑reward episodes show delayed or premature turning that leads to back‑tracking and missed shortcuts. Moreover, the high‑reward rollouts exhibit smoother velocity profiles, with fewer rapid accelerations followed by decelerations, indicating stable control, whereas the low‑reward rollouts display abrupt thrusts and stops that waste steps and increase collision risk. These timing and action‑selection differences directly translate into higher cumulative rewards by maximizing forward progress and minimizing corrective, penalised moves.
