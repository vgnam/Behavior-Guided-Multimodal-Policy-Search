High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a modest forward push followed quickly by a corrective turn (often an early action 0) that steers the agent away from nearby walls, whereas low‑reward episodes start with an aggressive, sustained forward thrust (repeated action 2) that leaves no time for adjustment and drives the agent into obstacles. Throughout the successful runs the agent alternates forward motions with short, purposeful backward or lateral tweaks, creating a rhythmic “oscillatory” pattern that keeps its trajectory centered in the corridor; the failing runs lack this alternation and instead maintain a single direction until a sudden, uncontrolled crash. The timing of the corrective actions in the high‑reward cases is regular – a few steps after each forward segment – which prevents overshoot, while the low‑reward cases either omit corrections entirely or apply a single late turn that is too late to avoid collision. By continuously using sensor‑driven adjustments rather than relying on a single large thrust, the high‑reward policy maintains safe distances from walls and reaches the goal, directly translating into the large positive cumulative reward.
