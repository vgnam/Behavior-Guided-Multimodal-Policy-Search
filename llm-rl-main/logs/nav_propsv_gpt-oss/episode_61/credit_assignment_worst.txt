The high‑reward rollouts all contain a distinct, early change of action – usually a brief “2” followed by a different command (often “0”) that produces a single large state jump and terminates the episode after only a few steps, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep action = 2 unchanged for the entire horizon, generating long, noisy oscillations. This early decisive maneuver caps the number of timesteps over which step‑wise penalties are accrued, so the cumulative reward stays around –170 instead of drifting to the worst‑case –500. In contrast, the low‑reward trajectories exhibit sustained, repetitive motion with no action variability, causing the agent to remain in the maze and repeatedly incur the per‑step cost. The abrupt termination in the high‑reward cases therefore prevents the accumulation of additional negative rewards, while the endless oscillatory control in the low‑reward cases maximizes the penalty. Consequently, the presence of a quick action switch and early episode end is the key behavioral component that separates the comparatively better outcomes from the worst ones.
