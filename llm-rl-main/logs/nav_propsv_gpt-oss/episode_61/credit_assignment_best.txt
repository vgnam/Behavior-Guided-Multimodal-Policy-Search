High‑reward rollouts consistently interleave a brief forward action (0) with a corrective turn (1 or 2) every few steps, so the agent never runs many steps straight ahead without checking its heading; low‑reward rollouts often contain long uninterrupted forward streaks or a single “thrust” that pushes the state far before any turn is taken. The early‑stage turns in the successful runs appear within the first‑few frames, keeping the trajectory aligned with the corridor and preventing the large –200 penalty seen when the agent later collides; the failing runs either omit these early corrections or apply a sudden, aggressive turn only near the episode’s end. Successful episodes also keep the magnitude of each turn modest, yielding the small –0.5 penalty, whereas the poor episodes execute a large, destabilising turn that instantly flips the state into an undesirable region. By constantly correcting the heading while maintaining forward progress, the high‑reward policy avoids early termination and accumulates many +5 forward rewards, whereas the low‑reward policy’s lack of timely, incremental adjustments leads to collisions and the dramatic negative rewards.
