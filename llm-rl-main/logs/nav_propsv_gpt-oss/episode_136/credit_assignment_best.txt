High‑reward rollouts repeatedly begin with a sustained forward thrust (action 2) that carries the agent deep into the corridor before any turn is made, whereas low‑reward rollouts often issue an immediate turn or over‑correction within the first few steps. In the successful runs the agent’s corrective turns are spaced out and modest, creating a rhythm of “forward → small turn → forward” that keeps the trajectory centered and avoids contact with walls; the failing runs show rapid, large‑amplitude turns (or jittery alternating actions) that push the agent toward the boundaries almost instantly. The good trajectories also exhibit a clear pause or slower speed after each turn, allowing the new heading to settle before the next forward burst, while the poor trajectories continue turning aggressively without such settling periods, leading to oscillatory or chaotic motion. Finally, the high‑reward episodes maintain a safe margin from obstacles throughout, only adjusting direction when a corner is truly reached, whereas the low‑reward episodes collide with walls shortly after an early, mistimed turn, terminating the episode and incurring large negative rewards.
