High‑reward rollouts keep a prolonged sequence of forward‑thrust actions (action 0) at the episode start, only inserting minor corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) after several steps of positive reward, whereas low‑reward rollouts begin with an immediate sharp turn or an aggressive thrust that quickly leads to a wall collision. The successful trajectories space their corrective actions evenly, using brief, low‑amplitude adjustments that preserve forward momentum, while the failures cluster abrupt direction changes together, creating chaotic, high‑frequency steering switches. When corrections are needed, the high‑reward runs apply a single small turn and then resume forward motion, whereas the low‑reward runs over‑correct with consecutive large turns that reverse direction and cause repeated negative‑reward encounters. This timing difference means the high‑reward agent maintains a stable heading through narrow passages, reducing the chance of hitting obstacles, while the low‑reward agent’s erratic steering produces frequent collisions and premature episode termination. Consequently, the pattern of “steady forward thrust punctuated by sparse, gentle tweaks” consistently correlates with higher cumulative reward.
