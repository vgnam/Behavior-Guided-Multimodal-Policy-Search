High‑reward rollouts all contain a brief corrective action (often action 0 or 1) shortly after an initial aggressive thrust, whereas the low‑reward runs never deviate from the constant forward command action 2. This early switch lets the agent re‑orient or brake before it leaves the safe corridor, while the always‑forward policy in the low‑reward cases drives the agent into ever‑larger oscillations and eventual collisions. In the high‑reward episodes the aggressive move is limited to one or two steps and is followed by a pause or opposite thrust, which keeps the trajectory close to the inner wall; the low‑reward episodes sustain the forward thrust for the entire episode, causing the state values to swing wildly and drift outward. Because the corrective step curtails uncontrolled acceleration, the agent incurs fewer large negative penalties, producing the comparatively higher (less negative) rewards.
