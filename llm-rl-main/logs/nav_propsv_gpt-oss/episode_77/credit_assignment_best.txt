High‑reward rollouts consistently pair a dominant forward action with short, regularly‑spaced corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) that are brief enough to keep momentum but large enough to steer around obstacles, creating an “oscillatory‑control” rhythm. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts either omit these frequent micro‑adjustments—driving straight until they collide—or apply a single, overly‑aggressive turn that abruptly changes direction and leads to loss of control. The timing pattern in the successful runs shows corrective turns occurring every few timesteps, preventing the agent from lingering in a single orientation, whereas the poor runs exhibit long gaps between turns or a solitary massive turn late in the episode. This steady alternation of forward thrust and modest re‑orientation maintains forward progress while avoiding walls, directly translating into higher cumulative rewards.
