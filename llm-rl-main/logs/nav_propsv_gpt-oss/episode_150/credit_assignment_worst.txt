The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one deliberate change of action away from the default forward move, producing a sharp left or right turn that lets the agent follow an inner or outer wall for a few steps, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep using action 2 (forward) throughout the episode and never execute a turn. This early turning—usually within the first few frames—creates a short period of purposeful navigation that delays collision and therefore yields a less negative reward. In the high‑reward cases the agent also begins with a smooth, modest acceleration before the turn, while the low‑reward cases show immediate large jumps in the state value, indicating abrupt, uncontrolled thrusts that lead to erratic oscillations. The combination of varied actions (turns), timed early direction changes, and smoother acceleration in the high‑reward rollouts reduces drift and prevents the extreme penalties seen when the agent merely pushes forward with constant action 2.
