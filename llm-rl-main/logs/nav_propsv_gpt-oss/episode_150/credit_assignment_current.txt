High‑reward rollouts keep a steady stream of forward actions (action 2) and intersperse them with brief, small‑angle corrective turns (actions 0 or 1) every 2–4 frames, which keeps the agent centered in the corridor and prevents drift toward walls. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts either omit these regular forward bursts or replace them with sudden large turns or backward moves, allowing the orientation to drift and producing abrupt state jumps that lead to collisions. The timing of corrections also differs: successful runs apply quick adjustments immediately after a forward step, whereas failing runs delay corrections until the agent is already off‑course, resulting in over‑corrections and chaotic oscillations. Because the high‑reward behavior continuously reinforces forward progress while only minimally perturbing direction, it accumulates positive rewards; the opposite pattern—sporadic, aggressive or delayed actions—creates negative rewards and early terminations.
