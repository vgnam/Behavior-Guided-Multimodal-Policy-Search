High‑reward rollouts consistently contain long uninterrupted stretches of the forward action (action 0) that slowly raise the state value, punctuated only by occasional, small corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) that are spaced several timesteps apart, whereas low‑reward rollouts flip rapidly between forward and turning actions, producing jittery, over‑corrected motions and early spikes or drops in the state value. The successful trajectories also delay any “fail” termination, only invoking it after many stable steps, while the failing runs include an abrupt “fail” action immediately after a single thrust or a sudden sharp turn. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes exhibit smooth, incremental orientation changes that keep the agent away from walls, whereas the low‑reward episodes feature abrupt, sharp turns that send the agent into collisions or dead‑ends. These patterns together preserve momentum and positional stability in the good rollouts, while the erratic timing and aggressive turning in the bad rollouts quickly degrade control and trigger large negative rewards.
