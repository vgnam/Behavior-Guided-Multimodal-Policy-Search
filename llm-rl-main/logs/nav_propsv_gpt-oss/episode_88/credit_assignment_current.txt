High‑reward rollouts all feature long uninterrupted stretches of forward thrust (action 0) punctuated by brief, purposeful turns—usually a single sharp right turn—after which the agent promptly resumes forward motion, keeping its state values relatively stable; low‑reward rollouts, in contrast, contain frequent back‑and‑forth or backward actions (e.g., action 1) and rapid, successive direction changes that produce a noisy, oscillatory trajectory. The successful runs also time their corrective turns early (within the first few hundred frames) and then allow a period of stabilization, whereas the poor runs delay or repeat corrections, leading to over‑correction and drift. When a turn is made in the high‑reward cases, the agent quickly re‑aligns and proceeds without lingering wobble, while the low‑reward episodes exhibit lingering wobble and repeated over‑steering that culminate in collisions. This disciplined pattern of “forward‑most, turn‑once‑then‑stabilize” preserves momentum toward goal states and avoids the chaotic oscillations that incur large negative rewards. Consequently, the presence of sustained forward motion and minimal, well‑timed turning explains the large reward gap between the two groups.
