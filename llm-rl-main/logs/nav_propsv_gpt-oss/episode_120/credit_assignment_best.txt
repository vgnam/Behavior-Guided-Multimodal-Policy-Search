High‑reward rollouts constantly interleave forward moves (action 0) with short, frequent corrective turns (actions 1 and 2) every 2–4 steps, keeping the state value in a moderate range and preventing the agent from drifting into walls; in contrast, the low‑reward examples contain long monotonic stretches of action 0 (sometimes hundreds of steps) followed by a single abrupt turn that produces a large state jump and a collision. The successful trajectories also begin their first turn almost immediately after the initial forward step, whereas the poor runs delay any turning until after a prolonged straight segment, allowing momentum to carry the agent into unsafe regions. Moreover, high‑reward episodes exhibit a pattern of brief, low‑amplitude oscillations that continuously correct the heading, while the low‑reward cases show either no oscillation (flat forward motion) or a single over‑correction that flips the orientation sharply. This consistent early‑and‑regular correction prevents extreme state changes that trigger heavy penalties, explaining why the mixed‑action, tightly timed pattern yields substantially higher total reward.
