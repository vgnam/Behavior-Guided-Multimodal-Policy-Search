High‑reward rollouts repeatedly employ short bursts of **action 0** (forward thrust) followed quickly by a corrective **action 2** (right turn) that steers the agent back toward the central corridor, producing a rhythmic “forward‑then‑right” cadence that keeps the state values hovering around the optimal range; in low‑reward rollouts this cadence is missing, with long stretches of a single action (often only 0 or only 2) that let the agent drift into extreme high or low states and terminate prematurely. The successful episodes also execute **timely corrective turns** within the first few timesteps after an initial forward move, preventing the rapid descent seen in the failing runs where the agent continues straight until the height collapses. Moreover, high‑reward trajectories display **small‑amplitude oscillations**—brief back‑and‑forth adjustments that correct overshoot without abandoning progress—whereas the low‑reward cases exhibit either no oscillation (a monotonic slide) or large, destabilising swings that push the state beyond safe limits. Finally, the best rollouts consistently **avoid sustained sequences of action 1** (left turn), which in the poor rollouts lead to prolonged lateral movement and eventual collisions, indicating that disciplined use of forward and right‑turn actions together with rapid correction is the key differentiator in reward.
