High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a single, confident forward thrust (action 1) that quickly moves the agent into open space and then pause or switch to a neutral action, allowing the vehicle to stabilize before any later maneuver; low‑reward rollouts instead keep accelerating (repeated action 0) without a stabilising pause, which drives the agent straight into walls. When a turn is needed, the better rollouts execute it only after the initial thrust has settled, often using a gentle, delayed adjustment, whereas the poor rollouts make abrupt cornering actions (e.g., an immediate action 2) that immediately throw the agent off the corridor. The timing of the thrust also differs: successful episodes sustain the forward motion for many steps before any sharp change, giving the agent time to gather reliable sensor feedback, while failures show rapid, successive actions that leave little time for correction. This combination of an early, decisive thrust followed by a brief period of controlled inertia, and the avoidance of sudden early turns, appears to keep the agent within safe lanes longer and thus yields the higher cumulative rewards.
