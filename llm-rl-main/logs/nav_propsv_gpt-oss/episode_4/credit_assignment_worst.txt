The high‑reward rollouts all contain a short burst of the forward‑thrust action 2 followed almost immediately by a change of action (often 0 or a small rotation), which causes the episode to terminate after only a few steps; the low‑reward rollouts, by contrast, persist with action 2 for dozens of frames, producing long, oscillatory trajectories that accumulate large negative rewards. In the better rollouts the agent makes a single, purposeful turn or a brief wall‑following maneuver—evidenced by a quick switch to a different action—whereas the poorer rollouts never break the monotony of forward thrust and instead swing back and forth along the same axis. This early diversification of actions prevents the agent from drifting into costly collisions or endless loops, keeping the total penalty small. The low‑reward trajectories show repeated overshoots and resets (large jumps in state) that only occur when the agent keeps applying the same action despite worsening position, amplifying the penalty. Consequently, brief, adaptive action changes and early episode termination are the key behavioral differences that explain why the high‑reward rollouts achieve markedly better scores.
