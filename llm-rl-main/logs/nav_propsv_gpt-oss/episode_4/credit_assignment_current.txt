High‑reward rollouts all contain a clear, sustained burst of action 0 (forward thrust) that is triggered after an initial positioning phase, whereas the low‑reward runs either never produce such a burst or abort before it can begin. In the top rollouts the agent first follows the outer wall using alternating actions 1 and 2, then—typically around frame 8‑10—switches to a straight‑ahead drive that rapidly advances toward the goal; the low‑reward episodes either continue wobbling with actions 1/2, revert to a chaotic mix of actions, or terminate immediately after a single forward step. The successful trajectories also show a short period of smooth, monotonic forward motion before any oscillatory corrections appear, while the failures exhibit immediate over‑corrections or abrupt resets that prevent accumulation of positive reward. Finally, when oscillations do occur in the high‑reward cases they are bounded by a preceding forward surge, whereas in the low‑reward cases the oscillations dominate the entire episode, leading to missed targets and collisions. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the high‑reward rollouts gather much larger returns.
