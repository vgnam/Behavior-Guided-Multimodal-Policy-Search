[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts all keep a sustained forward thrust for several consecutive steps before any turn, using only small corrective actions to fine‑tune their heading; this yields smooth, monotonic progress and lets the agent stay inside the corridor. By contrast, the low‑reward episodes make an immediate, large change in state—often a single sharp turn or a sudden reversal—so the agent jumps toward a wall or away from the goal and terminates early. The successful trajectories also postpone aggressive steering until after they have built momentum, whereas the failures apply strong steering inputs (the “sharp turn” action) right at the start, producing over‑correction and instability. Finally, the high‑reward behavior exhibits gradual, low‑amplitude oscillations that correct drift without breaking forward motion, while the low‑reward cases show abrupt, high‑amplitude swings that destabilize the vehicle and lead to collisions, directly accounting for the large reward gap.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently alternate a short burst of forward moves (action 0) with early, modest corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) that keep the state inside a narrow band, whereas low‑reward runs either press forward for too long or make abrupt, large‑angle turns only after the state has already drifted far from the safe region. In the successful episodes the first turn appears within the first few timesteps, preventing the agent from accumulating excess momentum and steering it back toward the centre; the failing episodes either delay that turn until the agent is already near a boundary or replace it with an erratic, high‑frequency switching that amplifies oscillations. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories rarely execute action 0 when the state is close to a terminal threshold, opting instead for a corrective turn that averts collapse, while the low‑reward trajectories often persist with action 0 right up to the point of termination. These disciplined, timely corrections limit state excursions and maintain smooth progress, whereas the absent or mistimed adjustments in the low‑reward rollouts lead to large deviations, collisions, and large negative rewards.

[WORST POLICY]
The higher‑reward rollouts all contain at least one moment where the agent deviates from a monotonic “forward‑only” policy – they either switch to a left/right turn (action 0 or 1) or briefly reverse (action 2→0) within the first few timesteps, producing a quick corrective maneuver that steers the robot away from a wall or corner. In contrast, the low‑reward episodes keep the same forward action (action 2) for the entire horizon, resulting in relentless oscillation against the maze boundaries and repeated collisions. This early, purposeful change of direction limits the amount of time the agent spends in high‑penalty states, so the episode ends with a less severe cumulative penalty. By contrast, the never‑changed forward thrust in the worst rollouts forces the agent to bounce repeatedly, amplifying negative rewards and driving the total score toward the minimum.
