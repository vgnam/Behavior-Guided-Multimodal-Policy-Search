The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, decisive deviation – either a sharp turn or a non‑forward action (often “0”) within the first few frames – that forces the episode to terminate quickly, capping the number of per‑step penalties and thus keeping the total reward relatively less negative. By contrast, the low‑reward rollouts persist with the forward‑thrust action “2” for most of the episode, producing long, oscillatory trajectories that repeatedly hit the walls or drift without stopping, which accumulates many negative step rewards before the inevitable termination. The abrupt direction changes seen in the better rollouts (e.g., rapid right turns) break the oscillatory pattern and prevent the agent from entering the sustained wobble that characterises the poorer runs. Consequently, the early termination and occasional early “stop” action in the high‑reward cases limit exposure to penalty, while the prolonged, monotonic forward motion with continuous oscillations in the low‑reward cases drives the large negative returns.
