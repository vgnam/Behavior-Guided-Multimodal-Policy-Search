High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a solid stretch of forward motion (action 0) and then execute a sharp right‑turn (action 2) almost immediately when a corner is encountered, keeping the agent tightly on the intended corridor; low‑reward rollouts either linger in forward motion or wander before turning, often inserting extra left‑turns or stops that waste steps. The successful trajectories show only brief, purposeful oscillations—typically a single corrective adjustment lasting one or two frames—whereas the poorer runs contain prolonged back‑and‑forth swings (repeated action 1 or alternating 0/2) that drift the agent toward walls. Timing of the turn is another distinguishing factor: in high‑reward episodes the turn occurs within the first few frames of the corner, while in low‑reward episodes the same decision is delayed, allowing the state value to degrade and sometimes leading to collisions. These patterns give the high‑reward agent steady forward progress and efficient navigation, whereas the erratic, delayed, and overly corrective behaviors of the low‑reward agent squander time and increase the chance of hitting boundaries, directly lowering cumulative reward.
