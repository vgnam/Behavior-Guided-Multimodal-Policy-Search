High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a brief, decisive alignment phase – a single turn (action 0) followed almost immediately by sustained forward motion (action 1 or 2) – whereas low‑reward rollouts linger in the first few steps, alternating turns and forward moves and creating prolonged early oscillations. Once aligned, the high‑reward episodes contain long uninterrupted forward stretches that carry the agent quickly through corridors, while the low‑reward episodes intersperse short forward moves with frequent corrective turns, causing the agent to waste steps and drift near walls. When a corner is reached, the high‑reward policy executes a sharp turn (action 0) and then proceeds forward in the next step, whereas the low‑reward runs often over‑correct with multiple successive turns before moving ahead. This pattern of early commitment, timely corner turning, and extended forward progress reduces unnecessary back‑and‑forth motion, allowing the agent to accumulate more positive reward per timestep compared with the jittery, indecisive behavior seen in the low‑reward rollouts.
