The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early decisive move – usually a single forward thrust (action 2) followed within one or two steps by a sharp corrective action (often action 0) that forces an immediate failure, truncating the episode after only a handful of frames. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts never introduce such a corrective turn; they persist with action 2 for the entire episode, producing long, jittery trajectories that oscillate back and forth for dozens of steps. This timing difference means the high‑reward trajectories accrue far fewer per‑step penalties before the terminal negative reward is applied, whereas the low‑reward runs continually add small penalties through their prolonged oscillations. Consequently, the brief, abrupt crash pattern in the high‑reward rollouts limits total loss, while the sustained, undirected oscillatory behavior in the low‑reward rollouts drives the much larger negative return.
