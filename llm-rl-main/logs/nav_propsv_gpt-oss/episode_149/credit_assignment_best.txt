High‑reward rollouts keep a steady forward drive for several steps before issuing only modest, short‑duration turns, which preserves momentum and lets the agent stay on a smooth trajectory; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts insert abrupt, large‑angle direction changes or sudden jumps in state value early on, breaking that momentum and often sending the agent into a wall. The successful rollouts also postpone their biggest corrective actions until they are close to the goal, using fine‑grained adjustments that gradually shrink oscillations, whereas the failing rollouts make coarse, over‑correcting moves throughout the episode, causing persistent jitter and divergence from the target. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories exhibit relatively uniform action magnitudes with no spikes, while the low‑reward ones contain occasional high‑magnitude action bursts that correspond to the large negative rewards at termination. This combination of continuous forward progress, delayed precise corrections, and avoidance of sudden large actions explains why the former achieve much higher cumulative rewards.
