High‑reward rollouts all begin with modest, steady forward steps followed by only small, frequent corrective turns, whereas the low‑reward examples show either an immediate large thrust or a sudden sharp turn that drives the agent into a wall. In the successful runs the agent interleaves brief bursts of motion with short stabilisation periods, keeping its speed moderate and allowing it to react to upcoming obstacles; the failing runs maintain a high velocity for many timesteps and never insert these damping intervals. The good trajectories also display a pattern of low‑amplitude oscillations—tiny left‑right adjustments that keep the agent centered—while the poor trajectories either lack any oscillatory correction or over‑oscillate with large, erratic swings that quickly diverge from the desired path. Finally, the high‑reward episodes postpone major direction changes until after the first few hundred frames, using the early phase to map the environment, whereas the low‑reward episodes make abrupt directional decisions early, leading to premature collisions and large negative rewards. These contrasting timing and magnitude of movement and correction explain why the controlled, gradually adjusted behavior yields far higher returns.
