The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early deviation from the default “always‑thrust” action 2 – either an immediate action 0 (as in rollout 1) or a quick switch to action 0 after a short burst of forward thrust (rollout 2), or a brief corrective maneuver such as a sharp left turn before the episode ends (rollouts 4–5). By inserting this non‑2 action within the first few hundred steps the agent cuts the length of the episode, avoids prolonged oscillations and prevents a late‑stage collision, which limits the accumulated penalty and yields a less negative total reward. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts keep applying action 2 continuously for almost the entire horizon, showing no corrective or terminating action until a catastrophic crash occurs near the end. This monotonic thrust produces larger, sustained oscillations in the state, leading the agent to wander far from any safe region and incur a much larger negative reward. Consequently, early action diversification and timely termination are the behavioural components that consistently separate the comparatively higher‑reward trajectories from the worst ones.
