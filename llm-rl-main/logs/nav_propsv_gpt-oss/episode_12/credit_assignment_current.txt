High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a brief forward push followed almost immediately by a sharp right turn, after which the agent interleaves short forward bursts (action 0) with quick corrective turns (action 2) and settles into a period of low‑amplitude adjustments before the episode ends; low‑reward rollouts either omit the early turn altogether or delay it, instead committing to long, uninterrupted forward thrusts that drive the agent straight into the outer corner of the L‑shaped layout. The successful trajectories also show a rhythmic alternation of forward and turn actions every few frames, keeping the orientation aligned with the corridor, whereas the poor trajectories display prolonged sequences of the same action (often forward) punctuated by abrupt, large‑scale direction changes that overshoot the intended path. By turning early, the high‑reward agent positions itself on the correct side of the bend and can fine‑tune its heading with small oscillations, which prevents wall collisions and yields steady positive reward accumulation. In contrast, the aggressive, uncorrected forward motion of the low‑reward rollouts quickly exhausts the safe region, leading to sudden loss of control, collisions, and large negative rewards.
