High‑reward rollouts all begin with an extended series of forward actions (action 0) before any turn is taken, allowing the agent to build momentum and progress toward the goal; low‑reward episodes start with an immediate sharp turn (action 1 or 2), which quickly diverts the trajectory and leads to early termination. When a turn does occur in the successful runs, it is timed after several consecutive forward steps and is executed as a single, smooth adjustment rather than a rapid back‑to‑back sequence, keeping the path stable; the failing runs exhibit multiple consecutive turn actions or abrupt direction changes within a few timesteps, creating chaotic oscillations. The high‑reward trajectories also show brief, isolated corrective turns that promptly return to forward motion, whereas the low‑reward trajectories contain clusters of turns that never resume forward movement, causing the agent to stall or hit walls. Finally, successful episodes maintain a mostly monotonic increase in the state metric, reflecting consistent forward progress, while unsuccessful ones display sudden drops or spikes in the same metric, indicating loss of control and collisions. These timing and sequencing differences explain why the former accumulate large positive rewards and the latter incur heavy penalties.
