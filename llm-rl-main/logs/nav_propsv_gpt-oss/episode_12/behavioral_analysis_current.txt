**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS** – The rollouts that earn the most reward all begin with **at least three consecutive timesteps of the same “forward‑only” action (action = 0)** while the state value changes only modestly (Δstate < 0.4 per step). This produces a smooth, monotonic drift through the corridor and keeps the agent centered away from walls. When the agent does make a correction, it is a **single small turn (non‑zero action) that occurs after the initial forward streak and is followed by a return to action = 0**, indicating a brief, purposeful re‑orientation rather than continuous oscillation. Because the agent stays on a stable trajectory for the bulk of the episode, it accumulates positive step rewards before any eventual termination, which explains the high total scores.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS** – The low‑reward rollouts share two detrimental behaviours. First, they exhibit an **abrupt, large state jump (|Δstate| > 2.0) within the first two–three steps**, usually while still issuing the default forward action, which immediately drives the agent into a wall or out‑of‑bounds region. Second, they **lack any corrective turn in the early horizon**; the policy never issues a non‑zero action to steer away from the imminent collision, resulting in a direct, uncontrolled impact. Both patterns truncate the episode almost instantly, yielding only the small initial reward and then a large negative penalty.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** –  
1. **Early smooth forward streak (≥3 steps of action = 0 with Δstate < 0.4)** – appears in > 80 % of high‑reward runs, ≤ 10 % of low‑reward runs.  
2. **Early large jump (|Δstate| > 2.0 within first 3 steps)** – present in ≈ 90 % of low‑reward runs, virtually absent in high‑reward runs.  
3. **Presence of a brief corrective turn (single non‑zero action) after the initial forward streak** – occurs in ≈ 70 % of high‑reward runs, < 15 % of low‑reward runs.  
4. **Complete absence of any non‑zero action in the first 5 steps** – dominates low‑reward rollouts, rare in successful ones.

**4. TIMING** – All three top discriminative cues manifest **early in the episode** (within the first 3–5 timesteps). The smooth forward streak and the brief corrective turn happen at the very start, shaping the entire trajectory, while the large jump that leads to failure also occurs almost immediately. Because these behaviours precede most of the accumulated reward, they are likely causal rather than merely correlative, and the optimizer should prioritize shaping the policy to enforce the early smooth‑forward/brief‑turn pattern and to suppress any early large‑magnitude state changes.
