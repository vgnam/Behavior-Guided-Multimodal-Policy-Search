The high‑reward rollouts all contain a brief, pronounced change of direction – a sharp turn or sudden thrust – that occurs early in the episode, whereas the low‑reward rollouts never deviate from a monotonic forward action and simply bounce back and forth. This early decisive maneuver produces a large jump in the state variable, quickly driving the agent into a terminal condition and truncating the episode, which limits the accumulated penalty. In contrast, the low‑reward trajectories remain stuck in a repetitive oscillation, applying the same action continuously and persisting for the full horizon, thereby accruing the maximum negative reward. The presence of a single, timed correction (or aggressive thrust) in the high‑reward cases thus explains why those rollouts end sooner and earn a less severe score than the endlessly wandering low‑reward runs.
