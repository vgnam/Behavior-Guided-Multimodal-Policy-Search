High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long stretch of action 0 (forward thrust) that lasts through the first half of the episode, keeping the state monotically decreasing and building positive reward before any turn is attempted; low‑reward episodes either introduce a turn or switch to action 2 almost immediately, causing abrupt state changes and early penalties. When a direction change is needed, the successful rollouts insert a brief, single‑step turn (action 1 or 2) and then return to action 0, whereas the poor rollouts either linger in turning actions or oscillate between actions 0 and 2 for several consecutive frames, producing over‑correction and negative rewards. The high‑reward trajectories also avoid any sudden reversal late in the episode—there is no abrupt switch back to action 0 when the state is already low—while the low‑reward runs commonly feature a final abrupt forward action that spikes the state upward and triggers a large penalty. This disciplined timing preserves momentum and prevents the destabilizing spikes that the low‑reward agents incur. Consequently, sustained early forward motion combined with minimal, well‑timed turning actions explains the systematic reward gap between the two groups.
