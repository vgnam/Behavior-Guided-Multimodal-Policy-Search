High‑reward rollouts consistently combine short bursts of forward thrust (action 0) with immediate corrective actions (actions 1 or 2) that re‑align the agent after each movement, creating a rapid “push‑and‑steer” rhythm that keeps it centered in narrow passages; low‑reward rollouts either maintain a prolonged forward thrust without such corrections or delay corrective actions until after a large deviation, causing the agent to drift into walls or dead‑ends. In the successful episodes the agent executes sharp turns early, often within a few timesteps of entering a new corridor, whereas the poor episodes postpone turning until the trajectory is already misaligned, leading to abrupt crashes. The high‑reward trajectories also exhibit periodic oscillations—alternating forward and corrective actions every 1–3 steps—that prevent the buildup of momentum that would otherwise carry the agent into obstacles, while the low‑reward runs show monotonic motion that accumulates unchecked speed. Finally, successful rollouts intersperse brief pauses or backward nudges (action 2) after a forward surge, effectively “braking” before tight corners; the failing rollouts lack these braking cues, so they overshoot corners and incur large negative rewards. These timing‑specific action patterns give the agent finer positional control and thus substantially higher cumulative rewards.
