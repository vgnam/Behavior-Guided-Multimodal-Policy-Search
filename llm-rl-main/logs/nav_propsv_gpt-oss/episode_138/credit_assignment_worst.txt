High‑reward rollouts all contain an early decisive change of action – they begin with a forward thrust (action 2) but within the first few dozen steps switch abruptly to a braking or reverse command (action 0), usually when they encounter the first corner. This quick reversal limits how far they travel into the confined corridor and prevents them from accumulating many forward‑thrust penalties, whereas the low‑reward rollouts either never issue the braking command or postpone it until the very last frame, staying in a perpetual forward thrust that produces long‑lasting oscillations. The high‑reward episodes also show a recognizable pattern of an initial straight‑line segment followed by a sharp turn attempt, even if the turn is over‑corrected, indicating that the agent at least attempts to respond to the geometry of the L‑shaped maze. In contrast, the low‑reward trajectories display smooth but aimless forward motion with only minor adjustments, lacking any clear response to upcoming bends. Because the environment penalizes time spent in the wrong region and collisions, the early corrective action in the high‑reward rollouts reduces exposure to those penalties, yielding a comparatively better (less negative) total reward.
