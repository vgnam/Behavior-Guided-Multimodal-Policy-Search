High‑reward rollouts all contain an early phase of brief exploratory actions (often a mix of 1 and 2) followed by a **sustained stretch of action 0** that propels the agent forward for several consecutive frames, whereas the low‑reward episodes either never produce such a forward‑thrust segment or terminate before it can develop. When the agent does deviate from the forward path, the successful runs insert **quick corrective actions** (a single 1 or 2) exactly after a few frames of overshoot, then immediately resume action 0, while the failing runs either make **abrupt, large state jumps** without any corrective step or apply corrective actions too late, leading to collisions. Timing-wise, the best rollouts exhibit **regular, short‑duration oscillations** (one‑step corrections) spaced at predictable intervals, whereas the poor rollouts show **irregular, long‑gap pauses** or sudden spikes in state value that precede termination. This pattern suggests that maintaining a continuous forward drive and promptly counter‑steering small errors keeps the agent on a viable trajectory, while missing the forward streak or correcting too late lets the agent drift into failure states, producing the observed reward gap.
