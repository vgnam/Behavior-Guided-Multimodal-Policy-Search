The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, purposeful change of action – a sharp turn or a brief reversal of thrust – that steers the agent onto a wall‑following path, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep the same forward‑thrust action for the entire episode and never execute a corrective turn. This early re‑orientation limits lateral drift and keeps the state values within a tighter band, so the agent spends more time progressing rather than oscillating wildly. In the better rollouts the agent also inserts short periods of reduced or opposite thrust before collisions, which slows its approach to walls and prevents the large overshoots seen in the worst rollouts. By contrast, the low‑reward episodes show persistent, unmodulated thrust that produces high‑amplitude oscillations and frequent over‑corrections, driving the state far from the intended corridor and causing earlier terminations. These timing and action‑change differences plausibly explain why the high‑reward trajectories lose less reward despite still ending in failure.
