High‑reward rollouts all begin with brief, exploratory thrusts followed quickly by a transition to steady, wall‑following motion, whereas low‑reward runs launch into a single large forward thrust that ends abruptly. The successful trajectories keep their actions modest and alternate smoothly between small forward pushes and gentle corrective turns, while the failures contain sudden, high‑magnitude direction changes that produce large spikes in state values. In the good rollouts the agent consistently aligns its heading with nearby boundaries and then proceeds parallel to them, reducing the chance of collision; the poor rollouts ignore this cue and head straight into corners or open space, leading to crashes. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes delay any sharp turns until after a few steps of stable movement, whereas the low‑reward episodes execute sharp turns early, destabilising the dynamics. This combination of early exploration, gradual speed build‑up, and disciplined boundary tracking enables the agent to accrue far higher cumulative rewards than the erratic, impulsive behaviors seen in the low‑reward rollouts.
