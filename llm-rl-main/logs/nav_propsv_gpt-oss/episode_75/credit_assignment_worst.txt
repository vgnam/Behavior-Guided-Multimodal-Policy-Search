The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early action switch – they start with a forward thrust (action 2) and then change to a different command (often action 0) within the first few dozen steps, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep issuing the same forward action for the entire episode. This brief turn or braking introduces a sudden change in the state trajectory that truncates the period of uncontrolled drift, so the agent spends far fewer steps accumulating large negative step‑rewards. In the high‑reward cases the state value spikes quickly and then the episode ends, limiting exposure to wall collisions or oscillations; the low‑reward trajectories maintain a persistent, roughly sinusoidal fluctuation in position for hundreds of steps, repeatedly incurring penalties. Consequently, the mixture of an early corrective action and an early termination in the high‑reward rollouts reduces total loss, while the monotonous, prolonged forward thrust in the low‑reward rollouts drives the reward down to the worst values.
