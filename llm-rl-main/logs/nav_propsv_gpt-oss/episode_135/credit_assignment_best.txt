High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a brief exploratory thrust followed by **long stretches of sustained forward actions (action 0)** that last many time steps before any correction, whereas low‑reward runs intersperse forward moves with **frequent backward or turn actions (actions 1/2)** almost immediately after starting. The successful trajectories also **delay their first corrective turn** until they have accumulated a stable forward velocity, while the poor runs **oscillate early**, repeatedly reversing direction and over‑correcting within a few steps. When a turn is finally needed, the high‑reward episodes execute it **once and then resume forward motion**, whereas the low‑reward episodes show **multiple rapid turn‑back‑turn sequences** that disrupt progress and cause collisions. Finally, the best rollouts maintain a **steady state‑value (or position) gradient**, indicating smooth advancement toward the goal, while the worst rollouts exhibit **sharp spikes and drops** in state value, reflecting unstable, jittery control that quickly leads to wall contact and large negative penalties. These timing and action‑selection differences explain why the former accumulate large positive rewards and the latter terminate early with low scores.
