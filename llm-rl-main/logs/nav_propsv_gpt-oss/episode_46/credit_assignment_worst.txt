The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, sharp change in action – typically a switch from the forward thrust (action 2) to a reversal or stop (action 0) within the first few timesteps – which drives the agent into a failure state quickly and truncates the episode, limiting the amount of accumulated negative reward. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts keep action 2 active for the whole episode, producing long, persistent forward thrusts that generate many small penalties as the agent oscillates and drifts without ever forcing an early termination. The brief, controlled thrust that precedes the abrupt turn in the high‑reward cases gives the agent a momentary gain in state value before the crash, whereas the low‑reward cases show no comparable corrective action and instead wander erratically for hundreds of steps. Because the total reward is summed over the episode length, the early‑crash pattern yields a comparatively higher (less negative) return, while the prolonged, uncorrected forward motion inflates the cumulative penalty and produces the worst scores.
