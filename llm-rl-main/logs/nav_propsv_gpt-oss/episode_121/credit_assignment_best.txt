High‑reward rollouts all feature extended periods of forward‑motion (action 0) that carry the agent deep into the corridor before any turn is made, and when a turn occurs it is executed smoothly and only after the agent has approached a corner, allowing the state value to decrease gradually. In contrast, the low‑reward episodes contain abrupt, early turns (often action 2 or 1) or sudden accelerations that reverse direction or collide with walls, producing spikes in the state value and immediate negative rewards. The successful trajectories also show a clear pattern of alternating forward stretches with brief, well‑timed corrective turns, whereas the failures exhibit erratic, back‑and‑forth oscillations with no sustained forward progress. Moreover, high‑reward rollouts avoid over‑correction by limiting successive turn actions; low‑reward rollouts repeatedly flip between opposite turn actions, creating instability. This disciplined timing and the restraint from unnecessary or premature turning keep the agent on a productive path, directly translating into the large reward differences observed.
