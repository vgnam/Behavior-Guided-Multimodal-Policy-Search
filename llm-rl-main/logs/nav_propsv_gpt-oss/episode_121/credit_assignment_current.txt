High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a steady forward move (action 2) followed by a single, well‑timed sharp turn (action 0 or 1) and then maintain long stretches of forward motion before any subsequent correction, producing smooth, progressive state values; low‑reward rollouts instead switch actions almost immediately, often taking a turn right after the first step and generating abrupt state drops and large negative rewards. The successful trajectories also exhibit brief pauses or small corrective adjustments rather than rapid back‑and‑forth oscillations, which keeps the agent near the intended path and avoids the destabilizing over‑corrections seen in the failing runs. Moreover, high‑reward episodes delay major direction changes until the agent has built up momentum, whereas the poor episodes make early, aggressive turns that lead to collisions or failures. These timing and action‑sequencing differences plausibly explain why the former accrue steady positive reward while the latter incur sharp penalties and early terminations.
