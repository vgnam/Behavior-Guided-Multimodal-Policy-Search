The higher‑reward rollouts all contain a brief initial “2” thrust followed quickly (within a few timesteps) by an opposite or neutral action such as “0”, which bends the trajectory away from walls and prevents a hard collision; the low‑reward rollouts never switch actions, staying on “2” for the entire episode and therefore overshooting and oscillating. In the better rollouts the agent makes a single, well‑timed correction right after entering a corner or narrow corridor, whereas the poor rollouts exhibit no corner‑specific adjustments and simply bounce back and forth. The successful trajectories also show short bursts of motion interleaved with moments of little or no thrust, producing smoother, more controlled paths, while the worst trajectories maintain a constant forward thrust that generates large amplitude oscillations. These timing and action‑switch patterns reduce contact with obstacles and keep the agent nearer the desired region, which explains why they achieve substantially higher (less negative) rewards.
