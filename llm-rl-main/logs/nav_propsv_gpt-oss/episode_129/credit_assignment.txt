[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a steady forward action and then introduce a **sharp, well‑timed turn** (often to the right) that quickly realigns the agent with the corridor before resuming forward thrusts, whereas low‑reward runs either postpone the turn or make a sudden, large‑magnitude change late in the episode, causing loss of control. The successful trajectories also exhibit **frequent short forward bursts** interleaved with brief, purposeful adjustments, keeping the state within the navigable band, while the poor runs contain long periods of unchecked acceleration or single large jumps that drive the state out of bounds. In the high‑reward cases the oscillatory corrections are **moderate and damped**, allowing the agent to settle into a stable orientation near the episode’s end; the low‑reward examples show **excessive over‑correction**, producing jerky, chaotic motion that culminates in collisions. Finally, the timing of corrective actions in the top rollouts occurs **early enough to prevent drift**, whereas the low‑reward episodes delay or skip these corrections, letting errors accumulate and dramatically reducing total reward.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early deviation from the default “forward‑only” action 2 – usually a brief turn (action 0 or 1) that re‑orients the agent toward the corridor axis before it continues forward, whereas the low‑reward rollouts never change from action 2 and simply thrust forward until they bounce off walls. This early corrective turn appears within the first few frames in the better rollouts, limiting the later overshoot and preventing the large back‑and‑forth oscillations that dominate the poor rollouts. In the higher‑reward episodes the agent also pauses or reduces speed (by switching to a non‑forward action) just before reaching a tight turn, which lets it negotiate the corner smoothly; the low‑reward sequences lack any such pause and crash into the corner, incurring the big negative penalty. Consequently, the high‑reward trajectories show a pattern of timely orientation adjustments and occasional speed modulation, while the low‑reward trajectories exhibit relentless forward thrust with no corrective timing, leading to chaotic drift and far lower returns.
