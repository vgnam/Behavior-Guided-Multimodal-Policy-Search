High‑reward rollouts repeatedly begin with a quick, decisive turn (action 0) followed by several consecutive forward‑oriented steps (action 2) that are kept for longer intervals before any corrective turn, creating a smooth, forward‑driving trajectory; low‑reward rollouts either delay the first turn, alternate rapidly between actions 0, 1 and 2, or insert short forward bursts that are immediately undone, producing fragmented, back‑and‑forth motion. The successful runs also show a clear pattern of postponing corrective actions until the agent has accumulated a sizable positive state value, whereas the poorer runs intersperse corrections almost every few timesteps, leading to frequent over‑corrections and oscillations around the same position. When a negative‑reward action (typically 1) is taken in the high‑reward trajectories, it is followed by a sustained period of the same beneficial action (often another 0 or 2), allowing the agent to recover quickly; in the low‑reward cases a negative action is usually followed by another opposite or random action, preventing recovery and extending the penalty. These timing differences keep the high‑reward agent moving efficiently toward goal regions and limit wasted steps, while the erratic timing in low‑reward rollouts wastes time correcting itself and thus accrues less cumulative reward.
