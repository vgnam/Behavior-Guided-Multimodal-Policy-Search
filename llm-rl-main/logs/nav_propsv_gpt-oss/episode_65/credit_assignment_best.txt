High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with several consecutive forward actions (action 0) that build steady momentum, only inserting corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) after a few steps, whereas low‑reward episodes either make an early sharp turn or pause before any forward motion, causing the agent to lose progress and collide. In the successful runs the timing of adjustments is regular—brief, bounded bursts of turning that quickly return the agent to forward motion—while the poor runs exhibit either overly frequent or mistimed corrections, producing jittery oscillations and loss of control. The high‑reward trajectories also keep the agent centered in open corridors, using modest backward or slight‑left actions (action 1) only when necessary to fine‑tune alignment, whereas the low‑reward cases apply large or repeated backward/left actions that drive the agent into walls. This disciplined pattern of sustained forward thrust punctuated by minimal, well‑timed steering prevents premature contact with obstacles, directly translating into the large reward gap.
