High‑reward rollouts keep a long uninterrupted stretch of the forward‑movement action (action 0) early in the episode, maintaining steadily increasing state values and only inserting occasional turns (actions 1 or 2) after the agent has travelled a safe distance; low‑reward rollouts either terminate soon after a single forward step or intersperse forward actions with rapid, back‑and‑forth switches to actions 1/2, producing frequent negative rewards and collisions. The successful trajectories also exhibit a disciplined timing pattern: they postpone sharp turns until the agent’s state has stabilized, whereas the failing runs introduce abrupt direction changes (often at the first sign of a slight deviation) that push the agent into walls or dead‑ends. Moreover, high‑reward episodes show minimal oscillatory correction—when a small negative reward occurs the agent quickly returns to action 0 and re‑establishes forward progress, while low‑reward episodes linger in oscillation, repeatedly alternating between opposite actions and never regaining momentum. This combination of sustained forward thrust, delayed and purposeful turning, and rapid recovery from minor setbacks keeps the agent in high‑reward regions of the navigation map, whereas the opposite pattern leads to early failures and large penalty spikes.
