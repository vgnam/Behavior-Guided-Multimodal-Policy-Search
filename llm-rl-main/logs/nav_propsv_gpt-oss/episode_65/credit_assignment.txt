[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep a long uninterrupted stretch of the forward‑movement action (action 0) early in the episode, maintaining steadily increasing state values and only inserting occasional turns (actions 1 or 2) after the agent has travelled a safe distance; low‑reward rollouts either terminate soon after a single forward step or intersperse forward actions with rapid, back‑and‑forth switches to actions 1/2, producing frequent negative rewards and collisions. The successful trajectories also exhibit a disciplined timing pattern: they postpone sharp turns until the agent’s state has stabilized, whereas the failing runs introduce abrupt direction changes (often at the first sign of a slight deviation) that push the agent into walls or dead‑ends. Moreover, high‑reward episodes show minimal oscillatory correction—when a small negative reward occurs the agent quickly returns to action 0 and re‑establishes forward progress, while low‑reward episodes linger in oscillation, repeatedly alternating between opposite actions and never regaining momentum. This combination of sustained forward thrust, delayed and purposeful turning, and rapid recovery from minor setbacks keeps the agent in high‑reward regions of the navigation map, whereas the opposite pattern leads to early failures and large penalty spikes.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with several consecutive forward actions (action 0) that build steady momentum, only inserting corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) after a few steps, whereas low‑reward episodes either make an early sharp turn or pause before any forward motion, causing the agent to lose progress and collide. In the successful runs the timing of adjustments is regular—brief, bounded bursts of turning that quickly return the agent to forward motion—while the poor runs exhibit either overly frequent or mistimed corrections, producing jittery oscillations and loss of control. The high‑reward trajectories also keep the agent centered in open corridors, using modest backward or slight‑left actions (action 1) only when necessary to fine‑tune alignment, whereas the low‑reward cases apply large or repeated backward/left actions that drive the agent into walls. This disciplined pattern of sustained forward thrust punctuated by minimal, well‑timed steering prevents premature contact with obstacles, directly translating into the large reward gap.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, decisive maneuver—either a sharp turn followed by a straight‑line thrust or a single, confident forward thrust that is quickly stabilized—whereas the low‑reward rollouts spend most of the episode wavering with repeated small corrections and no clear directional commitment. In the better runs the agent switches actions only once or twice (e.g., a quick right turn then a steady forward action), while the poorer runs either hold the same forward action for the entire episode or oscillate between forward and corrective actions at regular intervals (notably spikes at frames 5, 10, 16, 17). This timing contrast means the high‑reward trajectories maintain momentum and avoid excessive state fluctuations, reducing time spent in inefficient back‑and‑forth motions that accrue negative reward. Conversely, the low‑reward behavior’s continual over‑correction and internal‑state spikes waste steps and increase the chance of collisions, producing the much larger penalty.
