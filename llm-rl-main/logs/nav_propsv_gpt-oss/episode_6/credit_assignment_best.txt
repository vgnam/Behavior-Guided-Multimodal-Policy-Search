High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave a stabilising **action 0** with forward moves (action 2) and short corrective turns (action 1), keeping the state value in a modest range (≈1–2) and applying a corrective step just before a potential overshoot; low‑reward rollouts either omit action 0 altogether or use it only once, instead thrusting forward for many steps and allowing the state to spike above 3 before any turn is attempted. The successful episodes also time their turns early—typically within the first few dozen frames—so that the agent negotiates corners while the velocity is still low, whereas the poor episodes defer turning until after a long acceleration phase, producing jerky over‑corrections and loss of control near the end. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s motion is a rhythmic oscillation between forward thrust and brief re‑orientation, which continually resets momentum and prevents collisions; the low‑reward cases show prolonged straight runs followed by sudden, large‑angle turns that generate instability. This disciplined alternation of thrust, correction, and stabilisation yields steady positive rewards, while the unchecked acceleration and missing corrective actions in the bad rollouts lead to abrupt crashes and large negative penalties.
