High‑reward rollouts consistently contain extended runs of the forward thrust action (action 0) that generate large positive reward spikes, followed only later by brief, precise corrective turns (actions 1 or 2) to fine‑tune the trajectory; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts break forward thrust almost immediately with frequent turns, preventing the agent from accumulating sustained progress and often driving it into walls. The successful episodes also exhibit a clear timing pattern: a stabilizing pause after each forward burst during which the agent makes only small adjustments, whereas the poor episodes show rapid, back‑to‑back oscillations with no such pause, leading to over‑correction and loss of control. When the agent approaches a corner, high‑reward trajectories anticipate the turn and execute a single, well‑timed right‑hand turn, while low‑reward runs tend to overshoot the corner or execute multiple redundant turns, resulting in negative reward spikes. Finally, the high‑reward behavior keeps the agent away from the maze boundaries, using forward momentum to stay centered, whereas the low‑reward behavior repeatedly contacts walls, generating the large negative penalties that dominate the total return.
