High‑reward rollouts all begin with an immediate forward action and then execute short, well‑timed right‑turns (or left‑turns) after only a few steps, keeping the agent on the corridor while preserving forward momentum; the low‑reward episodes either delay those corrective turns or omit them entirely, allowing the agent to drift into walls. In the successful runs the agent repeatedly alternates between a forward action and a turn within a tight 2‑to‑3‑step window, which creates a smooth “oscillatory” trajectory that stays near the centre of the passage, whereas the failing runs contain long stretches of straight motion or sudden backward thrusts that push the state toward hazardous boundaries. The high‑reward behavior also includes a rapid re‑acceleration forward immediately after each turn, preventing loss of speed and maintaining positive per‑step rewards; low‑reward episodes often linger after a turn or execute a backward move, resulting in a rapid drop in reward and eventual collision. These timing differences plausibly explain the reward gap because early forward progress and prompt corrective turning keep the agent in safe, high‑reward regions, while delayed or absent corrections cause drift, instability, and wall crashes that heavily penalize the episode.
