High‑reward rollouts keep action 0 (forward) dominant, stringing together long uninterrupted forward runs and inserting only isolated single‑step turns that are immediately followed by forward motion, which lets the agent progress steadily along the corridor. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts contain clusters of consecutive turn actions (1 or 2) and frequent alternations between forward and turn, producing oscillatory trajectories that waste steps and drift away from the optimal path. The successful episodes also time their turns so that each rotation is quickly compensated by a forward step, preventing the agent from lingering in a rotated orientation, whereas the poor episodes exhibit delayed or missing compensations, leading to over‑corrections and repeated re‑orientation. Additionally, the low‑reward trajectories occasionally employ backward‑movement actions (action 2) that directly reverse progress and incur negative rewards, a behavior that is absent from the high‑reward sequences. These differences in action dominance, spacing of turns, and avoidance of reverse motions plausibly explain why the former achieve much higher cumulative rewards.
