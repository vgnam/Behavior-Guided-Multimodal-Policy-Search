In the better rollouts the turn is timed just before the agent reaches a corner, producing a brief, sharp re‑orientation that lets the agent stay close to the boundary without colliding; the poorer rollouts exhibit either no turn or a turn that occurs after the agent has already overshot the safe corridor, leading to an immediate crash. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes intersperse short bursts of acceleration with immediate corrective actions, creating a “fast‑then‑adjust” rhythm that prevents the state value from drifting far from the safe region, while the low‑reward episodes show monotonic acceleration followed by a sudden stop, causing large spikes in the state value and a terminal collision. These timing and action‑selection differences keep the agent within navigable space longer, yielding the modestly higher (less negative) returns.
