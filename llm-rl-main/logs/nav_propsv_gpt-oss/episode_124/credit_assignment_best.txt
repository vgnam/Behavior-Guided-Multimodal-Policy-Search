High‑reward rollouts repeatedly sustain long sequences of the forward action (0) before inserting a single, smooth turn (1 or 2), whereas low‑reward episodes intersperse turns almost every step and often follow a turn immediately with the opposite turn, creating rapid oscillations. The successful trajectories also avoid any backward‑motion pattern—no instance of the agent reversing direction is observed—while the poorest runs include a clear backward drift or a sudden reversal that leads to wall collisions. When a turn is required, the high‑reward policy executes it gradually, keeping the state values within a narrow band and then promptly returns to forward motion; the low‑reward rollouts over‑correct, producing sharp, successive left‑right swings that push the state out of range and precipitate premature termination. Finally, timing-wise, the best rollouts delay corrective actions until after several stable forward steps, giving the agent time to make progress, whereas the worst rollouts react immediately to minor deviations, resulting in fragmented, unstable paths that incur large penalties. These consistent differences—extended forward runs, single smooth turns, absence of reverse motion, and delayed, moderate corrections—explain why the high‑reward trajectories accumulate positive reward while the erratic, over‑turning low‑reward ones quickly incur large negatives.
