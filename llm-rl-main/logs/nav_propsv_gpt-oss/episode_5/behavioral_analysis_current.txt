**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across the four better rollouts the agent never fires a raw “full‑forward” command (action = 0) straight into a wall; instead it either keeps the action modest or injects a non‑zero steering signal within the first two decision steps. This early steering produces a gentler, monotonic decline in the state value rather than the huge drops seen in failures, keeping the agent inside the corridor long enough to collect any positive reward that is available. When a wall is sensed the agent’s policy produces a small corrective turn rather than persisting on the same heading, which prevents the immediate termination that dominates the low‑reward set. Consequently, episodes that include an early, low‑magnitude forward thrust combined with a quick directional adjustment end up with the highest (least negative or even positive) returns.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
In the majority of the –175 reward rollouts the policy repeatedly issues the raw forward action (action = 0) regardless of sensor readings, producing an abrupt plunge in the state value and an immediate “fail”. There is no evidence of any turn, speed reduction, or pause; the trajectory is a single, uncontrolled thrust that either collides with a wall or drops the agent out of the viable region. This pattern yields the steepest negative reward because the agent never corrects its motion and therefore never reaches any goal region before termination.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS**  
| Rank | Pattern (high vs low) | Why it matters for the optimizer |
|------|-----------------------|-----------------------------------|
| 1 | **Early non‑zero steering (or reduced forward magnitude) within the first 1–2 steps** – present in 4/5 high‑reward rollouts, absent in all low‑reward ones. | Directly prevents the catastrophic state drop; the optimizer should strongly increase the probability of selecting a turn or a smaller forward magnitude when sensors detect a nearby obstacle. |
| 2 | **Gradual state evolution (small incremental changes)** – evident in all high‑reward episodes, contrasted with the single‑step large jumps in every low‑reward episode. | Encourages smoother control gains; the policy should be penalized for actions that cause > 50 % state change in one step. |
| 3 | **Any corrective turn after the first forward move** – seen in 3/5 high‑reward rollouts, never in low‑reward rollouts. | Reinforce turn‑angle outputs that are triggered by wall‑proximity sensors; suppress policies that keep the heading constant after the initial thrust. |

**4. TIMING**  
All three discriminative patterns manifest almost immediately after episode start (within the first two decision cycles), making them likely causal rather than merely correlative. Early steering and reduced thrust appear before any collision, so adjusting the policy to react in this window should directly improve reward. Later‑stage behaviors (e.g., occasional small adjustments) are less decisive; the optimizer should prioritize shaping the initial action distribution above all.
