High‑reward rollouts repeatedly combine long stretches of forward motion (action 0) with brief, well‑timed turns (actions 1 or 2) that align the agent with the maze’s corridors, whereas low‑reward rollouts either terminate after a single turn or execute turns at inappropriate moments, causing collisions with walls. In the successful trajectories the agent postpones its first turn until after it has accumulated sufficient forward steps to clear the initial corridor (e.g., > 100 timesteps in Rollout 4), while the failing runs turn almost immediately and lose alignment with the L‑shaped path. The high‑reward episodes also show a pattern of occasional corrective oscillations that quickly settle back to forward motion, providing fine‑grained adjustments without over‑steering; the low‑reward cases lack this stabilization, showing abrupt state drops and a permanent loss of control. Moreover, the best rollouts maintain a steady forward bias even when oscillating, ensuring net progress toward the goal, whereas the poor rollouts exhibit either pure forward motion that ends abruptly at a wall or erratic, unbalanced turning that leads to early termination. These timing and action‑selection differences keep the agent inside the navigable corridor longer, accumulating positive reward, while the opposite patterns drive it into failure states with large negative penalties.
