High‑reward rollouts all contain long uninterrupted stretches of action 2 (forward) – often dozens of steps at the start – followed by only occasional turns, whereas the low‑reward rollouts intersperse short forward bursts with rapid left‑right turns almost immediately. In the successful trajectories the agent postpones corrective actions until it has built up a substantial forward displacement, while the poor trajectories repeatedly over‑correct with back‑to‑back actions 0 or 1, causing the state to oscillate around the same region. The high‑reward episodes also show turns that are spaced out and balanced (a single left or right turn then a long forward run), whereas the low‑reward ones feature repeated sharp turns in the same direction, leading to over‑steering and loss of alignment with the corridor. By preserving forward momentum and limiting unnecessary turning, the agent traverses more of the maze per time step and avoids collisions, directly boosting cumulative reward; the opposite pattern wastes steps on oscillations and frequent re‑orientations, which explains the large reward gap.
