The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, deliberate change of heading – the agent turns once (or twice) within the first few frames to line up with the corridor or corner, then proceeds with a short, smooth forward thrust before stopping or colliding only after a brief, bounded trajectory. By contrast, the low‑reward rollouts never execute such early turns; they persist with the same forward‑thrust action throughout the episode, producing long, repetitive oscillations that keep the agent wandering back and forth and repeatedly striking walls. This early alignment in the better rollouts limits exposure to the maze’s tight geometry, so the agent accrues fewer penalty steps before termination, whereas the constant‑action, oscillatory behaviour of the worse rollouts forces the agent to spend many steps in unstable, high‑penalty states. Consequently, the presence of an initial corrective turn and a brief, controlled motion segment explains why the high‑reward trajectories lose far less reward than the endlessly thrusting, uncorrected low‑reward ones.
