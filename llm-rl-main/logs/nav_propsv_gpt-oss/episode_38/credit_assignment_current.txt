High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave a short burst of forward thrust (action 0) that yields a +5.0 reward with an immediate corrective turn (action 1 or 2) within one or two frames, keeping the state confined to the narrow “sweet‑spot” band around 1–2; low‑reward rollouts either sustain forward thrust for many steps without a timely correction or postpone the turn until the state has already drifted far outside that band, leading to large negative rewards or collisions. The successful runs also exhibit a regular rhythm of positive‑reward spikes every few steps, whereas the failing runs show long stretches with no +5.0 reward and only occasional, poorly timed turns that cause abrupt drops in state value. By correcting almost as soon as the state begins to oscillate, the high‑reward policy prevents the accumulation of momentum that later results in overshoot, while the low‑reward policy lets momentum build unchecked until a catastrophic turn is forced. This disciplined timing of corrective actions therefore preserves stability and yields the higher episodic returns.
