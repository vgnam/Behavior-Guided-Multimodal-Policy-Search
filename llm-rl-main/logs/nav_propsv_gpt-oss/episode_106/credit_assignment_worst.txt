The higher‑reward rollouts all contain an early corrective action (typically a single “0” or turn command) after the first forward thrust, which redirects the agent away from the left wall before the drift becomes large, whereas the lower‑reward rollouts keep issuing the forward‑thrust action (2) for dozens or hundreds of steps without any timely correction, allowing the state to overshoot and collide. In the high‑reward cases the corrective turn occurs within the first 3–5 timesteps, curbing the exponential growth of position error; in the low‑reward cases the lack of such a turn lets the position spikes accumulate, producing chaotic oscillations that drive the cumulative penalty higher. The brief, decisive change in action also shortens the episode length, so the agent accrues fewer negative step‑wise rewards before termination, while the low‑reward trajectories persist far longer, compounding large penalties. Consequently, early, targeted corrections versus prolonged unchecked thrust explain the systematic reward gap between the two groups.
