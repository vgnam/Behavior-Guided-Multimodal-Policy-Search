High‑reward rollouts keep a steady, low‑magnitude action (often zero or a small corrective value) for long stretches, producing smooth, incremental state changes that let the agent accumulate positive rewards without triggering the large penalty associated with sudden jumps. In contrast, low‑reward episodes contain abrupt, high‑magnitude actions that instantly propel the state far from its current value, leading to rapid destabilisation and immediate failure penalties. The successful trajectories also delay any large corrective action until after a long period of stable motion, whereas the failing runs apply drastic corrections early or at the final step, causing a crash. Moreover, the high‑reward rolls exhibit a pattern of minor, periodic adjustments that keep the agent within a safe corridor, while the low‑reward ones lack these fine‑grained tweaks and instead rely on single, uncontrolled thrusts. This combination of prolonged smooth motion, late‑stage gentle corrections, and continual minor steering allows the agent to harvest reward, whereas abrupt, premature, or absent adjustments produce the negative outcomes observed.
