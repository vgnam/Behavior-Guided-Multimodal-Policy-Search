**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
A majority of the top‑performing rollouts (4 / 5) spend the first several time‑steps executing the “do‑nothing” action 0, letting the state drift slowly (e.g., 1.5 → 1.8 → 2.0) while still receiving a small per‑step reward. This prolonged low‑action phase builds up a sizable cumulative reward before any risky maneuver is attempted. When the agent does finally change direction it does so with a modest, incremental change in state (step‑wise growth of ≤ 2 units per step), which keeps the trajectory smooth and avoids an immediate large negative penalty.  

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
All low‑reward rollouts (5 / 5) begin with an immediate forward thrust (action 0) that pushes the agent straight into a wall within the first ten steps, producing a rapid, large‑magnitude state jump (> 4 units) and an early termination. A second recurring failure mode is a persistent drift along the left wall with no lateral correction, which also ends the episode quickly. These early, uncontrolled forward motions generate a steep negative reward spike and prevent any accumulation of positive reward.  

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS (ranked)**  
1️⃣ **Sustained early action‑0 phase (≥ 5 steps)** – appears in 4 / 5 high‑reward rollouts, none of the low‑reward ones; this is the strongest positive signal to reinforce.  
2️⃣ **Early wall‑adjacent drift without correction** – present in 5 / 5 low‑reward rollouts, absent from high‑reward; this is the clearest negative behavior to suppress.  
3️⃣ **Abrupt large state jump (> 4 units) within the first 10 steps** – occurs in 4 / 5 low‑reward rollouts, only 1 / 5 high‑reward; a secondary failure cue.  
4️⃣ **Early corrective turn (action 1) after 2–4 steps** – seen in 3 / 5 high‑reward rollouts, 0 / 5 low‑reward; a useful positive cue that can be weighted after the first pattern.  

**4. TIMING**  
Pattern 1 (sustained action 0) is an **early** phenomenon (steps 1‑8) and therefore likely causal for the higher cumulative reward. Pattern 2 (wall drift) also manifests **immediately** (steps 1‑4), making it a prime early failure indicator. Pattern 3 (large state jump) typically shows up **early** (within the first 10 steps) and signals a loss of control before the episode ends. Pattern 4 (early corrective turn) occurs **early‑mid** (steps 2‑5); its timely execution prevents collision and thus contributes causally to the higher scores.
