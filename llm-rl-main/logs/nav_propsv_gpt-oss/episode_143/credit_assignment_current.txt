High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave short bursts of turning actions (actions 1 or 2) with longer stretches of the forward action (action 0), using the turns only to fine‑tune alignment before resuming forward motion; low‑reward rollouts either persist with a single forward action for too many frames or execute an abrupt, prolonged turn that never settles back into forward progress, leading to collisions. In the successful episodes the agent delays large‑angle turns until after it has already moved a few steps forward, allowing it to gather information about the corridor before reorienting, whereas the poor episodes make a big turn immediately or accelerate into a turn without first establishing a stable forward trajectory. The timing of corrections is also distinct: high‑reward runs apply brief corrective turns every 2‑4 frames, keeping the trajectory roughly straight, while low‑reward runs either omit corrections entirely or apply them too late, resulting in runaway drift and eventual failure. This pattern of “forward‑most, corrective‑turn‑as‑needed” preserves momentum through the maze and prevents the agent from hitting walls, directly explaining the large reward gap.
