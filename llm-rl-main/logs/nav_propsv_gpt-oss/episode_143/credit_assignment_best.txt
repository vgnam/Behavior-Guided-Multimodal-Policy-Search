High‑reward rollouts consistently alternate short bursts of the forward action (0) with frequent corrective turns (1 or 2) every few frames, producing a steady “wiggle” that keeps the agent hugging walls without hitting them, whereas the low‑reward episodes contain a single, large forward move (often action 2) early on that produces a big state jump and a collision. The successful trajectories also exhibit early, modest adjustments that keep the agent centered in corridors, while the failures show abrupt, over‑steered turns or sudden large‑magnitude actions that send the agent into walls. In the best rollouts the corrective actions are spaced evenly (roughly every 2–4 steps), creating a smooth oscillatory path; the poor rollouts either omit these regular corrections or apply them too late, resulting in a loss of control. This pattern of continuous, low‑amplitude corrections prevents large‑scale errors and allows the agent to accumulate reward, whereas the sporadic, high‑amplitude moves in the low‑reward cases rapidly terminate the episode. Consequently, the presence of frequent, evenly timed turn actions and the avoidance of single, aggressive forward pushes are the key behavioral differences driving the reward gap.
