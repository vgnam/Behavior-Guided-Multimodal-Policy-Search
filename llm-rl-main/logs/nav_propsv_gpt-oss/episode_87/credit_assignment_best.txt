The high‑reward rollouts all keep a long uninterrupted stretch of the forward action (action 0) before issuing a turn, so the agent builds momentum along each straight segment and only switches to a turn (actions 1 or 2) after it has aligned with the upcoming bend; the low‑reward rollouts, by contrast, introduce a turn too early or execute a sharp turn abruptly, causing the vehicle to overshoot corners or collide with walls. In the successful runs the agent’s sensor orientation stays roughly parallel to the corridor walls, letting it sense the boundary and adjust smoothly, whereas the failures show the sensors quickly pointing toward a wall and the agent drifting sideways. Timing of corrective actions is also distinct: the good trajectories apply minor, gradual corrections after a few steps of deviation, while the bad trajectories make large, sudden reversals (including backward motion) that abort progress. These consistent differences—sustained forward motion, well‑timed gentle turns, and steady sensor alignment—prevent the agent from losing control and therefore generate much higher cumulative rewards.
