High‑reward rollouts consistently introduce a sharp right turn within the first few frames and then resume sustained forward motion, creating a rhythm of “forward → turn → forward” that keeps the agent centered in the corridor; low‑reward rollouts either never turn or delay the first turn until very late, running straight into a wall or boundary. The successful runs also display brief, high‑frequency corrective oscillations (alternating between slight left/right actions) immediately after each turn, which quickly dampen the deviation and stabilize the trajectory, whereas the poor runs show either no such micro‑adjustments or only a single large correction that overshoots. Timing is another key difference: in the high‑reward episodes the agent executes its second turn shortly after the first (within a few frames), maintaining momentum through a series of bends, while the low‑reward episodes either linger in prolonged straight segments or make a sudden, isolated turn that coincides with a large negative reward. Finally, the high‑reward behavior avoids long periods of unmodulated forward thrust that lead to early termination, instead interleaving turns and modest speed changes, whereas the low‑reward trajectories feature continuous unidirectional thrust until a catastrophic collision ends the episode. These patterns together keep the agent navigating the maze efficiently and prevent the fatal overshoots that dominate the low‑reward rollouts.
