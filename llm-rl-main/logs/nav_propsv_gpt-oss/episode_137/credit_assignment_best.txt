High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long, uninterrupted stretch of forward actions that builds a large positive reward before any turning is attempted, whereas the low‑reward runs either truncate this forward streak early or replace it with a sudden, large‑amplitude turn that immediately drives the state into a failure region. When a turn is needed, the successful episodes insert a single corrective action (left or right) and then promptly resume forward motion, keeping the magnitude of the turn small and the timing early enough to re‑stabilize the trajectory; the poor episodes either over‑rotate with multiple successive turn actions or delay the correction until the agent is already deviating sharply, producing oscillatory drift and eventual divergence. The high‑reward trajectories also maintain relatively smooth state values, avoiding prolonged back‑and‑forth oscillations, while the low‑reward paths spend a substantial portion of the episode cycling between opposite directions, which dissipates accumulated reward and raises the chance of a terminal crash. Finally, the best rollouts never execute a sharp, late‑stage turn that pushes the agent off the intended corridor, whereas the worst rollouts often finish with an abrupt turn that terminates the episode with a large negative penalty, directly accounting for the observed reward gap.
