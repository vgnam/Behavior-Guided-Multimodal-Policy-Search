The high‑reward rollouts all contain a brief, purposeful burst of forward thrust (action 2) followed almost immediately by a decisive change – either a turn (action 0/1) or a halt – which ends the episode after only a few timesteps, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep the same forward action for most of the trajectory and only terminate after a long, meandering sequence. In the better rollouts the state value changes smoothly and only once or twice, showing minimal oscillation, while the poorer rollouts exhibit repeated large swings and over‑corrections in the state trace. The high‑reward trajectories also show an early, single direction change that prevents the agent from lingering near walls or corridors; the low‑reward runs lack such timely turns and instead bounce repeatedly against obstacles. Because reward is accumulated negatively over time, the short, controlled episodes incur far fewer penalty steps than the prolonged, oscillatory ones, directly explaining the reward gap. This pattern of “quick decisive action → early termination” versus “persistent constant action → extended chaotic wandering” is the primary behavioral difference between the two groups.
