High‑reward rollouts repeatedly use action 0 (steady forward thrust) for long uninterrupted stretches, only inserting brief corrective actions 1 or 2 when the state begins to drift, whereas low‑reward episodes either terminate after a single forward step or intersperse forward moves with frequent, premature turns that quickly push the agent out of the corridor. In the successful runs the corrective turns occur after a measurable lag—typically after several seconds of forward motion—allowing the agent to build momentum before a small adjustment, while the failing runs apply turns almost immediately (or at the very end) resulting in abrupt state changes and collisions. The best rollouts also display a “smooth‑oscillation” pattern: a dominant forward phase followed by a single, modest turn, then a return to forward motion, which keeps the trajectory inside the navigable region; the low‑reward cases show erratic, back‑and‑forth oscillations with multiple successive turns or backward actions that destabilize the path. Finally, high‑reward episodes avoid any prolonged backward or sideways actions, whereas the poorest rollouts contain sudden backward‑type moves or large angular changes that correlate with large negative rewards, indicating loss of control.
