High‑reward rollouts consistently combine a dominant forward action with frequent, short corrective turns (left or right) that are applied immediately after a change in heading, keeping the agent centered on the corridor and preventing large deviations; low‑reward rollouts either persist with a single action for many steps or make only a single late correction, which lets the trajectory drift toward walls and results in premature failure. The successful rollouts also show a pattern of modest, steady speed—small incremental state increases—whereas the poor rollouts contain abrupt, large state jumps or aggressive thrusts that overshoot the safe region. Timing-wise, the high‑reward agents insert corrective actions within one or two steps of encountering a corner, while the low‑reward agents delay corrections until the agent has already collided or entered an unstable region. Because the environment penalizes collisions heavily, the early and regular micro‑adjustments in the high‑reward runs maintain safe navigation and accrue positive reward, whereas the delayed or absent adjustments in the low‑reward runs lead to loss of control and large negative penalties.
