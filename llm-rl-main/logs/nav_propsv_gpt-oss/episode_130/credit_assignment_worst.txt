The high‑reward rollouts all contain a brief, purposeful forward thrust followed quickly by a change of action (usually a stop or turn) that limits the length of the forward excursion, whereas the low‑reward rollouts keep the forward‑action (action 2) active for the entire episode and never switch to a braking or corrective command. This early action switch in the better rollouts prevents the state value from drifting far from the start and curtails the large oscillations that dominate the poorer runs. Because the high‑reward episodes terminate after a short, controlled movement, they avoid the cumulative penalty incurred by repeatedly overshooting and correcting, which is what drives the very negative scores in the low‑reward cases. Consequently, the presence of an early corrective action—and the corresponding reduction in sustained forward motion—appears to be the key behavioral difference that yields the modestly higher rewards.
