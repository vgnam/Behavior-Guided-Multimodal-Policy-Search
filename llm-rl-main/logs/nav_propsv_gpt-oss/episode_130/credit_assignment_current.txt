High‑reward rollouts consistently string together longer uninterrupted forward segments (action 0/1) before executing a decisive, single‑direction turn, whereas low‑reward runs interleave turns with forward moves every few steps, creating a rapid back‑and‑forth pattern. In the successful trajectories the agent often makes a brief corrective backward step right at the start, then commits to forward motion that carries it through a corner, while the poorer runs either skip this alignment or repeat backward adjustments throughout the episode. The high‑reward behavior shows a clear alternation of “forward → turn → forward” with each turn followed by several steps of forward progress, reducing wasted thrust and keeping the agent moving toward the goal; the low‑reward episodes display clusters of consecutive turns or immediate reversals that stall progress. Because sustained forward progress accrues larger per‑step rewards, the disciplined forward‑then‑turn rhythm of the high‑reward rollouts yields higher cumulative return, whereas the erratic, overly frequent turning of the low‑reward rollouts dissipates reward potential.
