High‑reward rollouts all begin with an early, small forward thrust (action 0) that yields an immediate positive reward and then quickly settle into a rapid alternation between forward moves and corrective turns (actions 0 and 2) that keep the agent oscillating around a safe corridor rather than committing to long straight motions. This alternating pattern appears consistently after the first few frames and provides frequent modest rewards while preventing the agent from hitting walls. In contrast, the low‑reward rollouts either issue a single, unchecked forward thrust (often action 2) that drives the agent straight into a boundary, or they fail to introduce the corrective turn cycle, leaving the trajectory monotonic and ending in a crash. The presence of early positive feedback combined with continuous corrective oscillations therefore sustains progress and avoids catastrophic collisions, explaining the large reward gap between the two groups.
