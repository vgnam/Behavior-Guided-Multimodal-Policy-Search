High‑reward rollouts all begin with an immediate burst of action 0 (forward thrust) and keep long uninterrupted stretches of this action, only inserting brief corrective actions 1 or 2 before quickly resuming forward motion; the low‑reward rollouts either delay their first forward thrust, insert several consecutive turns, or spend extended periods on actions 1/2 that yield negative rewards. In the successful runs the corrective turns are tightly timed—usually a single 1 or 2 immediately after a forward segment—so the agent restores alignment and returns to forward motion within a few steps, whereas the poor runs show clusters of back‑to‑back turns that create oscillations and prevent the agent from regaining momentum. Successful trajectories also avoid any late‑stage surge of rapid, erratic action changes that produce large negative rewards, while the worst rollout ends with a sudden series of sharp right turns and accelerations that culminate in a crash. Finally, the high‑reward episodes exhibit a pattern of “forward → brief turn → forward” repeated throughout, maintaining a net positive reward flow, whereas the low‑reward episodes contain “turn → turn → forward” or “forward → prolonged turn” patterns that interrupt the reward stream and lead to lower cumulative scores. These timing and sequencing differences explain why the policy earns substantially higher returns when it front‑loads and quickly re‑establishes forward motion and penalizes it when it lingers in corrective or destabilising actions.
