High‑reward rollouts consistently sustain long sequences of action 0 (forward) punctuated by a single, well‑timed left turn (action 2) at each corner, keeping the agent close to the wall and preserving a steady forward momentum; low‑reward rollouts break this pattern with frequent short bursts of forward motion interrupted by rapid alternating turns or occasional backward steps (action 1), producing erratic, over‑corrected trajectories. The high‑reward episodes also avoid abrupt accelerations, instead showing gradual speed changes and smooth curvature, whereas the low‑reward cases exhibit sharp accelerations followed by sudden decelerations that lead to loss of control. When a turn is needed, the successful rollouts execute it once and then resume forward motion, while the poor rollouts often oscillate between left and right turns within a few frames, creating instability. Maintaining a consistent distance from the walls—evident in the smooth wall‑following of the high‑reward paths—prevents collisions, whereas the low‑reward agents drift into narrow corridors and hit obstacles. These differences in action continuity, turn timing, and speed modulation directly translate into longer, collision‑free trajectories and therefore higher cumulative rewards.
