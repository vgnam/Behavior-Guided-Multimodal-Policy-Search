The high‑reward rollouts all contain a brief, modest forward thrust followed quickly by either a change of action (often to 0) or a small corrective turn, so the agent’s state only drifts a little before termination; in contrast, the low‑reward rollouts keep action 2 applied for many timesteps, producing long stretches of unchecked forward motion that generate large overshoots, oscillations and repeated over‑corrections. This early switch away from the constant thrust limits the cumulative distance travelled and therefore reduces the magnitude of the negative penalties that accrue in the stochastic environment. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes show occasional orientation adjustments (turns) that keep the agent from colliding with obstacles, whereas the low‑reward episodes show virtually no heading changes, causing the agent to repeatedly hit walls or undesirable regions. By terminating sooner and correcting its trajectory, the higher‑reward rollouts avoid the severe penalty spikes that dominate the low‑reward trajectories.
