High‑reward rollouts repeatedly interleave the forward‑propelling actions (0 / 1) with brief, low‑amplitude corrective moves rather than issuing a single, large turn; this pattern keeps the trajectory smooth and lets the agent recover from small deviations before they amplify. In contrast, low‑reward episodes contain a sudden, high‑magnitude turn (often a switch to action 0 or 2 after many forward steps) that abruptly changes heading and leads directly to a wall collision. The successful runs also spread their adjustments evenly throughout the episode, using small‑scale oscillations to stay centered, whereas the failures concentrate the decisive maneuver near the episode’s end, giving the agent insufficient time to correct. By maintaining continuous, modest corrections instead of a lone, abrupt change, the high‑reward policy avoids the destabilising spikes that terminate the low‑reward trajectories.
