High‑reward rollouts consistently string together brief forward actions (action 0) followed quickly by modest corrective turns (actions 1 or 2), keeping the agent’s state within a narrow corridor and preventing large‑scale drift. They also avoid long idle periods or extended backward moves, instead using short, frequent adjustments that correct orientation before error accumulates. In contrast, low‑reward rollouts feature either prolonged pauses or sudden, large jumps in state—often through excessive backward or aggressive turning actions—that push the agent toward walls or unstable regions. These abrupt or delayed corrections lead to over‑correction, loss of control, and eventual crashes, sharply reducing the cumulative reward. Consequently, the disciplined pattern of regular, small forward thrusts with timely, limited steering present in the successful runs appears absent or reversed in the failing episodes, directly accounting for the reward gap.
