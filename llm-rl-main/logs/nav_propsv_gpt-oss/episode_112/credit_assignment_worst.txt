The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one **non‑constant action** – usually a brief use of action 0 (a reverse or brake) or a turn that steers the agent away from an imminent wall – whereas the low‑reward episodes apply action 2 every step with no corrective maneuvers. This occasional reversal or turn appears early in the episode, creating a short “reactive” segment that pulls the agent back from a collision course, while the low‑reward runs never break the forward thrust and therefore accumulate larger penalty swings. In the higher‑reward traces the agent’s **timing of the corrective action** is clustered around the moment it would hit a boundary, giving it a chance to limit the severity of the failure; the worst rollouts lack any such timing cue and simply oscillate under an unchecked forward acceleration. Consequently, the presence and timely execution of a single opposing action reduces the magnitude of the terminal penalty, producing the modestly better rewards observed in the high‑reward rollouts.
