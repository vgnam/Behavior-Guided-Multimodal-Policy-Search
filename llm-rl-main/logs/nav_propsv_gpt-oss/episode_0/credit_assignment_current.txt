The high‑reward rollouts all include an early wall‑hugging phase followed almost immediately by a sharp, deliberate turn that redirects the agent away from the boundary and into open space, whereas the low‑reward rollouts either continue straight into a wall or drift slowly without any corrective turn. In the successful episodes the agent interleaves brief forward thrusts with short pauses or stabilising moments, giving it time to re‑orient before the next acceleration; the poor episodes maintain a single, sustained thrust that leaves no opportunity to correct a growing error. Moreover, the high‑reward trajectories make purposeful use of inner corners—first sliding along a wall to stay protected, then exploiting the corner to pivot sharply—while the low‑reward runs ignore these safe guides and wander off the corridor. These timing and directional adjustments keep the agent from colliding and allow it to approach the goal region, directly accounting for the higher cumulative rewards.
