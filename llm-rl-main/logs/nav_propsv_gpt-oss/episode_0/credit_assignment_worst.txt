The higher‑reward rollouts all contain moments where the agent switches away from the default “2” thrust and executes sharp left‑ or right‑turn actions that steer it toward a wall or corridor, followed by brief pauses or small corrective steps that try to hold its heading; these directional changes appear within the first few seconds and give the trajectory a purposeful, if imperfect, shape. In contrast, the worst rollouts never deviate from the single “2” action, producing a monotonous forward thrust that leaves the state oscillating back‑and‑forth without ever re‑orienting toward any structural feature. The brief stabilizing motions—small forward moves combined with a turn that reduces drift—present in the high‑reward cases are absent in the low‑reward episodes, where the agent’s state continually swings between high and low values. Because the occasional turn and pause prevent runaway drift and allow the agent to exploit nearby walls, they limit the magnitude of the negative reward, whereas the relentless, unvaried thrust in the low‑reward runs creates persistent oscillation and rapid penalty accumulation.
