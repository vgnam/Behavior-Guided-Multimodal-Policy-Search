High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with an extended stretch of forward actions (action 0) that builds momentum and keeps the state value rising before any turns are introduced, whereas low‑reward episodes often start with an immediate, isolated thrust or early turn that sends the agent toward a wall. In the successful runs the agent postpones lateral actions (1 or 2) until it has already established a stable trajectory, then applies a single corrective turn and quickly returns to forward motion, while the poor runs exhibit rapid, repeated oscillations between forward and turn actions, producing jerky back‑and‑forth movements. The best trajectories also show brief, well‑timed corrections—usually a single turn followed by two or three forward steps—so the agent stabilizes after an over‑correction instead of chaining multiple turns, whereas the low‑reward cases chain turns together or reverse direction repeatedly, leading to loss of control. Finally, high‑reward agents tend to maintain positive reward feedback by staying in regions where forward actions are reinforced, only deviating when a clear obstacle is detected; low‑reward agents ignore this signal, persisting with negative‑reward turns or backward motions that culminate in collisions or boundary exits.
