[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently intersperse the goal‑directed **action 0** (which yields the +5 reward) with corrective actions, creating short bursts of forward thrust followed by brief pauses that let the agent re‑orient before the next move; in the low‑reward episodes the agent either never uses action 0 or uses it only once, persisting with a single thrust action (usually 2) that drives it into drift or collision. The successful trajectories also show an early phase of rapid, exploratory adjustments that quickly give way to a sustained period of smooth forward motion, whereas the poor rollouts remain erratic for the whole episode, never settling into a stable forward segment. Timing‑wise, the high‑reward runs apply a corrective action exactly when a positive reward is received, using the feedback to lock onto the corridor, while the low‑reward runs ignore that signal and continue the same control input despite negative cues. This pattern of **alternating thrust‑pause‑turn** versus **monotonic thrust** explains why the former accumulates many +5 rewards and reaches the goal, whereas the latter accumulates penalties and drifts away.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts repeatedly exhibit long uninterrupted stretches of action 0 (forward) early in the episode, allowing the state value to rise steadily, whereas low‑reward rollouts either lack such forward bursts or replace them with immediate, isolated actions that cause abrupt drops. The successful trajectories also intersperse turns (actions 1 or 2) with short, deliberate micro‑adjustments rather than rapid, back‑to‑back switches, which keeps the agent’s orientation stable and prevents the jitter seen in failing runs. Timing-wise, the best rollouts delay large directional changes until after a clear forward momentum has been established, while the poor rollouts execute sharp turns or oscillations right after the start, leading to collisions or “crash” states. Moreover, high‑reward episodes maintain a smooth, monotonic increase in state value, reflecting continuous progress toward the goal, whereas low‑reward episodes show sudden spikes or drops in state value that correspond to uncontrolled thrusts or wall contacts. These consistent forward‑driven, smoothly‑modulated behaviors plausibly yield higher cumulative rewards by advancing the agent efficiently while avoiding the chaotic, destabilizing actions that terminate episodes early.

[WORST POLICY]
The higher‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early deviation from the default “2” thrust—typically a brief switch to action 0 or 1 within the first few dozen steps—that nudges the agent off a purely forward trajectory and prevents it from building up large state oscillations, whereas the lowest‑reward rollouts stay on action 2 almost continuously and only change actions, if at all, after many hundreds of steps. This early corrective move in the better runs produces modest, bounded fluctuations in the state value, while the long‑running monotonic thrust in the worse runs lets the state swing to extreme highs and lows before a catastrophic termination. Moreover, the higher‑reward episodes show a pattern of occasional, purposeful turns (a quick right turn followed by a short straight segment) that keep the agent hugging the outer wall, whereas the poorest rollouts exhibit no such wall‑following and instead drift aimlessly, resulting in larger, uncontrolled excursions. By breaking the relentless forward push and introducing timely directional adjustments, the better rollouts avoid the runaway oscillations that dominate the low‑reward trajectories and thus incur a smaller cumulative penalty.
