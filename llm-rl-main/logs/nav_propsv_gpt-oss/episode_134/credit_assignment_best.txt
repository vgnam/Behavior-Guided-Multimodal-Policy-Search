High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with short bursts of forward action (0) followed almost immediately by a corrective turn (1 or 2), keeping the agent close to walls and preventing it from drifting into open space; low‑reward runs either thrust forward for many steps without any early turn or keep a single forward action for too long before a sudden, large‑magnitude turn that ends the episode. The successful trajectories also alternate forward and turn actions at regular short intervals, producing a rhythmic “step‑turn‑step” pattern, whereas the poor trajectories show long monotonic forward stretches or a single late‑stage turn that over‑corrects and causes a crash. In the high‑reward cases the agent makes its first turn within the first few steps, using it to align with the corridor and then repeats modest adjustments, which continuously yields positive per‑step rewards; the low‑reward cases delay the first turn or skip it altogether, allowing the agent to accumulate negative positional rewards before a catastrophic failure. Finally, the high‑reward rollouts avoid any prolonged period of inaction or excessive acceleration, while the low‑reward rollouts exhibit either aggressive acceleration away from the start or a final abrupt acceleration that leads to loss of control, explaining the large reward gap.
