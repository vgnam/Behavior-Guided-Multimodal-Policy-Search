In the successful trajectories the agent keeps a forward‑biased action (typically 0 or 2) and, as soon as a negative reward is observed, immediately follows it with a corrective turn (action 1 or 2) within one or two steps, which prevents it from hitting a wall; the failing runs either ignore the negative signal and continue straight or abruptly switch to a stop action, leading to collisions. High‑reward rollouts also exhibit regular short‑duration oscillations—brief forward moves punctuated by quick opposite turns—that keep the agent centred in the corridor, whereas low‑reward episodes lack this micro‑adjustment and drift into obstacles. The timing of these corrections is early, occurring within the first few timesteps after a reward dip, so the agent can re‑align before the error compounds, while the poor rollouts delay or omit the turn until termination. Finally, successful episodes intersperse occasional brief backward or deceleration steps that act as safety buffers, a pattern missing from the low‑reward runs that maintain monotonic forward motion until impact.
