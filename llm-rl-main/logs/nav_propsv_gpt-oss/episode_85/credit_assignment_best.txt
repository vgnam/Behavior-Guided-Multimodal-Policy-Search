High‑reward rollouts keep long uninterrupted stretches of the forward‑motion action (typically 0 or 1) before any turn, building steady positive reward while the state stays near the centre of the maze; low‑reward rollouts intersperse turns much earlier, producing rapid oscillations that push the state toward the walls and trigger negative rewards. When corrective turns do occur in the successful runs they are spaced out and followed by a brief period of straight motion, allowing the agent to re‑align without overshooting, whereas the poor runs execute back‑to‑back turns or abrupt direction changes that immediately destabilise the trajectory. Successful episodes also delay any large‑amplitude state changes until the very end of the episode, using smooth, low‑frequency adjustments, while the failing episodes show sudden spikes in state value (large jumps) just before the crash, indicating loss of control. Finally, the high‑reward trajectories exhibit a pattern of “steady forward → minor correction → steady forward” that preserves momentum, whereas the low‑reward ones show “forward → sharp turn → immediate reversal,” a pattern that repeatedly erodes accumulated reward and leads to termination.
