The high‑reward rollouts all contain an early, deliberate change of action – after an initial forward thrust (action 2) the agent quickly issues a corrective turn (action 0 or 1) within the first few timesteps, which steers it away from the immediate wall and limits the duration of uncontrolled motion; by contrast, the low‑reward rollouts keep the same forward‑thrust action for the majority of the episode, producing long‑lasting oscillations and eventual collisions. In the better runs the corrective turn is followed by a short burst of straight, steady movement before any failure, whereas the worst runs show no such stabilising segment and instead drift continuously, causing larger cumulative penalties. The timing of the turn is also critical: high‑reward episodes execute the correction almost immediately (t ≈ 1–3), preventing the agent from building up large state errors, while low‑reward episodes delay or omit the turn entirely, allowing state values to explode and the trajectory to become chaotic. This early, purposeful reorientation reduces wall impacts and keeps the accumulated negative reward lower, explaining why those patterns consistently appear in the higher‑scoring rollouts and are missing from the lower‑scoring ones.
