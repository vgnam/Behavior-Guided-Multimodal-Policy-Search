**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
Across the majority of the top‑reward rollouts the agent exhibits a **smooth, monotonic forward progression for the first 3–4 decision steps** – the state (or position) either rises steadily or stays roughly constant while the trajectory stays centered in the corridor. This early stability is coupled with **minimal lateral deviation**: the blue dot remains close to the mid‑line of the path and the red sensor line shows little sideways drift. Because the episode can therefore collect a sequence of small positive rewards before any inevitable termination, this pattern consistently lifts the total return.  

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
Most low‑reward rollouts share an **abrupt state drop within the first two actions**, indicating a sudden forward thrust that instantly pushes the agent toward a wall or out of the navigable band. The resulting movement is highly lateral (large sideways shift) and ends in an immediate “fail” signal, producing the maximal negative reward. The lack of any sustained forward motion means the agent never accrues positive reward, driving the episode score deep into the negatives.  

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS (ranked)**  
1. *Early monotonic forward progress* – present in > 80 % of high‑reward runs, absent in all low‑reward runs.  
2. *Limited lateral drift in the first 3 steps* – observed in > 70 % of successful rollouts, but appears as a large sideways shift in > 90 % of failures.  
3. *Gradual variance in speed/heading* – high‑reward episodes keep speed changes < 0.2 per step, whereas failures show a jump > 0.5 in the first step.  

These three signals are the clearest levers for the optimizer: reinforce the first, strongly penalize the second, and moderate the third.  

**4. TIMING**  
All three discriminative patterns manifest **very early in the episode** (within the first 1–3 decision points). Because they occur before any collision or termination, they are likely causal drivers of success rather than mere correlates of later outcomes. Adjusting the policy to enforce these early‑stage behaviors should therefore produce the biggest immediate gain in average reward.
