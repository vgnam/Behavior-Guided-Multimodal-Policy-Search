[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts all feature long uninterrupted forward thrusts (the same forward‑type action repeated for dozens or even hundreds of steps) followed by only brief, single‑step corrective turns, whereas low‑reward episodes turn almost every few steps and never sustain a forward run. The successful trajectories also keep the corrective turn to a single direction (typically a right‑hand adjustment) and execute it after a predictable interval, while the failing runs show alternating or random left/right turns that quickly push the agent toward walls. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s state value stays relatively stable during the forward phases, indicating it stays centered in the corridor, but in the low‑reward cases the state value drifts steadily toward an extreme, revealing a lateral drift that culminates in a collision. Finally, the best rollouts delay any sharp turn until after the agent has cleared the initial cluttered region, whereas the worst rollouts make an early, abrupt turn that traps them in tight corners; this timing difference lets the high‑reward policy accumulate positive reward before risking a penalty.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently interleave the forward thrust action 0 with timely corrective turns (actions 1 and 2), producing a “push‑forward‑then‑adjust” rhythm that keeps the agent hugging walls and avoids collisions; in contrast, low‑reward rollouts either omit action 0 entirely or use a single aggressive forward action (often action 2) without subsequent turning, driving straight into obstacles. The successful episodes also begin with short exploratory moves before committing to longer bursts of action 0, giving the agent time to sense nearby geometry, whereas the failing runs start with an immediate large thrust that leaves no room for correction. Throughout the high‑reward trajectories the agent’s turn actions occur precisely at corners or junctions, creating smooth cornering, while the low‑reward cases show either no turn at all or turns that are mistimed, resulting in abrupt wall contacts. This pattern of balanced forward progress punctuated by well‑timed corrective actions plausibly yields higher cumulative reward by maintaining forward momentum without incurring collision penalties.

[WORST POLICY]
The higher‑reward rollouts all introduce a non‑forward action (a turn or brake) within the first few timesteps, creating an early directional change that steers the agent away from the corridor walls, whereas the low‑reward rollouts persist with action 2 (forward thrust) for the entire episode and only switch to a different action at the very end when a collision occurs. This early maneuver in the high‑reward cases limits the amplitude of the agent’s position oscillations, while the low‑reward trajectories display large, unchecked swings in the state value because they never interrupt the forward motion. Moreover, the high‑reward episodes tend to combine a brief burst of acceleration with a quick deceleration or turn, producing a compact, purposeful trajectory, whereas the low‑reward episodes exhibit continuous, jerky forward thrusts with no deliberate slowing, leading to prolonged exposure to obstacles. By breaking momentum early, the high‑reward behavior reduces the chance of a catastrophic penalty, which explains why those rollouts achieve substantially better (less negative) returns.
