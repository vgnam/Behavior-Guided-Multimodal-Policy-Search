High‑reward rollouts all settle into a long, uninterrupted stretch of forward‑motion actions (mostly action 0, sometimes action 2) within the first few timesteps, using only brief, gentle turns to correct the heading, whereas low‑reward rollouts either never establish such a forward streak or break it almost immediately with repeated turns (actions 1 or 2). The successful episodes keep the state trajectory smooth and monotonic, avoiding the large, sudden jumps in state that appear right before termination in the poor episodes. When a correction is needed, the high‑reward trajectories make a single, modest turn and then resume forward progress, while the low‑reward ones over‑correct, oscillate between left and right turns, and often reverse direction shortly after. This disciplined timing—early commitment to forward thrust followed by minimal, well‑spaced adjustments—prevents collisions and keeps the agent in rewarding regions, whereas the erratic, turn‑heavy timing in the low‑reward rollouts drives the agent into walls or out‑of‑bounds, producing the stark reward gap.
