**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS**  
The rollouts that rank highest all contain an **early, strong forward thrust** (a large positive control input in the first 1‑2 timesteps) that pushes the agent several state‑units forward before any corrective action is taken. This thrust is **single‑directional and sustained** – the policy keeps issuing the same “forward” action for several steps rather than oscillating or switching directions. The effect is a rapid reduction of the state value (i.e., moving quickly toward the goal corridor) that yields a comparatively higher reward even though the episode later terminates. Because the agent covers distance early, the cumulative penalty per timestep is smaller, making the overall return less negative.

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS**  
The low‑reward rollouts share two detrimental habits. First, many episodes exhibit **abrupt, large‑angle turns** within the first two steps (often a rightward rotation that over‑corrects), which instantly sacrifices forward progress and places the agent against a wall. Second, a subset of failures shows **near‑zero movement** – the policy repeatedly outputs an action that produces almost no change in state, leaving the agent stalled for most of the episode. Both behaviours waste timesteps and increase the per‑step penalty, driving the total reward further down.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** (ranked by how uniquely they separate the two groups)  

1. **Early aggressive forward thrust without immediate over‑steer** – present in 4/5 high‑reward rollouts, absent in all low‑reward ones.  
2. **Absence of large early turns** – high‑reward episodes never make a >30° orientation change in the first two steps, whereas 2/5 low‑reward rollouts do.  
3. **Sustained forward action vs. stagnation** – high‑reward runs maintain a non‑zero forward command for ≥3 consecutive steps; low‑reward runs contain ≥2 episodes where the action magnitude is essentially zero for the majority of the horizon.

**4. TIMING**  
All three discriminative behaviours manifest **within the first two timesteps** of an episode. The early thrust and the lack of a sharp turn are causal levers: they set the trajectory before the agent encounters walls, while the sustained forward command simply ensures the agent does not fall into the “stalled” regime that dominates the low‑reward set. Consequently, the optimizer should bias the policy toward producing a confident forward push right at the start and penalize any immediate large rotational output or prolonged zero‑action periods.
