[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently alternate short bursts of forward thrust (action 0) with immediate corrective turns or slight braking (actions 1 or 2) within one or two timesteps, keeping the agent centered on a productive path; low‑reward episodes either sustain forward motion for many consecutive steps or delay the corrective turn until after the vehicle has drifted far enough to collide or lose control. The timely interleaving of all three actions creates a rhythmic “oscillatory control” that continuously re‑aligns the heading, whereas the failing runs show a dominance of a single action (often prolonged acceleration) and a late, abrupt reversal that overshoots the safe corridor. In the high‑reward cases the agent probes its surroundings early—evidenced by brief exploratory thrusts followed by rapid adjustments—so it can exploit open space before committing to longer moves, while the low‑reward trajectories lack this early probing and instead commit to a direction without verification. This proactive, balanced action sequencing prevents the agent from veering into obstacles or dead‑ends, directly translating into higher cumulative rewards.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long uninterrupted stretch of action 0 that pushes the state value upward before any turn is taken, whereas the low‑reward episodes insert actions 1 or 2 almost immediately, causing early drops in the state and a negative reward spike. The successful trajectories reserve corrective actions (1 or 2) for moments when the forward momentum has already built up, using them sparingly and only after several consecutive forward steps, while the failing runs alternate rapidly between forward and turning actions, producing a jittery, oscillatory pattern. In the high‑reward cases the agent’s state value rises monotonically for most of the episode, indicating steady progress toward the goal, whereas the low‑reward cases show frequent reversals and a final surge that overshoots the target, reflecting loss of control. This disciplined timing—maximising forward moves first and limiting early turns—keeps the agent on a smooth path and prevents the destabilising oscillations that repeatedly truncate the low‑reward episodes.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all begin by quickly aligning the agent with a nearby wall and then make a purposeful turn at the first corner, whereas the low‑reward episodes keep the same forward‑thrust action throughout and never execute a deliberate reorientation. In the better runs the agent’s speed and heading are adjusted within the first few frames, producing a smoother, wall‑following trajectory that stays inside the corridor, while the poorer runs show large, unchecked oscillations in the state value because no corrective actions are taken. Those early corrective turns prevent the agent from drifting into open space and colliding with obstacles, so the episode lasts longer and accumulates a higher (less negative) reward. Conversely, the constant‑action, oscillatory behavior of the low‑reward rollouts leads to repeated over‑corrections and eventual crashes, which explains the much larger penalty.
