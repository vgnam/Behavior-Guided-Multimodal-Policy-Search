High‑reward rollouts keep long stretches of the forward action (0) before inserting any turn, and when a correction is needed they apply a single, brief action 1 or 2 immediately after a small deviation is observed, preserving forward momentum. In contrast, low‑reward episodes either rush into a turn without first stabilising forward motion or accelerate aggressively, producing a rapid succession of large‑amplitude actions that drive the agent into walls or off‑track. The successful runs also exhibit a pattern of early, modest adjustments—tiny turns right after the first few frames—whereas the poor runs delay correction until the error is large, resulting in a sudden, high‑penalty collision. By limiting the duration of corrective bursts and spacing them predictably, the high‑reward behavior maintains a steady accumulation of positive reward, while the erratic timing and magnitude of turns in the low‑reward cases wipe out that gain.
