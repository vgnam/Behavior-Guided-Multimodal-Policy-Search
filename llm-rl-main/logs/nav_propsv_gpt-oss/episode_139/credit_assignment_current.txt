High‑reward rollouts contain long uninterrupted stretches of the forward action (action 0) followed by a single, well‑timed sharp turn (usually action 2) at the maze junction, whereas low‑reward rollouts break the forward motion with many premature or back‑to‑back turns. In the high‑reward cases the decisive turn is delayed until after roughly 10–15 steps, aligning the agent with the inner corridor, while the low‑reward runs turn within the first few steps and waste forward progress. When a turn does occur in the high‑reward trajectories the agent immediately resumes action 0, limiting the number of consecutive turn actions that generate negative –0.5 rewards; the low‑reward trajectories often string together multiple turn actions, prolonging periods of penalty. High‑reward rollouts also include occasional larger corrective turns that re‑center the agent on the optimal path, whereas the low‑reward rollouts rely on many small, hesitant adjustments that keep the agent drifting off‑center. These timing and action‑selection differences yield more frequent +5 reward events and smoother advancement through the maze, producing the observed reward gap.
