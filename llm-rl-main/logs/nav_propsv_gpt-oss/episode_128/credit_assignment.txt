[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long stretch of consecutive forward actions (action 0) that carry the agent several dozen steps before any turn is taken, whereas low‑reward runs insert sharp left/right actions (1 or 2) almost immediately after the first forward move. When corrective turns do appear in the successful episodes they are brief—typically a single left or right step followed quickly by another forward step—while the poor rollouts exhibit clusters of back‑to‑back turns or repeated forward–backward oscillations that keep the agent trapped in a small region. The timing of adjustments in the high‑reward trajectories is spaced out, allowing the agent to build momentum and make steady progress toward the goal; the low‑reward trajectories react too frequently, over‑correcting and often colliding with walls. This smoother, forward‑biased pattern reduces wasted motion and prevents the large negative rewards associated with abrupt direction changes, which explains the substantial reward gap.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a sustained forward thrust that lasts several timesteps before any turn is made, allowing the agent to gain distance while staying near the maze centre; low‑reward rollouts either turn too early or drift toward walls, producing short forward segments and early boundary contact. When a turn is required, the successful runs execute a sharp, decisive rotation and immediately re‑establish forward motion, whereas the poor runs make abrupt turns that lead into cul‑de‑sacs or cause the agent to linger without regaining forward velocity. The top rollouts display regular, modest‑amplitude oscillatory corrections that keep the trajectory aligned with the intended path, while the failing trajectories show larger, unchecked drifts toward the outer limits and lack these stabilising nudges. Finally, the high‑reward episodes avoid premature termination by maintaining a clear margin from obstacles, whereas the low‑reward episodes terminate early because their over‑corrections or boundary drifts result in collisions or loss of control. These timing and control differences explain why the former accumulate large positive rewards and the latter accrue negative or modest scores.

[WORST POLICY]
The higher‑reward rollouts all contain an initial phase of modest, forward‑thrust action that is followed by a single, deliberate turn (action 0 or 1) when the agent reaches a corner, after which the episode terminates; in contrast, the low‑reward rollouts never execute a turn and remain on the forward‑thrust action (action 2) for the entire episode, producing large, repeated oscillations in the state value. This brief, purposeful turning step in the better runs keeps the agent from overshooting the maze geometry, limiting the distance travelled and preventing the cascade of corrective jumps seen in the poorer runs. The better rollouts also display a smoother, more monotonic trajectory before the turn, whereas the worse rollouts exhibit jerky, back‑and‑forth motions that repeatedly destabilize the agent. By curbing the duration of uncontrolled forward motion and inserting an early corrective turn, the high‑reward trajectories avoid the cumulative errors that drive the very negative rewards observed in the low‑reward episodes.
