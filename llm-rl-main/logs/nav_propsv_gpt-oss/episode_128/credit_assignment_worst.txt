The higher‑reward rollouts all contain an initial phase of modest, forward‑thrust action that is followed by a single, deliberate turn (action 0 or 1) when the agent reaches a corner, after which the episode terminates; in contrast, the low‑reward rollouts never execute a turn and remain on the forward‑thrust action (action 2) for the entire episode, producing large, repeated oscillations in the state value. This brief, purposeful turning step in the better runs keeps the agent from overshooting the maze geometry, limiting the distance travelled and preventing the cascade of corrective jumps seen in the poorer runs. The better rollouts also display a smoother, more monotonic trajectory before the turn, whereas the worse rollouts exhibit jerky, back‑and‑forth motions that repeatedly destabilize the agent. By curbing the duration of uncontrolled forward motion and inserting an early corrective turn, the high‑reward trajectories avoid the cumulative errors that drive the very negative rewards observed in the low‑reward episodes.
