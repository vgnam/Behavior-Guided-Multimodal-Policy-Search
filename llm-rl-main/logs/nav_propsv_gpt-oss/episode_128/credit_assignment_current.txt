High‑reward rollouts all begin with a long stretch of consecutive forward actions (action 0) that carry the agent several dozen steps before any turn is taken, whereas low‑reward runs insert sharp left/right actions (1 or 2) almost immediately after the first forward move. When corrective turns do appear in the successful episodes they are brief—typically a single left or right step followed quickly by another forward step—while the poor rollouts exhibit clusters of back‑to‑back turns or repeated forward–backward oscillations that keep the agent trapped in a small region. The timing of adjustments in the high‑reward trajectories is spaced out, allowing the agent to build momentum and make steady progress toward the goal; the low‑reward trajectories react too frequently, over‑correcting and often colliding with walls. This smoother, forward‑biased pattern reduces wasted motion and prevents the large negative rewards associated with abrupt direction changes, which explains the substantial reward gap.
