High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a sustained forward thrust that lasts several timesteps before any turn is made, allowing the agent to gain distance while staying near the maze centre; low‑reward rollouts either turn too early or drift toward walls, producing short forward segments and early boundary contact. When a turn is required, the successful runs execute a sharp, decisive rotation and immediately re‑establish forward motion, whereas the poor runs make abrupt turns that lead into cul‑de‑sacs or cause the agent to linger without regaining forward velocity. The top rollouts display regular, modest‑amplitude oscillatory corrections that keep the trajectory aligned with the intended path, while the failing trajectories show larger, unchecked drifts toward the outer limits and lack these stabilising nudges. Finally, the high‑reward episodes avoid premature termination by maintaining a clear margin from obstacles, whereas the low‑reward episodes terminate early because their over‑corrections or boundary drifts result in collisions or loss of control. These timing and control differences explain why the former accumulate large positive rewards and the latter accrue negative or modest scores.
