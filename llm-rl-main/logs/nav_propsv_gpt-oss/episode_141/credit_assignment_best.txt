High‑reward rollouts consistently sprinkle short bursts of **action 0** (the forward thrust that yields the +5.0 reward) among a few corrective turns, so the agent gains frequent positive increments while never lingering on a single steering command for more than a couple of steps; low‑reward runs either omit these forward bursts or let a single action (usually 1 or 2) run unchecked until a catastrophic collision occurs. In the successful episodes the agent’s **timing pattern** is a rapid “forward‑then‑tiny‑adjust‑forward” rhythm that keeps it hugging walls or corridors, whereas the poor episodes exhibit long uninterrupted straight runs followed by a sudden large turn or overshoot that throws it off the safe path. The high‑reward behavior also shows **small‑amplitude oscillations** (minor left/right tweaks) immediately after each positive reward, which act as micro‑stabilizers and prevent drift, while the low‑reward trajectories lack these stabilizing tweaks and instead display either no correction or overly aggressive re‑orientation. Because the former strategy continually harvests the high‑value forward reward and corrects drift before it accumulates error, it produces much larger total returns than the latter, which sacrifices early gains and ends in costly crashes.
