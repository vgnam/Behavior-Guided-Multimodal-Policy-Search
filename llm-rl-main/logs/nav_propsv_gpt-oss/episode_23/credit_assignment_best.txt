High‑reward rollouts keep a long uninterrupted stretch of forward actions (action 0) before any corrective turn, whereas low‑reward rollouts intersperse turns (actions 1 or 2) almost every step, breaking momentum. When a turn is needed, the successful runs execute a single, well‑timed sharp turn (action 2) after several forward steps, positioning the agent near the outer bounds of the arena and then resume forward motion; the poor runs either turn too early, turn repeatedly, or use backward moves (action 1) that send them back toward the centre. The timing of corrections in the high‑reward trajectories is sparse and deliberate, producing smooth state trajectories with only modest fluctuations, while the low‑reward trajectories exhibit rapid, jittery oscillations that cause the agent to linger in unsafe zones. Because the reward function heavily favors forward progress and penalizes collisions or stagnation, maintaining forward thrust for as many steps as possible and limiting corrective actions to essential, well‑spaced turns directly yields higher cumulative reward. Consequently, the presence of sustained forward sequences and minimally timed sharp turns distinguishes the high‑reward behavior from the erratic, over‑correcting patterns of the low‑reward rollouts.
