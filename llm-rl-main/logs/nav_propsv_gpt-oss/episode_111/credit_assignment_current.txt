High‑reward rollouts all begin with a sustained forward thrust (the dominant early‑stage action) followed by timely, short corrective turns that keep the agent near the target corridor, whereas the low‑reward runs either linger on a single direction for many steps or end with an abrupt, uncontrolled forward action that crashes the episode. In the successful episodes the agent interleaves brief bursts of forward motion with immediate opposite‑direction adjustments, creating a rhythmic “push‑and‑pull” pattern that prevents drift; the failing episodes lack this rhythm, showing either monotonic motion or a sudden reversal only at the very end. The high‑reward trajectories also execute sharp, purposeful turns exactly when approaching a wall or boundary, effectively re‑orienting before collision, while the low‑reward trajectories either turn too late, turn erratically, or never turn at all. This combination of early forward momentum, regular corrective oscillations, and well‑timed boundary‑avoidance turns maintains stable progress and yields higher cumulative reward, whereas the absence or mistiming of those components leads to loss of control and lower scores.
