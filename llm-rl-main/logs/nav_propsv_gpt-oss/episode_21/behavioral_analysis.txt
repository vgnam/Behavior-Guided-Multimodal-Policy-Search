These micro‑adjustments keep the trajectory centred in the corridor and prevent early collisions, which directly translates into higher cumulative reward.  
- **Early “wall‑hugging” positioning** – The agent typically moves along the inner wall right after the first few steps, using the wall as a reference. By anchoring to the wall it reduces lateral drift and gains extra positive reward for staying on the safe side of the lane.

- **Aggressive early acceleration** – In ≥ 4/5 low‑reward rollouts the first 5–10 steps contain a maximal‑thrust forward action that spikes the state value, sending the agent straight into a wall. The resulting immediate large negative reward dominates the episode.  
- **Absence of corrective turns** – The failing trajectories never issue a turn action after the initial thrust; they continue a straight line until collision. Without any heading correction the agent cannot avoid the boundary, leading to the consistent low reward.  
- **Immediate wall contact** – All low‑reward episodes terminate within the first 20 steps, indicating that the policy is not learning to stay away from the outer perimeter long enough to accrue positive per‑step reward.

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS (ranked)**  
1. **Micro‑turn corrections every ~10–20 steps** – Present in 4/5 high‑reward runs, absent in all low‑reward runs. Reinforce a bias toward occasional turn actions rather than continuous forward thrust.  
2. **Moderate‑speed forward thrust (no large acceleration spikes)** – High‑reward episodes keep the forward command at a constant, modest magnitude for > 30 steps; low‑reward episodes exhibit a spike in the first ≤ 5 steps. Suppress large‑magnitude forward actions early in the episode.  
3. **Early wall‑hugging (initial positioning adjacent to inner wall)** – Observed in most high‑reward rollouts, never mentioned in low‑reward ones. Encourage the policy to bias the initial heading toward the inner corridor edge.

**4. TIMING**  
- **Micro‑turn corrections** appear throughout the episode but the first corrective turn is typically seen within the first 15–20 steps, making it an early causal factor for success.  
- **Moderate‑speed forward thrust** is established immediately (first 5 steps) and maintained; the absence of an early acceleration spike is a decisive early signal.  
- **Wall‑hugging positioning** is set in the very first few steps (≤ 5) and persists, so it is an early‑episode driver of high reward.  

*Actionable takeaway:* bias the policy toward low‑magnitude forward commands from the start, insert a scheduled small‑turn action roughly every 12 steps, and initialise the heading so the agent slides along the inner wall. Simultaneously penalise large thrust values in the first 10 steps and remove any turn‑action suppression that prevents these micro‑adjustments. This combination targets the most discriminative, early‑occurring behaviors that separate high‑ from low‑reward outcomes.
