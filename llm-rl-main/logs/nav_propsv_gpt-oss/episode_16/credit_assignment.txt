[CURRENT POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts consistently begin with a decisive forward step followed almost immediately by a right‑turn (action 0) that yields an immediate positive reward, and they repeat this forward‑then‑right pattern at regular short intervals; low‑reward rollouts either delay the first turn or replace it with a left‑turn or a neutral action, causing the agent to spend more time drifting without gaining reward. In the successful runs the agent’s corrective actions occur within one or two timesteps after an overshoot, so the oscillations are brief and quickly bring the state back into a high‑reward region, whereas the poorer runs exhibit prolonged back‑and‑forth sequences lasting several steps before correcting, which dilutes the accumulated reward. The high‑reward trajectories also contain noticeably fewer occurrences of the negative‑reward action (action 2); when it does appear, it is swiftly countered by a subsequent action 0, while the low‑reward episodes allow action 2 to persist longer, dragging the state value down. Finally, the timing of turns in the best rollouts aligns with clear waypoints in the map (sharp corners), producing smooth, goal‑directed navigation, whereas the low‑reward episodes turn erratically or miss those waypoints, leading to unnecessary wandering and lower total reward.

[BEST POLICY]
High‑reward rollouts keep a steady forward thrust for most of the episode and only intervene with short, frequent corrective turns – the agent repeatedly “wiggles” right or left while still moving forward, which lets it stay near the maze walls without getting stuck. By contrast, the low‑reward rollouts contain a single large, abrupt turn (often early in the episode) that sends the agent into a dead‑end or wall, terminating the run. The successful trajectories also exhibit a pattern of continuous motion (no long pauses or backward steps), whereas the poor trajectories include backward or stationary steps and a pronounced loss of forward momentum before the failure. Finally, the high‑reward behavior tends to over‑correct in small increments, smoothing out errors, while the low‑reward behavior either under‑corrects (letting the error grow) or over‑corrects with a single violent maneuver that ends the episode.

[WORST POLICY]
The high‑reward rollouts all contain at least one early corrective maneuver—either a brief turn (action 1) or a brake/stop (action 0)—that tempers the initial forward thrust, whereas the low‑reward episodes keep the forward‑only action 2 for the entire run and never intervene to curb drift. In the better rollouts the agent’s speed spikes are short and followed quickly by a stabilising action, producing modest state‑value changes; the worst rollouts show long, uninterrupted bursts of action 2 that generate huge jumps in the state and repeated overshoot‑undershoot cycles. Moreover, the higher‑reward trajectories exhibit a brief window of smooth, roughly linear motion before any oscillation begins, while the low‑reward ones are oscillatory from the first frame, never achieving a steady segment. These early corrections and limited‑duration thrusts keep the agent nearer to the intended path and prevent catastrophic wall collisions, which explains why they earn substantially higher (less negative) rewards than the always‑forward, wildly oscillating behavior of the low‑reward rollouts.
