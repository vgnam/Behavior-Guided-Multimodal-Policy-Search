**1. SUCCESS PATTERNS** – The rollouts that earned the highest scores all spent a long initial segment (≈200‑300 steps) applying a near‑zero or very low‑magnitude action, which kept the internal “state” value in a modest band (≈0.8‑6.5).  During that same window they performed small, smooth turning adjustments rather than a straight‑line drift, allowing the agent to weave around obstacles while preserving forward progress.  Because the agent never lets the state explode past ≈8, it avoids the catastrophic collisions that terminate episodes, so the combination of steady low thrust plus gentle curvature yields the bulk of the reward.  

**2. FAILURE PATTERNS** – The low‑reward rollouts share two dominant flaws: (a) a persistent, uncorrected drift in a single direction with essentially no turning or corrective action, and (b) a sudden large jump in the state value (often > 8) that immediately precedes a negative reward.  The drift means the agent reaches a wall or boundary without ever attempting to steer away, while the abrupt state spike signals an uncontrolled acceleration that ends in a crash, both of which truncate the episode and keep reward low.  

**3. DISCRIMINATIVE PATTERNS** –  
1️⃣ **Sustained low‑magnitude action (≈0) for the first 200‑300 steps** – present in 4/5 high‑reward rollouts, absent in any low‑reward rollout.  
2️⃣ **Smooth, incremental turning while moving forward** – appears in 3/5 high‑reward rollouts and never in the low‑reward set.  
3️⃣ **Avoidance of large state spikes (> 8) especially after step ≈250** – high‑reward rollouts keep the state ≤ 6.5; low‑reward rollouts always exhibit a spike that triggers failure.  
4️⃣ **Active corrective thrust when the state approaches the upper safe bound** – observed implicitly in the high‑reward trajectories (state nudges back down) and missing from the low‑reward ones.  

**4. TIMING** – The most discriminative behaviours (1 & 2) occur early in the episode, establishing a safe trajectory before any risk of collision arises; thus they are likely causal drivers of success.  Pattern 3 (spike avoidance) tends to manifest later (after the 250‑step mark) and serves as a strong indicator that the episode will fail if it appears, while pattern 4 (corrective thrust) shows up in the middle phase when the agent first nears the safe‑state ceiling.  Reinforcing the early low‑thrust, smooth‑turn regime and training the policy to intervene before a late‑stage spike should therefore raise average reward.
